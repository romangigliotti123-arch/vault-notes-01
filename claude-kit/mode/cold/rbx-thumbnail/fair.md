# Fair promise vs manipulative click-bait

The test: could a first-time player recognise this image in the first 60 seconds of play? Yes = a strong promise. No = bait. Roblox measures qualified play-through, so bait that earns clicks and fast bounces lowers the thumbnail's own score, and misleading metadata can cost Home and Charts exposure; near-copies of another experience's thumbnail are deprioritised too.

| Fair (allowed, keeps players) | Manipulative (rule risk and bounce risk) |
|---|---|
| Exaggerate the scale or drama of something that exists (a boss, a rare pet, a tall tower) | Show a mechanic, UI, reward or theme the game lacks |
| Heighten colour and lighting in the style the game really has | Graphics enhanced beyond what players see; mockups of assets that do not exist |
| Curiosity about a real, unresolved element the first minute answers | Implied free Robux or money, or a reward that is not given |
| Mild post-processing, a logo, in-game UI text | Fake counters, ratings, "official" labels or event badges |
| Genre-legible cues plus one twist of your own | Copying another game's key art, title look or composition |
| A teaser of a real update shown in game | Scary imagery aimed at young players in an all-ages context; gore |

## How the vault enforces it
- Rubric gates G1 (promise is real), G2 (rule-safe) and G3 (original vs competitors) fail the candidate whatever its total ([cold:rbx-thumbnail/rubric.md]). A gate is decided by looking, with the first-minute screenshot beside the image.
- Curiosity is scored only when the game answers it (check 7): a gap with no payoff is bait.
- The ledger compares qPTR AND average playtime per qualified play: a variant that wins clicks but loses playtime is not called a winner (protocol.md).
- Social proof is the platform's job: Roblox overlays player counts and likes on the tile, so a fake crowd or counter in the image is both useless and risky.
