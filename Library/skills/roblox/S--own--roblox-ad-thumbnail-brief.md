# Roblox icon, thumbnail and ad brief

Output: a brief a designer or image tool can execute, plus a test plan. Patterns, not copies: never reuse another game's art, characters or name.

## Inputs to collect
Genre and core hook in one sentence, target age band, the single most exciting moment in the first minute, art style used in-game, current click-through and retention if known.

## Brief
1. Concept options: 3 distinct ideas, each a one-line scene (action moment, reward moment, social moment). Pick the clearest at thumbnail size on a phone.
2. Composition: one focal subject, high contrast against the background, readable at small size, character expression visible, no clutter. Keep key content inside the centre so crops do not cut it.
3. Text: none or at most 2-3 words large and legible; do not promise what the game lacks ("free Robux", fake counters, fake UI buttons, copied logos break rules and trust).
4. Specs to verify against current Roblox docs before delivery: experience icon is square (commonly 512x512), thumbnails are 16:9 (commonly 1920x1080); ad formats and sizes differ by placement in Ads Manager. Check the live requirements, do not trust memory.
5. Safety: all ages, no real-world weapons gore, no impersonation, no misleading claims, all assets owned or original.

## Test plan
- Change one variable at a time (icon first, it drives most impressions; then thumbnail order).
- Run each variant for a fixed period or impressions count (decide in advance); compare click-through and D1 retention of the visitors it brought, not clicks alone.
- Keep the winner, log the result with date in the project notes, then test the next idea.
- For paid ads set a small daily budget cap approved by Roman (spending money needs his approval), pause variants with the worst cost per retained visit.

Deliverable: brief text, prompt/spec sheet per variant, test table (variant | dates | impressions | clicks | CTR | D1).
