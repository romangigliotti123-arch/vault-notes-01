# Docs: production / game-design / content-updates

[Open](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/content-updates.md) | Roblox Creator Docs | 0 views | 2026-10-28

## Summary
Roblox official docs page on content cadence within LiveOps: rapid, small content releases (art-based variants, themed drops, limited-time events, season passes) kept sustainable under a fixed schedule, with scope capped at under three weeks of effort.

## Tips
- [page](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/content-updates) **[[updates-live-ops]]** Build content cadence from existing gameplay systems; avoid adding new systems, since that turns cadence content into an expansion and is unsustainable.
- [page](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/content-updates) **[[updates-live-ops]]** Favour simple art-based variants (e.g. color swaps of existing assets) that few team members can produce quickly on a recurring schedule.
- [page](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/content-updates) **[[analytics-metrics]]** Use player behaviour analytics and feedback to pick which content types players regularly want most.
- [page](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/content-updates) **[[updates-live-ops]]** Keep each content cadence update to under three weeks of effort to maintain a rapid schedule and leave room for other LiveOps work.
- [page](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/content-updates) **[[updates-live-ops]]** Release on a fixed routine; many games ship cadence updates every two weeks to one month to sustain engagement between expansions.
- [page](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/content-updates) **[[updates-live-ops]]** Make cadence content earned over time (progression, limited-time events, season passes) rather than instantly consumed, so each release lasts longer.
- [page](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/content-updates) **[[core-loops-and-game-design]]** Start content planning from the core loop to identify which systems to support with releases, and offer varied content types to avoid player boredom.
- [page](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/content-updates) **[[updates-live-ops]]** Add permanent content at the end of progression gates so veteran players get fresh content immediately while newer players have goals to reach.
- [page](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/content-updates) **[[updates-live-ops]]** Use themed releases (seasons, holidays) to generate assets that fit together, but check that the chosen theme appeals to your audience.
- [page](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/content-updates) **[[updates-live-ops]]** Balance limited-time event objectives so most players need weeks to exhaust the content; the time limit itself drives engagement.

## Numbers
- [page](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/content-updates) Content cadence effort per update: **under 3 weeks** (Recommended maximum effort per cadence update to keep schedule rapid)
- [page](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/content-updates) Release frequency: **every 2 weeks to 1 month** (Typical cadence update interval to maintain engagement between expansions)

## Games
- **Adopt Me!**: Pet variants (spotted, colored) and themed updates are good examples of cheap, recurring cadence content.
- **Robloxian High School**: Seasonal content works as a limited-time cadence source, if the season suits the audience.
- **Jailbreak**: Season pass shows a straightforward progression framework adaptable to most games.
