# MustWatch - How to make successful Roblox games

MustWatch is a channel aggregating Roblox-official and RDC talks plus dev interviews. Core message: pick an easy-to-explain idea with mass appeal, build a satisfying, replayable core loop and layer progression goals (short/mid/long) on top, teach players in the first session, then win retention first (D1/D7/D30), because Roblox discovery rewards long-term retention plus monetisation. Ship small MVPs fast, playtest, seed with modest ads or creators, then run a steady live-ops cadence (biweekly content, major expansions every 4-6 months, hyped events), measuring everything with analytics and A/B experiments, and monetise fairly after the fun is proven.

**Style:** A curated library of official Roblox DevRel/RDC sessions and top-studio panels (Jailbreak, Adopt Me, Red Manta, TDS) mixed with solo-dev devlogs. Practical, metrics-driven and platform-aware; suits developers wanting studio-grade process, live-ops, analytics and monetisation guidance rather than a single personal formula.

## Playbook
1. **Choose a simple, mass-appeal idea and validate the market** [[game-ideas-and-genres]]: Pick ideas that are easy to explain, naturally fun and have built-in retention (FOMO). Study the market and player psychology; fuse proven concepts with trends (Steal a Brainrot); choose a Bartle primary and secondary audience; build only what you'd play yourself.
2. **Design a satisfying core loop and tie systems to it** [[core-loops-and-game-design]]: Core loop is the most-repeated minute-to-minute action; every system should feed it. Make games replayable, not quickly completed. Major updates should change the loop; fix unfun features within a week.
3. **Layer goals and progression** [[retention-d1-d7]]: Use short (session), mid (days to a week) and long (weeks to months) goals feeding each other. Show progress, make rewards meaningful, pace content against average earnings, and don't lock the initial fun.
4. **Scope small, build an MVP fast and playtest** [[development-process-and-speed]]: Cut everything non-essential from the loop, ship a minimal version, run alpha/beta with community testers on a mirror server, fix bugs first. Push the date ~1 extra week if testers say not ready. Small-scope games can be built in about a month.
5. **Nail the first session** [[first-session-onboarding]]: Put key features like the shop on screen, teach core mechanics explicitly, gate advanced features (trading, merging) behind progression, and pace unlocks to avoid bombarding new players.
6. **Set up analytics before launch** [[analytics-metrics]]: Track DAU/MAU, session length, D1/D7/D30 retention, ARPDAU, conversion and ARPPU; instrument features, funnels and economy events (AnalyticsService). Prioritise engagement, then monetisation, then acquisition.
7. **Make thumbnails/icons that reflect gameplay and test them** [[thumbnails-icons]]: Use Thumbnail Personalization with 2-5 thumbnails judged on qualified playthrough rate; avoid clickbait, keep a consistent theme, test icon and thumbnail separately, and refresh with updates.
8. **Seed launch with hype, creators and modest ads** [[ads-and-sponsored-experiences]]: Tease for weeks, show a playable build to creators (~3 months before for big launches), run tournaments, and use Ads Manager with daily budgets for 7-10+ days after a small test. Ads cannot fix a weak game.
9. **Design for the discovery algorithm** [[discovery-algorithm]]: Recommended-for-You uses retrieval then ranking on play-through rate, playtime, retention, spend and co-play, with 28-day windows. Long-term retention plus non-spammy monetisation wins; no gaming of impressions.
10. **Monetise after the fun is proven** [[monetisation-devproducts]]: Add monetisation where it fits; offer tiered prices, starter packs after the tutorial, event currencies, infrequent sales, and price by time-to-earn (bundles skipping about 3-6 hours). Avoid pay-to-win and paid access unless unique.
11. **Run a live-ops cadence** [[updates-live-ops]]: Content drop every 1-2 weeks, major expansion every 4-6 months, seasonal events and season passes, Friday releases, teasers and countdowns, and a retrospective after each. Fix purchase and data-loss bugs first.
12. **Add social hooks and community** [[social-features-and-virality]]: Friends drive joins and retention: parties, voice, leaderboards, trading, titles. Use Discord/Twitter for announcements, feedback channels and community testers.
13. **Experiment and iterate with configs and A/B tests** [[analytics-metrics]]: Use Configs and Experiments to test shops, offers and thumbnails; avoid peeking early, segment results, and prefer tuning from real data.
14. **Protect team, scope and business** [[team-and-budget]]: Keep a small stable core team plus contractors, avoid crunch, don't pull the team off a healthy live game, hire small first, use written agreements, and register your IP.

## Key numbers
- World Zero launched with 70,000 users and zero ad spend (alpha/beta hype)
- Hyped countdown updates doubled or tripled player counts (Red Manta)
- Major update cadence: 4-6 weeks (Red Manta), quarterly (Bad Business), 4-6 months (Dan's expansions)
- Biweekly content drops; Friday releases; cutoff Wednesday night/Thursday midday
- 1 extra week (~20%) of polish beats rushing
- Jailbreak Oceans event: 550,000+ CCU; live events gave over 500% CCU increase
- Goals: short = session, mid = days to a week, long = weeks to months
- Season pass example: 10 tiers, 30 days, ~300 Robux premium, free 2/premium 3 missions/day, 75% activity
- Price dev-product bundles at about 3-6 hours of average earnings
- Sales at 25-30% off gave 2-4x revenue spikes; run only a few times per year
- Average dollar: 29% to developer payouts, 25% experience costs, 22% third-party platforms, 24% Roblox ops
- DevEx rate up 8.5%; 42% DevEx rate for verified 18+ US players
- Thumbnail Personalization: 2-5 thumbnails, hourly updates, judged on qualified playthrough rate
- Discovery uses 28-day windows; ads: daily budgets, 7-10+ days, ~$25 test ad, ~24h learning period
- Fish went from ~10K to 1.3M+ CCU via social design; 99 Nights built in 3 months

## Common mistakes
- Letting players finish all content on day one (no per-day pacing of missions)
- Hiding key features like the shop deep in menus; assuming players know how to play
- Trying to appeal to every player type
- Shipping rushed updates instead of delaying a week
- Relying on ads to fix a weak game; ads that stall once spend stops
- Frequent sales that train players to wait; poor-value starter packs reducing spend
- Pay-to-win, or paid access without unique value (Red Manta's earned nothing and kept creators away)
- Removing earned cosmetics after events, or hiding the reasons behind unpopular changes
- Pulling the team off a healthy live game for a new project; crunch and weekend fixes
- Pitching creators an unfinished idea, or using clickbait thumbnails

## Launch checklist
- [ ] Idea explainable in one sentence with mass appeal
- [ ] Core loop is fun in minutes and replayable
- [ ] Short, mid and long-term goals with visible progress
- [ ] Shop and key features visible; core mechanics taught in first session
- [ ] Mobile and low-end Android tested
- [ ] Alpha/beta playtest with community done; bugs fixed first
- [ ] Analytics, funnels and economy events instrumented
- [ ] Retention (D1/D7/D30) and monetisation benchmarks defined
- [ ] Thumbnails (2-5) and icon reflect real gameplay and are tested
- [ ] Title/description with keywords, no bloat
- [ ] Fair monetisation with a free path and starter pack after tutorial
- [ ] Server-side validation and exploit checks in place
- [ ] Discord/Twitter set up and teasers scheduled
- [ ] Creators contacted with a playable build
- [ ] Small ad test then daily-budget campaign planned
- [ ] Friday release timing with team rest planned
- [ ] Content backlog and 2-week update roadmap ready
- [ ] Social hooks (friends invites, leaderboards, trading) enabled

## How the advice changed over time
- 2021-2022 advice centred on big expansions, hype countdowns, Twitter/YouTube teasers and paid early access; by 2023 sources recommend avoiding paid access and favour a steady biweekly cadence plus 4-6 month expansions.
- Discovery advice moved from CCU/engagement-based ranking (2021-2023) to multi-signal, long-term retention plus monetisation with 28-day windows (2025-2026); QPTR was replaced by play-through rate and first-play bounce rate (2026, newest).
- Ads moved from Sponsored Experiences (lifetime budgets, 2024) to Ads Manager with daily budgets and 7-10+ day runs (2025-2026); newer advice supersedes the older lifetime-budget tip.
- Analytics evolved from basic KPI reading to AnalyticsService funnels/economy events and built-in Configs/Experiments A/B testing (2024-2025).
- 2025 trend advice emphasises strategy-first simple trend games and small fast builds over passion projects, while 2025-2026 studio talks stress pacing content and small core teams to avoid burnout.
