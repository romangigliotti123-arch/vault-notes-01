# The Retention Playbook: Turning Players into Fans | RDC 2025

[Watch](https://youtu.be/YgyfcMfxQcI) | MustWatch | 7,444 views | 2025-09-26

## Summary
RDC 2025 panel with developers of Jailbreak, Evade, Beast Warm Simulator, and Gunfight Arena on retention and live-service management. Covers following the fun early, pacing content releases instead of chasing trends, keeping small core teams, leveraging community testers, and balancing new versus veteran players.

## Tips
- [5:17](https://youtu.be/YgyfcMfxQcI?t=317) **[[core-loops-and-game-design]]** Make the core loop fun first; doubling retention of an unfun game still yields zero. Test the core loop with friends early.
- [7:18](https://youtu.be/YgyfcMfxQcI?t=438) **[[core-loops-and-game-design]]** Leave room for player feedback in the roadmap; plan a predicted roadmap but be ready to pivot when players ask for something else.
- [10:12](https://youtu.be/YgyfcMfxQcI?t=612) **[[development-process-and-speed]]** After a viral launch, list problems to fix immediately, list player-requested directions, and pick updates at the intersection with what you want to build.
- [12:54](https://youtu.be/YgyfcMfxQcI?t=774) **[[updates-live-ops]]** Save some content for later updates and align releases with holidays; real-world events give a ready-made reason for live-ops content when ideas run dry.
- [14:21](https://youtu.be/YgyfcMfxQcI?t=861) **[[updates-live-ops]]** Pace content releases: spreading 10 guns over 10 weeks keeps community interest better than one large update, even when the team can build it all at once.
- [15:43](https://youtu.be/YgyfcMfxQcI?t=943) **[[monetisation-gamepasses]]** Season pass systems let you ship a lot of content while players progress toward unlocks, avoiding a flood of items in the shop at once.
- [17:45](https://youtu.be/YgyfcMfxQcI?t=1065) **[[common-mistakes]]** Avoid chasing short-term trends; games that copy trend-of-the-week updates become hollow. Build trust by releasing what you think players will want long-term.
- [19:06](https://youtu.be/YgyfcMfxQcI?t=1146) **[[team-and-budget]]** Keep the original creative vision with a small team; a solo or small passionate core preserves the game's identity.
- [20:27](https://youtu.be/YgyfcMfxQcI?t=1227) **[[updates-live-ops]]** Watch closely for the first 48 hours after an update to catch players misusing new content, such as unfair competitive advantages from emotes.
- [24:24](https://youtu.be/YgyfcMfxQcI?t=1464) **[[community-and-discord]]** Treat engaged community members (testers, creators, bug reporters, Discord mods) as an extension of the team; let them self-organize.
- [25:46](https://youtu.be/YgyfcMfxQcI?t=1546) **[[team-and-budget]]** Avoid corporate-style contracts with collaborators when possible; friendship-based teams stay accountable longer than paid arrangements.
- [26:26](https://youtu.be/YgyfcMfxQcI?t=1586) **[[community-and-discord]]** Show up in-game as the developer (NPCs, spawned events, live messages) to build a sense of community and connection with players.
- [28:16](https://youtu.be/YgyfcMfxQcI?t=1696) **[[team-and-budget]]** Start with a passionate day-one team and add contractors for specific jobs like QA for events; avoid scaling the team for its own sake.
- [29:37](https://youtu.be/YgyfcMfxQcI?t=1777) **[[analytics-metrics]]** Watch analytics, YouTube videos and new-player sessions to see the first-time experience; designers lose fresh perspective over time.
- [30:43](https://youtu.be/YgyfcMfxQcI?t=1843) **[[playtesting-and-feedback]]** Recruit fresh testers outside your loyal testing team; long-time testers become set in their ways and miss new-player friction.
- [31:24](https://youtu.be/YgyfcMfxQcI?t=1884) **[[updates-live-ops]]** Keep the first-time user experience stable; for new features, make the first items fun and balanced like early items so both new and veteran players engage.
- [32:45](https://youtu.be/YgyfcMfxQcI?t=1965) **[[first-session-onboarding]]** Hide complexity behind gates and progression so the start looks as simple as day one; reveal deeper layers gradually instead of bloating early game.
- [34:07](https://youtu.be/YgyfcMfxQcI?t=2047) **[[common-mistakes]]** Avoid bloat: when most of the game is irrelevant to new players, they get rushed to late content and get overwhelmed and leave.
- [34:48](https://youtu.be/YgyfcMfxQcI?t=2088) **[[development-process-and-speed]]** Schedule sustainable hours and plan breaks to prevent burnout; constrained daily work sessions can keep the project fun.
- [36:10](https://youtu.be/YgyfcMfxQcI?t=2170) **[[playtesting-and-feedback]]** Don't listen only to the loudest voices; watch what players do in-game and on YouTube, since most players never speak up.

## Games
- **Jailbreak** [5:17](https://youtu.be/YgyfcMfxQcI?t=317): Launched with a tiny team (two devs); community testers and creators filled gaps. Season passes pace many new items.
- **Beast Warm Simulator** [3:14](https://youtu.be/YgyfcMfxQcI?t=194): Layered hooks with gated mechanics; slower cadence and resisting trends gave longevity; hidden depth keeps start simple.
- **Gunfight Arena** [6:37](https://youtu.be/YgyfcMfxQcI?t=397): Launched unfinished, saving content for updates; holiday-timed live ops filled gaps; paced gun releases.
- **Evade** [8:01](https://youtu.be/YgyfcMfxQcI?t=481): Started inspired by Nextbots, added cosmetics, emotes and voice chat for expression and social play.
