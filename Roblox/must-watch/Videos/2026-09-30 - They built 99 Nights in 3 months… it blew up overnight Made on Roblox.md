# They built 99 Nights in 3 months… it blew up overnight  | Made on Roblox #1

[Watch](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c) | MustWatch | 24,252 views | 2026-09-30

## Summary
Grandma's Favourite Games (NZ) discuss their Roblox history, from War Clans and lobby building to Parkour Tower, Mall Tycoon, Break In and 99 Nights in the Forest. Covers the rapid 2025 CCU surge, the burnout from weekly updates, their playtesting method (invisible ghost observer, solo vs group testing), and their advice to scope small and make games fast.

## Tips
- [0:00](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c?t=0) **[[first-session-onboarding]]** Keep lobbies small and streamlined; clutter makes players drop off before reaching the game. Their lobby averaged about 1.5 minutes.
- [7:00](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c?t=420) **[[trend-research-and-validation]]** Pick a category the leader dominates with no real competition, as Parkour Tower did against Tower of Hell, but expect rivals to copy quickly.
- [7:40](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c?t=460) **[[common-mistakes]]** Don't change default Roblox mechanics heavily; Parkour Tower's altered mechanics (portals, see-through viewports) confused players and underperformed.
- [9:20](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c?t=560) **[[game-ideas-and-genres]]** Ride a trend but add a differentiator; Mall Tycoon added tycoon customization and peaked at 65k players, enough for top 10 in 2021.
- [9:20](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c?t=560) **[[game-ideas-and-genres]]** Borrow a proven genre's familiar systems so players instantly understand the game; 99 Nights reused Dead Rails' sack mechanic and genre.
- [20:06](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c?t=1206) **[[first-session-onboarding]]** Make key interactables obvious; moving a quest board closer and adding an exclamation mark fixed players ignoring it.
- [20:06](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c?t=1206) **[[first-session-onboarding]]** Avoid spelling out every hint; leave some discovery, but verify players can actually find the critical next step.
- [25:41](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c?t=1541) **[[playtesting-and-feedback]]** Test solo and with friends separately; their game felt weak solo but fun in groups, so design for the intended play mode.
- [27:05](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c?t=1625) **[[playtesting-and-feedback]]** Discount feedback by source: fans may flatter or over-criticise, and developers are biased. Weigh feedback by who gave it.
- [27:46](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c?t=1666) **[[playtesting-and-feedback]]** Watch unaware players via an invisible ghost observer hidden from the leaderboard or alt accounts; you see what they really do.
- [28:28](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c?t=1708) **[[playtesting-and-feedback]]** Wait for repeated feedback before acting on it; one-off complaints can be ignored, but scrap a mechanic if many players reject it.
- [28:28](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c?t=1708) **[[core-loops-and-game-design]]** Try unconventional ideas, but kill mechanics that players don't click with; their random crafting-table roguelike idea failed after about 50 players.
- [31:54](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c?t=1914) **[[development-process-and-speed]]** Soft-release quietly with small ad spend and let players trickle in before any hype; this let them observe growth before scaling.
- [34:00](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c?t=2040) **[[updates-live-ops]]** Expect that a successful launch needs fast, regular updates early; their weekly drops built a content base, later moved to biweekly.
- [34:41](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c?t=2081) **[[common-mistakes]]** Don't ship updates untested and don't wipe player data; they admitted rushed weekly pushes and testing gaps caused data loss.
- [34:41](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c?t=2081) **[[common-mistakes]]** Schedule updates for your players' peak hours; their NZ-based release at 3–5am caused poor timing for the US audience.
- [36:01](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c?t=2161) **[[updates-live-ops]]** A strong core loop can sustain a game; a six-week hiatus let 99 Nights trend up, so content isn't the only retention driver.
- [36:43](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c?t=2203) **[[mindset-and-strategy]]** Take breaks before burnout; dread about working on a game hurt quality, and the rested team returned more productive.
- [41:24](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c?t=2484) **[[updates-live-ops]]** Plan updates early in the week with a strict schedule; late planning left too little build and test time.
- [43:28](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c?t=2608) **[[updates-live-ops]]** Step back between updates to tie loose ends and remove bloat; they found disconnected features only after a break.
- [47:01](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c?t=2821) **[[mindset-and-strategy]]** Expect many failures; learn from each and keep making new games rather than treating one flop as a verdict on yourself.
- [48:25](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c?t=2905) **[[development-process-and-speed]]** Scope small: find the core fun fast in a small project, then expand; they built some hits in about two weeks and typically spend 2–3 months.
- [48:25](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c?t=2905) **[[development-process-and-speed]]** Avoid multi-year scope when learning; Roblox trends move fast, so a long project can miss its moment.
- [38:03](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c?t=2283) **[[building-art-and-assets]]** Programmer art can stay if it is clear and charming; it became part of 99 Nights' identity.

## Numbers
- [0:00](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c?t=0) Peak concurrent users (CCU): **~1,000,000** (99 Nights in the Forest, reached within weeks of soft release (2025))
- [6:50](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c?t=410) Average lobby session time: **~1.5 minutes** (Lobby before game in 99 Nights in the Forest)
- [9:20](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c?t=560) Peak concurrent players: **65,000** (Mall Tycoon, 2021; enough for top 10 on Roblox then)
- [12:15](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c?t=735) Break In peak rank: **#2 on platform** (Story game released Feb 2020, held top 10 for a while)
- [12:56](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c?t=776) Break In 2 peak rank: **#4 on platform** (Released around 2023; about 1 billion players reported)
- [49:07](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c?t=2947) Typical project dev time: **2–3 months** (Their shortest game; Parkour Tower 3–4 months; Multi-Cube ~5 months)
- [35:24](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c?t=2124) Update cadence: **Weekly, later biweekly** (Weekly launches after 99 Nights boom; moved to biweekly later)

## Games
- **Tower of Hell** [7:00](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c?t=420): Dominant parkour game in an uncontested category; Parkour Tower tried to challenge it and failed.
- **Parkour Tower** [7:00](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c?t=420): Altered default parkour mechanics and confused players; underperformed. Keep core mechanics familiar.
- **Mall Tycoon** [9:20](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c?t=560): Added customisation to a tycoon trend and reached 65k peak, top 10 in 2021.
- **Wacky Wizards** [11:20](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c?t=680): Solo hit from Matt, a success that paused Mall Tycoon updates.
- **Break In / Break In 2** [12:15](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c?t=735): Story games; Break In peaked #2 after a February 2020 release, Break In 2 peaked #4.
- **99 Nights in the Forest** [15:00](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c?t=900): Survival forest game; soft-released mid-2025, reached ~1M CCU; reused proven mechanics from Dead Rails.
- **Dead Rails** [29:11](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c?t=1751): Major inspiration; familiar collaborative survival loop and sack mechanic let players understand the game instantly.
- **Survival Week in the Woods** [29:11](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c?t=1751): Second inspiration for the collaborative survival design.
- **DeadRouse** [22:10](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c?t=1330): First-person-only survival game that inspired the systems; 99 Nights switched to mostly third person.
- **Illegal Soccer** [14:19](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c?t=859): Recently played; the team cited its ranked updates as a model.
- **Adopt Me! / Blox Fruits / Murder Mystery 2** [50:42](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c?t=3042): Older front-page games still actively maintained for years, showing long-term live operation.
