# Cracky4 Reveals The Secrets Of Being A Top Roblox Developer | Interview

[Watch](https://youtu.be/JCMIb008Y6E) | MustWatch | 23,942 views | 2025-12-21

## Summary
Interview with Cracky4, creator of 99 Nights in the Forest, Break In, MTycoon, Icebreaker and Defenders of Roblox. Covers his start (2014 thumbnails, learning to code from wiki/YouTube), team process (3-person core plus contractors, weekly updates, content built back-to-front), optional difficulty for broad all-age appeal, minimal monetisation (revive only), simple art style for performance and clarity, and handling success.

## Tips
- [1:08](https://youtu.be/JCMIb008Y6E?t=68) **[[mindset-and-strategy]]** Learn programming yourself, even if not brilliant; he says top games are all made by people who can code, and it is essential early on.
- [4:38](https://youtu.be/JCMIb008Y6E?t=278) **[[development-process-and-speed]]** Learn from free resources: Roblox Developer Hub, YouTube tutorials, and reading well-made open-source games; escalate to a friend or AI when stuck.
- [19:45](https://youtu.be/JCMIb008Y6E?t=1185) **[[playtesting-and-feedback]]** Test with groups, not solo. Solo friends got bored and quit; groups of three played for hours and got addicted, revealing the real hook.
- [25:54](https://youtu.be/JCMIb008Y6E?t=1554) **[[mindset-and-strategy]]** Make games you yourself want to play; his ambition is a game that families and friends of all ages can enjoy together.
- [30:23](https://youtu.be/JCMIb008Y6E?t=1823) **[[core-loops-and-game-design]]** Keep visuals simple so gameplay is clear; flashy visual noise distracts players and reduces attention for good game design.
- [33:52](https://youtu.be/JCMIb008Y6E?t=2032) **[[building-art-and-assets]]** Simple part-based art runs on all devices and reaches a wider audience; performance matters more than graphical polish for reach.
- [33:52](https://youtu.be/JCMIb008Y6E?t=2032) **[[core-loops-and-game-design]]** Use optional difficulty: safe zones for kids, optional side bosses and crafted gear for experienced players, so one game serves ages 4 to adults.
- [44:05](https://youtu.be/JCMIb008Y6E?t=2645) **[[development-process-and-speed]]** Plan only the first ~20 minutes of gameplay in detail and leave the rest open-ended, then fill mid and late game from player data after launch.
- [44:47](https://youtu.be/JCMIb008Y6E?t=2687) **[[updates-live-ops]]** Build content from early to late game: most players never reach day 100 (about 1% earn the badge), so prioritise early and mid content first.
- [41:20](https://youtu.be/JCMIb008Y6E?t=2480) **[[updates-live-ops]]** Run a weekly update cadence and plan a rough roadmap spreadsheet, picking next week's work each week, prioritised roughly.
- [51:39](https://youtu.be/JCMIb008Y6E?t=3099) **[[monetisation-gamepasses]]** Keep monetisation sensible: in 99 Nights only revive is purchasable; he says removing it would get the game crucified.
- [26:35](https://youtu.be/JCMIb008Y6E?t=1595) **[[core-loops-and-game-design]]** Make the game engaging rather than idle; 99 Nights runs take one to two hours, showing active play can reach number one.
- [54:22](https://youtu.be/JCMIb008Y6E?t=3262) **[[core-loops-and-game-design]]** Saving progress (base saves) is requested often but is the hardest technical feature; weigh the cost before promising it.
- [63:00](https://youtu.be/JCMIb008Y6E?t=3780) **[[common-mistakes]]** Be careful changing a successful game; he is scared to alter things like music because the game may be succeeding despite or because of its current state.
- [57:42](https://youtu.be/JCMIb008Y6E?t=3462) **[[team-and-budget]]** Scale the team with popular success: hire specialists (animator, character designer, VFX, artist) to support the core team once weekly updates become stressful.
- [1:03:52](https://youtu.be/JCMIb008Y6E?t=3832) **[[updates-live-ops]]** Keep updating while you enjoy it; he updates because the game is fun to update, with RNG and many possible additions.
- [1:07:59](https://youtu.be/JCMIb008Y6E?t=4079) **[[core-loops-and-game-design]]** Present information clearly and explain thoroughly, making it understandable to a broad audience, including non-players.

## Games
- **99 Nights in the Forest** [0:00](https://youtu.be/JCMIb008Y6E?t=0): Simple parts-based art, optional difficulty, and revive-only monetisation; built from a 20-minute planned start, then expanded weekly.
- **Break In** [19:05](https://youtu.be/JCMIb008Y6E?t=1145): Early test stayed sound-free and icon-free, yet grew to thousands; iterative testing with returning players revealed the hook.
- **Survivor Week in the Woods** [31:47](https://youtu.be/JCMIb008Y6E?t=1907): Forest survival precedent by Unequip; peaked around 4,000 players, showing niche precedent was limited.
- **Dead Rails** [31:47](https://youtu.be/JCMIb008Y6E?t=1907): Source of the sack mechanic in 99 Nights; influenced core design and art style.
- **Doors** [29:01](https://youtu.be/JCMIb008Y6E?t=1741): Teaches mechanics through minimal cues (press C to crouch, minecart interaction); clear design without tutorials.
- **Work at a Pizza Place** [26:35](https://youtu.be/JCMIb008Y6E?t=1595): Praised as embodying Roblox with 17-year longevity and around 50,000 current players.
- **Blox Fruits, Brookhaven** [34:33](https://youtu.be/JCMIb008Y6E?t=2073): Top games with simple art styles; shows simplicity does not block success.
- **BlockTales** [25:54](https://youtu.be/JCMIb008Y6E?t=1554): Artistically interesting and tribute-driven; he worked on its music.
- **M Tycoon** [27:40](https://youtu.be/JCMIb008Y6E?t=1660): Reached top 10 with a few hundred million plays in 2021.
