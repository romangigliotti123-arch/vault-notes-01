# Finding Your Art Direction | RDC 2024

[Watch](https://youtu.be/vWx8UTxsi0A) | RobloxOfficial | 11,765 views | 2024-09-23

## Summary
Roblox RDC 2024 talk by Gino 'Heart_Hertz' (Sorse Interactive art director) on art direction: concept art, graybox-to-final world building from player POV, cinematic lighting (storytelling with light, global illumination, bounce lights, light count), and color balance and color for gameplay signals.

## Tips
- [3:53](https://youtu.be/vWx8UTxsi0A?t=233) **[[building-art-and-assets]]** Start with concept art and sketches before building; sketches let you lock in details and get approvals faster than redoing built geometry.
- [8:00](https://youtu.be/vWx8UTxsi0A?t=480) **[[building-art-and-assets]]** Draw your own reference images instead of relying on online references; it reduces revision cycles caused by inaccurate or off-style references.
- [10:08](https://youtu.be/vWx8UTxsi0A?t=608) **[[building-art-and-assets]]** Follow four stages: concept art, graybox, art build, finished map. Graybox early to test playability before art work.
- [11:32](https://youtu.be/vWx8UTxsi0A?t=692) **[[development-process-and-speed]]** Graybox your world first and playtest it so you can move landmarks and props before investing in final art.
- [12:13](https://youtu.be/vWx8UTxsi0A?t=733) **[[core-loops-and-game-design]]** Identify main gameplay areas (landmarks) and check travel time between them so players don't lose interest or get lost.
- [12:53](https://youtu.be/vWx8UTxsi0A?t=773) **[[thumbnails-icons]]** Compose scenes from the player spawn POV; reuse those camera positions to capture thumbnails for free.
- [14:14](https://youtu.be/vWx8UTxsi0A?t=854) **[[building-art-and-assets]]** Add your own style (personal interests, e.g. industrial architecture, concrete, organic shapes) to make the work stand out.
- [15:58](https://youtu.be/vWx8UTxsi0A?t=958) **[[core-loops-and-game-design]]** Treat each location as a movie scene with its own lighting mood so the player's journey progresses from beginning to end.
- [18:43](https://youtu.be/vWx8UTxsi0A?t=1123) **[[building-art-and-assets]]** Use atmosphere settings, sun rays and depth of field to separate foreground from background for a cinematic look.
- [19:23](https://youtu.be/vWx8UTxsi0A?t=1163) **[[building-art-and-assets]]** Analyze a movie still you like, then match its light direction, shadows and tone in Studio to learn cinematic lighting.
- [20:46](https://youtu.be/vWx8UTxsi0A?t=1246) **[[scripting-and-performance]]** Don't quadruple lights for global illumination; add a few bounce lights in key spots, since too many lights hurt optimization and can crash the game.
- [22:10](https://youtu.be/vWx8UTxsi0A?t=1330) **[[ai-and-tools]]** Use the Celestial Body Dragger plugin to move the sun or moon by mouse instead of tuning time of day and latitude.
- [23:35](https://youtu.be/vWx8UTxsi0A?t=1415) **[[building-art-and-assets]]** Counterbalance a strong skybox tint (it colors everything) when colors must stay accurate, such as brand colors.
- [26:19](https://youtu.be/vWx8UTxsi0A?t=1579) **[[building-art-and-assets]]** Limit the palette to two or three colors for a softer, calmer mood; a single color reads as more dramatic.
- [26:50](https://youtu.be/vWx8UTxsi0A?t=1610) **[[core-loops-and-game-design]]** Avoid saturated colors that conflict with game signals; players read red as lava and green as safe, so test colors in playtests.
- [27:31](https://youtu.be/vWx8UTxsi0A?t=1651) **[[playtesting-and-feedback]]** Run gameplay tests early: players avoided bushes and ran into hazards because the color cues misled them, prompting a redesign.
- [31:19](https://youtu.be/vWx8UTxsi0A?t=1879) **[[scripting-and-performance]]** Add lights as needed, then toggle them off and on to check which are actually necessary; use the lighting editor plugin by woot3 to audit light count and radius.
- [34:05](https://youtu.be/vWx8UTxsi0A?t=2045) **[[community-and-discord]]** Hold a kickoff meeting to get the team excited and introduce members to each other before starting a big project.
- [34:46](https://youtu.be/vWx8UTxsi0A?t=2086) **[[development-process-and-speed]]** Use a grid-based board like Milanote to lay out references and tasks; switch to Jira when the project gets more demanding.

## Games
- **Kingship Islands** [26:50](https://youtu.be/vWx8UTxsi0A?t=1610): Saturated lighting made players think green and red meant hazards; gameplay testing exposed the mismatch.
- **Soul de Janeiro (branded)** [5:16](https://youtu.be/vWx8UTxsi0A?t=316): Hid product shapes in Rio-inspired architecture so players constantly saw the brand.
- **Kung Fu Panda (branded)** [5:57](https://youtu.be/vWx8UTxsi0A?t=357): Built from sketches and a single viewing of the film; graybox to final stages shown.
- **Fenty Beauty skin experience** [16:50](https://youtu.be/vWx8UTxsi0A?t=1010): Used a magical, romantic lighting mood for one landmark.
- **Beetlejuice (branded)** [21:28](https://youtu.be/vWx8UTxsi0A?t=1288): Boiler room lighting: removed overhead shadows to make dark, cluttered interiors readable.
