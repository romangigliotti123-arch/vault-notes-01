# Roblox Developer Conference 2018 - Creating Worlds You Envision

[Watch](https://youtu.be/u0SxwDxFQB8) | RobloxOfficial | 4,298 views | 2018-07-24

## Summary
Roblox DevConf 2018 design talk by 3D lead Steven Jobe on making immersive, cohesive game worlds. Covers planning global design questions (genre, tone, camera perspective) before art, grey-boxing with proxies, consistency of mesh silhouette, color for focus, lighting as core rather than polish, and guiding players without telling them where to go.

## Tips
- [2:57](https://youtu.be/u0SxwDxFQB8?t=177) **[[core-loops-and-game-design]]** Answer global questions first (game type, tone, player camera perspective) before placing any art; they change UI, environment, and camera together.
- [8:29](https://youtu.be/u0SxwDxFQB8?t=509) **[[core-loops-and-game-design]]** Camera perspective changes layout: first-person needs close, narrowed focus; third-person needs multiple points of interest around the player; isometric needs clear foreground, midground, background.
- [13:53](https://youtu.be/u0SxwDxFQB8?t=833) **[[building-art-and-assets]]** Keep mesh detail and volume consistent across characters and environment; a chunky character in a thin, detailed world feels like it doesn't belong.
- [13:16](https://youtu.be/u0SxwDxFQB8?t=796) **[[building-art-and-assets]]** Check silhouettes by making models flat black; if each character or class is still recognisable at a glance, the shape language works.
- [15:48](https://youtu.be/u0SxwDxFQB8?t=948) **[[building-art-and-assets]]** Use a similar hue and saturation across the whole game, and reserve high contrast or complementary colours for what the player must focus on.
- [17:54](https://youtu.be/u0SxwDxFQB8?t=1074) **[[core-loops-and-game-design]]** Use color to tell players what enemies are and how dangerous they are, so threats are readable without text or tutorial.
- [20:19](https://youtu.be/u0SxwDxFQB8?t=1219) **[[first-session-onboarding]]** Make the play path brighter and more saturated than the surrounding set pieces, which are dimmed, so players instantly see where they can walk.
- [21:36](https://youtu.be/u0SxwDxFQB8?t=1296) **[[building-art-and-assets]]** Pick the palette, texture style (photoreal vs cartoon) and baked vs real-time lighting early, and apply them consistently across UI, decals and assets.
- [23:21](https://youtu.be/u0SxwDxFQB8?t=1401) **[[building-art-and-assets]]** Treat lighting as a foundation, not last-minute polish; lighting and skybox matching can make mismatched assets feel like one world.
- [24:58](https://youtu.be/u0SxwDxFQB8?t=1498) **[[building-art-and-assets]]** Adjust light direction, intensity and skybox together to change a scene's mood; matching the skybox to lighting makes the world feel unified.
- [26:58](https://youtu.be/u0SxwDxFQB8?t=1618) **[[development-process-and-speed]]** Grey-box the level first with primitives and toolbox assets, keeping it fast and flexible, to test mobility, focus and points of interest before textures.
- [29:00](https://youtu.be/u0SxwDxFQB8?t=1740) **[[building-art-and-assets]]** Place the main light so it doesn't flatten the key point of interest; in the demo, moving light off the focal object increased its contrast.
- [30:03](https://youtu.be/u0SxwDxFQB8?t=1803) **[[core-loops-and-game-design]]** Dim the space right around the player and make the key landmark the brightest object, so the player naturally heads toward it.
- [30:44](https://youtu.be/u0SxwDxFQB8?t=1844) **[[first-session-onboarding]]** Guide players by environment and asset placement rather than telling them where to go; players then feel they discovered the route themselves.
- [31:25](https://youtu.be/u0SxwDxFQB8?t=1885) **[[common-mistakes]]** Don't ignore environment quality: visual polish affects how outsiders judge a game's popularity, so unpolished art lowers results.
- [5:00](https://youtu.be/u0SxwDxFQB8?t=300) **[[game-ideas-and-genres]]** Define the game's tone (safe, frenetic, mysterious, playful) early; tone drives colors, lighting and asset treatment.
- [6:40](https://youtu.be/u0SxwDxFQB8?t=400) **[[core-loops-and-game-design]]** In open worlds, reuse assets heavily and use obstructions and partial blockers to steer players; Roblox instancing will reduce load for reused objects.
- [7:22](https://youtu.be/u0SxwDxFQB8?t=442) **[[core-loops-and-game-design]]** In obstacle courses, keep the play area clear: subdue and desaturate set pieces, and make collision match what players see, or they stop trusting the game.
- [38:53](https://youtu.be/u0SxwDxFQB8?t=2333) **[[building-art-and-assets]]** Games can break visual rules (different model styles) if they keep shared lighting and materials, which keeps the world feeling immersive.
- [39:34](https://youtu.be/u0SxwDxFQB8?t=2374) **[[core-loops-and-game-design]]** Limit play areas with deterrence: hills, dense trees, dim lighting and distant landmarks discourage going elsewhere; use invisible colliders as backup.
- [41:38](https://youtu.be/u0SxwDxFQB8?t=2498) **[[core-loops-and-game-design]]** For endless-looking worlds, use hills that rise and fall, fog, and far-off simplified objects; for edges, use cliffs or insurmountable water with visible limits.
- [34:04](https://youtu.be/u0SxwDxFQB8?t=2044) **[[ai-and-tools]]** Bake shadows by painting them in 3D Coat, ZBrush, Maya or Blender for discrete control, or use Studio's real-time lighting where it can change.
- [36:47](https://youtu.be/u0SxwDxFQB8?t=2207) **[[team-and-budget]]** To break into the industry, aim for an internship or a prop-modeling role, which teaches the production pipeline.

## Games
- **Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild** [5:59](https://youtu.be/u0SxwDxFQB8?t=359): Uses points of interest and partial obstructions to steer players without telling them where to go; reuses assets heavily in big worlds.
- **Donkey Kong Country** [7:22](https://youtu.be/u0SxwDxFQB8?t=442): Obstacle-course set pieces are subdued and less contrasting than the play space, so danger and path stand out.
- **Mirror's Edge** [10:33](https://youtu.be/u0SxwDxFQB8?t=633): First-person view gives clear footfall and hand cues; bright reds on a cool background focus the eye on the path.
- **Witcher 3** [9:52](https://youtu.be/u0SxwDxFQB8?t=592): Over-the-shoulder camera suits combat with enemies approaching from all sides; first-person would narrow focus too much.
- **Ori** [17:12](https://youtu.be/u0SxwDxFQB8?t=1032): Uses color to teach enemy types and difficulty at a glance.
- **Ninokuni** [20:19](https://youtu.be/u0SxwDxFQB8?t=1219): Bright, saturated road against dimmer, desaturated city shows the walkable path.
- **Feed Your Pets** [25:23](https://youtu.be/u0SxwDxFQB8?t=1523): Simplified, blocky, untextured models applied consistently from level to asset.
- **Bootleg Buccaneers** [26:46](https://youtu.be/u0SxwDxFQB8?t=1606): Lighting and materials keep detail level and mixed characters feeling like one world.
- **Super Mario Odyssey** [38:53](https://youtu.be/u0SxwDxFQB8?t=2333): Breaks rules on character realism but keeps lighting and materials consistent so the world still feels cohesive.
- **Toy Story** [24:01](https://youtu.be/u0SxwDxFQB8?t=1441): Toys from different makers feel like one world because lighting and materials are shared.
- **Neon District** [31:25](https://youtu.be/u0SxwDxFQB8?t=1885): Uses custom neon colors and lighting to get colors the default lighting cannot produce.
- **Egg Hunt** [32:07](https://youtu.be/u0SxwDxFQB8?t=1927): Distinct zones still feel like one big game because of shared visual language.
