# Brand Partnerships and IP Games Roundtable

[Watch](https://youtu.be/14RMDByrG1U) | RobloxCreators | 1,409 views | 2022-05-12

## Summary
Roundtable on brand partnerships and IP games on Roblox with three studios (Toya, Metaverse team, The Gang) and Roblox's DSPEV Dan. Covers what brand partnerships are, how to pick brands, deal and approval processes, communication, launch/live ops, defining success, and audience Q&A.

## Tips
- [12:12](https://youtu.be/14RMDByrG1U?t=732) **[[ads-and-sponsored-experiences]]** Brand IP lowers user acquisition costs because audiences already have affinity; retention still depends on the game being fun and authentic to the brand.
- [9:18](https://youtu.be/14RMDByrG1U?t=558) **[[game-ideas-and-genres]]** Before pitching, check that the brand's demographics (age, gender split) match the genre you want to build, and that your team actually wants to build it.
- [35:59](https://youtu.be/14RMDByrG1U?t=2159) **[[trend-research-and-validation]]** Benchmark what is succeeding on Roblox, then check whether those genres fit the IP; this helps convince brands to build a given game type.
- [27:41](https://youtu.be/14RMDByrG1U?t=1661) **[[development-process-and-speed]]** Brand partnerships take longer than internal projects; plan extra lead time for brand reviews and approvals, and be cautious about polish standards.
- [32:47](https://youtu.be/14RMDByrG1U?t=1967) **[[development-process-and-speed]]** Add buffer to schedules for revisions and approval windows; one team works in two-week sprints and found under 3-5 days or fewer than 2 revisions caused trouble.
- [33:29](https://youtu.be/14RMDByrG1U?t=2009) **[[team-and-budget]]** Be wary of minimum guarantee (MG) upfront payments offered by brands; they shift most risk to the developer while the brand carries little.
- [33:29](https://youtu.be/14RMDByrG1U?t=2009) **[[business-and-payouts]]** Discuss revenue share openly with the brand; large global brands may not allow rev share, while smaller brands may accept it, so the balance varies.
- [34:10](https://youtu.be/14RMDByrG1U?t=2050) **[[common-mistakes]]** Don't accept IP just because the licensor offers it; reps often push lesser-known titles. Pitch only IPs that genuinely interest you and fit the audience.
- [40:25](https://youtu.be/14RMDByrG1U?t=2425) **[[common-mistakes]]** Specify in the agreement which elements need approval (e.g., logos, specific models) versus which allow revisions (UI, effects); this avoids disputes.
- [41:14](https://youtu.be/14RMDByrG1U?t=2474) **[[first-session-onboarding]]** Explain Roblox to brand partners up front by walking them through joining an experience together, and translate their style into Roblox's visual language.
- [38:46](https://youtu.be/14RMDByrG1U?t=2326) **[[core-loops-and-game-design]]** Establish the core loop fast and make it tight; persistent brand games need a loop that gives players a reason to come back.
- [36:42](https://youtu.be/14RMDByrG1U?t=2202) **[[core-loops-and-game-design]]** Pick the game style that naturally fits the IP's fantasy (e.g., roleplay for a superhero show) and validate it with community playtests before showing the brand.
- [52:58](https://youtu.be/14RMDByrG1U?t=3178) **[[updates-live-ops]]** Plan a roadmap with the IP holder and sync weekly; have the first few post-launch updates planned before launch, then keep coordinating regularly.
- [56:22](https://youtu.be/14RMDByrG1U?t=3382) **[[updates-live-ops]]** Expect live-game curveballs (economy skew, overpowered items) and be ready to change the weekly plan on the fly.
- [58:14](https://youtu.be/14RMDByrG1U?t=3494) **[[mindset-and-strategy]]** Define success with the brand upfront: for marketing activations, reach and ratings (e.g., 80-85+ thumbs up); for licensed revenue games, revenue and user base for future deals.
- [58:56](https://youtu.be/14RMDByrG1U?t=3536) **[[mindset-and-strategy]]** Early in a studio's life, brand projects are about building credibility and shots on goal, even if they are not profitable.
- [22:24](https://youtu.be/14RMDByrG1U?t=1344) **[[social-features-and-virality]]** Build a portfolio of Roblox games first and network at licensing conferences or via LinkedIn to attract brand partners.
- [21:44](https://youtu.be/14RMDByrG1U?t=1304) **[[titles-descriptions]]** Cold-contact a brand's PR or licensing department with a track record of Roblox projects and a sample of what their brand could look like on the platform.
- [59:28](https://youtu.be/14RMDByrG1U?t=3568) **[[mindset-and-strategy]]** Communicate constantly with the brand, stay humble, and get communication expectations written into the agreement.
- [1:04:43](https://youtu.be/14RMDByrG1U?t=3883) **[[team-and-budget]]** Solo developers can start with a smaller brand product such as UGC, build a relationship, then scale to a full experience and form a team.
- [1:07:28](https://youtu.be/14RMDByrG1U?t=4048) **[[community-and-discord]]** Brands usually want to own the player base, so a brand-owned group is often preferred; community management must be planned for.
- [1:08:49](https://youtu.be/14RMDByrG1U?t=4129) **[[analytics-metrics]]** Use brand demographic data where provided and supplement with your own research; tools exist for tracking awareness and affinity.
- [1:09:32](https://youtu.be/14RMDByrG1U?t=4172) **[[core-loops-and-game-design]]** Immerse in the IP (read source material, watch the show) because fans notice inauthentic content and it can turn off the core evangelists.
- [1:10:53](https://youtu.be/14RMDByrG1U?t=4253) **[[game-ideas-and-genres]]** Balance casual and superfans by aiming for roughly 51% Roblox-native and 49% brand content, per the panel's rough guidance.

## Games
- **Lumberjack Legends** [6:35](https://youtu.be/14RMDByrG1U?t=395): The Gang's first Roblox project; it did not come out great, but it taught them the platform's learning curve.
- **Vans skate game (The Gang)** [37:25](https://youtu.be/14RMDByrG1U?t=2245): Brand-led concept with a tight core loop needed quickly, since the experience is persistent and must be worth returning to.
- **Miraculous roleplay game (Toya/ZAG)** [36:42](https://youtu.be/14RMDByrG1U?t=2202): Roleplay fit the superhero IP; validated with community playtests, updated weekly with a roadmap approved by the brand.
- **Nerf Strike** [45:56](https://youtu.be/14RMDByrG1U?t=2756): Licensed real-world blasters drove recognition; modelling each gun and part required performance work and many approval cycles.
- **Angry Birds Bird Island (Rovio)** [41:55](https://youtu.be/14RMDByrG1U?t=2515): Brand's own marketing team helped create branded assets; lowered acquisition but retention still needs a fun game.
- **Strongman Simulator** [30:05](https://youtu.be/14RMDByrG1U?t=1805): Used as an existing reference product to set client expectations on time and scope.
