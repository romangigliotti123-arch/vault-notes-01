# Finding the Fun with Jandel and Foryxe

[Watch](https://youtu.be/rVr0IyfYkPg) | RobloxCreators | 5,027 views | 2021-11-18

## Summary
Level Up roundtable with Jandel and Foryxe (Splittingpoint, Wacky Wizards). Covers finding fun via playtesting and soft launches, pivoting fast, community feedback channels, weekly update cadence, a poorly received pet update, scoping small, and when to kill projects.

## Tips
- [5:33](https://youtu.be/rVr0IyfYkPg?t=333) **[[playtesting-and-feedback]]** Soft release games quietly: minimal announcement, light advertising, let a few players in, and fix what they get stuck on in the first week.
- [4:32](https://youtu.be/rVr0IyfYkPg?t=272) **[[core-loops-and-game-design]]** Test your own game for long stretches; if you keep getting lost in testing, it's a good sign it's fun. If you're bored, players will be too.
- [6:13](https://youtu.be/rVr0IyfYkPg?t=373) **[[core-loops-and-game-design]]** Start with small core concepts and test them; if one mechanic gets players engaged, pivot the game toward it and invest more time only then.
- [10:14](https://youtu.be/rVr0IyfYkPg?t=614) **[[core-loops-and-game-design]]** Make player activity visible to others (Wacky Wizards moved from private rooms to an open world), enabling sharing potions and discovering recipes.
- [11:30](https://youtu.be/rVr0IyfYkPg?t=690) **[[playtesting-and-feedback]]** Get as many different players as possible to try the game early, including cousins and kids, and watch them silently to see where they get stuck.
- [12:11](https://youtu.be/rVr0IyfYkPg?t=731) **[[community-and-discord]]** Use the Roblox group for broad feedback and Discord for one-on-one idea discussions; kids and players give honest sentiment.
- [13:33](https://youtu.be/rVr0IyfYkPg?t=813) **[[discovery-algorithm]]** Search YouTube for recent uploads of your game (sorted by newest) to see unfiltered new-player behavior and spot UX confusion.
- [14:54](https://youtu.be/rVr0IyfYkPg?t=894) **[[updates-live-ops]]** Don't let players vote on upcoming updates without clearly stating the order; it caused anger when players thought they'd miss options.
- [17:37](https://youtu.be/rVr0IyfYkPg?t=1057) **[[common-mistakes]]** Don't add community-requested features that don't fit the core loop; the pet update was poorly integrated and was judged bad.
- [20:20](https://youtu.be/rVr0IyfYkPg?t=1220) **[[updates-live-ops]]** Weekly updates let you quickly pivot away from an unpopular update; the next one is only a week away.
- [20:44](https://youtu.be/rVr0IyfYkPg?t=1244) **[[common-mistakes]]** Don't over-complicate early games; successful Roblox games centre on a single fun core idea (Piggy, Bee Swarm Simulator).
- [22:07](https://youtu.be/rVr0IyfYkPg?t=1327) **[[development-process-and-speed]]** Launch with a small scope (Wacky Wizards shipped with about 50-60 potions) and expand weekly based on what players actually do.
- [24:10](https://youtu.be/rVr0IyfYkPg?t=1450) **[[common-mistakes]]** Filter additions by whether they fit what your game is meant to be; a housing system was rejected as too far from the core game.
- [26:44](https://youtu.be/rVr0IyfYkPg?t=1604) **[[retention-d1-d7]]** Track playtime as the key growth signal; long sessions in early playtests suggest friends will join and the game will spread.
- [28:44](https://youtu.be/rVr0IyfYkPg?t=1724) **[[development-process-and-speed]]** If a new game gets no players in its first week or two, consider ditching it; the speaker shelved about 16 of roughly 20 games that way.
- [30:06](https://youtu.be/rVr0IyfYkPg?t=1806) **[[community-and-discord]]** Engage players daily early on via group shouts, Discord, and in-game global chat, and build hype with a regular themed event day.
- [31:29](https://youtu.be/rVr0IyfYkPg?t=1889) **[[updates-live-ops]]** Set a regular update schedule and announce the date in advance (group post, update label, countdown in game); this spikes players before launch.
- [34:09](https://youtu.be/rVr0IyfYkPg?t=2049) **[[development-process-and-speed]]** Write every idea into a backlog and avoid swapping projects constantly; the speaker used to change projects every two weeks and lost progress.
- [41:52](https://youtu.be/rVr0IyfYkPg?t=2512) **[[development-process-and-speed]]** Prototype fast and accept messy code; get it out so players can play it, since code quality matters less than getting feedback.
- [43:56](https://youtu.be/rVr0IyfYkPg?t=2636) **[[playtesting-and-feedback]]** Get feedback from the first players by spying invisibly or using an alt; the first 50 players reveal most stuck points.
- [49:23](https://youtu.be/rVr0IyfYkPg?t=2963) **[[analytics-metrics]]** Track purchases and playtime with GameAnalytics and Roblox's built-in analytics; deeper tracking of drop-offs pays off for big updates.
- [53:28](https://youtu.be/rVr0IyfYkPg?t=3208) **[[analytics-metrics]]** Expect playtime per session to shrink once a game gets many players; around 15-20 minutes average is strong, 12 minutes is still decent.
- [1:00:18](https://youtu.be/rVr0IyfYkPg?t=3618) **[[updates-live-ops]]** Run major live events every few months and build them up with smaller updates; they draw a large player spike.
- [1:06:56](https://youtu.be/rVr0IyfYkPg?t=4016) **[[mindset-and-strategy]]** Accept failure as a normal part of learning and move on to the next project; some games only take off after a week of low numbers.
