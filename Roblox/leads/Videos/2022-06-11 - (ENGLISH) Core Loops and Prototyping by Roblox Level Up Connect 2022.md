# (ENGLISH) Core Loops and Prototyping by Roblox Level Up | Connect 2022

[Watch](https://youtu.be/Mep4_w8qxeE) | leads | 797 views | 2022-06-11

## Summary
Roblox Level Up (Connect 2022) panel by Dan Spventa (Roblox game insights). Explains core loops as the most repeated minute-to-minute action, "tumors" that compete with the core loop, paper vs studio prototyping, vertical slices and MVP, and when to add analytics.

## Tips
- [0:23](https://youtu.be/Mep4_w8qxeE?t=23) **[[core-loops-and-game-design]]** Define one core loop: the most repeated minute-to-minute action. If it isn't fun, the rest of the game won't be either.
- [13:28](https://youtu.be/Mep4_w8qxeE?t=808) **[[core-loops-and-game-design]]** Before adding any system, state which part of the core loop it improves. Systems that don't tie back to the loop feel random and jumbled.
- [15:01](https://youtu.be/Mep4_w8qxeE?t=901) **[[common-mistakes]]** Avoid 'tumors': systems that compete with the core loop for attention. Players split badly, one system dies, and some players quit.
- [19:42](https://youtu.be/Mep4_w8qxeE?t=1182) **[[development-process-and-speed]]** Prototype early (first 2-3 weeks) to find flaws, fun and technical roadblocks before building maps, art and features on top.
- [22:43](https://youtu.be/Mep4_w8qxeE?t=1363) **[[development-process-and-speed]]** Use paper prototypes for fast design iteration and UI/mobile layout testing; use studio prototypes for physics, AI and technical risk.
- [26:01](https://youtu.be/Mep4_w8qxeE?t=1561) **[[ui-ux-design]]** Test controls and layout on paper first. Around 66-67% of Roblox players are on mobile, and control design makes or breaks success.
- [28:14](https://youtu.be/Mep4_w8qxeE?t=1694) **[[development-process-and-speed]]** Build studio prototypes with reusable code, not throwaway hacks, though temporary placeholders (cylinders, auto-hit towers) are fine.
- [30:16](https://youtu.be/Mep4_w8qxeE?t=1816) **[[development-process-and-speed]]** Prototype the core loop first. It aligns the team and tells artists and programmers what to build next.
- [32:20](https://youtu.be/Mep4_w8qxeE?t=1940) **[[playtesting-and-feedback]]** Playtest with friends, family and followers who haven't seen the game. First-time players reveal gaps the team can't see.
- [35:13](https://youtu.be/Mep4_w8qxeE?t=2113) **[[development-process-and-speed]]** Milestones: prototype (core loop), then vertical slice (one login-to-logout session), then MVP (all features needed to ship).
- [37:44](https://youtu.be/Mep4_w8qxeE?t=2264) **[[analytics-metrics]]** Skip metrics during prototyping. Use qualitative feedback early and add analytics close to launch, when most systems exist.
- [38:48](https://youtu.be/Mep4_w8qxeE?t=2328) **[[core-loops-and-game-design]]** Aim for one core loop per experience. Multiple loops usually mean the loop is overcomplicated or a tumor is present.
- [39:42](https://youtu.be/Mep4_w8qxeE?t=2382) **[[core-loops-and-game-design]]** Remove social gates from the core loop. Adopt Me's solo pet feature boosted engagement after parents needed another player to earn currency.
- [42:13](https://youtu.be/Mep4_w8qxeE?t=2533) **[[core-loops-and-game-design]]** The core loop should work solo or multiplayer. Give single-player players meaningful loop actions, as Jailbreak did with armored vehicle heists.
- [44:21](https://youtu.be/Mep4_w8qxeE?t=2661) **[[core-loops-and-game-design]]** Separate core loop (repeated actions) from progression (levels, unlocks). Give max-level players an endgame, a season, or a periodic reset.
- [50:12](https://youtu.be/Mep4_w8qxeE?t=3012) **[[core-loops-and-game-design]]** Design the whole experience around the core loop, especially early on. Unanchored cool ideas create a confusing mess and a short novelty period.
- [52:31](https://youtu.be/Mep4_w8qxeE?t=3151) **[[common-mistakes]]** Don't spend months on maps, characters or tech without showing core gameplay. Teams that did this often threw away large amounts of work.
- [55:10](https://youtu.be/Mep4_w8qxeE?t=3310) **[[development-process-and-speed]]** If a feature would take more than 2-3 weeks, use a cheap intermediate solution to get an early feel for the game.

## Games
- **Jailbreak** [06:35](https://youtu.be/Mep4_w8qxeE?t=395): Cops and criminals share one core loop (explore, complete objective, power up). Vertical slice = one prison, one bank, guns and arrest.
- **Robloxian High School** [09:19](https://youtu.be/Mep4_w8qxeE?t=559): Core loop is explore, attend class, customize. Added mini-games and a season pass; class attendance was improved after it felt boring.
- **Adopt Me** [39:42](https://youtu.be/Mep4_w8qxeE?t=2382): Social-gated currency slowed the economy. The single-player pet feature increased engagement a lot.
- **Arsenal** [42:54](https://youtu.be/Mep4_w8qxeE?t=2574): Rounds earn currency that buys cosmetics, which players then use in later rounds. Multiplayer still feeds the core loop.
- **Escape from Tarkov** [44:21](https://youtu.be/Mep4_w8qxeE?t=2661): Core loop is raid, then return to base. Progression resets (data wipe) every six months to manage max-level players.
- **Pokemon** [46:28](https://youtu.be/Mep4_w8qxeE?t=2788): Example of an elder system: catching all Pokemon gives long-term goals for top players.

## Quotes
- [35:13](https://youtu.be/Mep4_w8qxeE?t=2113) "Prototype is the first milestone you want to hit and MVP is the last one you want to hit before you eventually ship your game."
- [52:31](https://youtu.be/Mep4_w8qxeE?t=3151) "What is your core loop? Get your core loop up early and think about how all of your systems work with it."
