# Research Methods to Improve Live Game Retention, D1 to D100

[Watch](https://youtu.be/WGRpglQ87XE) | leads | 7,397 views | 2022-02-01

## Summary
UX researchers from Glue, Sony San Diego, and Scopely explain how to use playtests, A/B tests, event funnel tagging, diary studies and longitudinal studies to find and fix retention problems in live mobile and console games. Each case study shows a method, the design change it drove, and the measured result.

## Tips
- [9:28](https://youtu.be/WGRpglQ87XE?t=568) **[[first-session-onboarding]]** Playtest the first-time experience with recruited target-audience players before shipping. Watch for usability gaps; Design Home found players missed room requirements, which caused churn.
- [16:09](https://youtu.be/WGRpglQ87XE?t=969) **[[ab-testing-and-experiments]]** Roll out new onboarding as an A/B test with a small traffic slice (Design Home used 5% vs 95%) and scale up only if retention improves.
- [17:22](https://youtu.be/WGRpglQ87XE?t=1042) **[[analytics-metrics]]** Tag each onboarding step as an event to build a funnel; the funnel pinpoints the exact churn points to fix.
- [16:49](https://youtu.be/WGRpglQ87XE?t=1009) **[[retention-d1-d7]]** Track day-2 and day-7 retention by cohort. Early retention gains cascade to later days, so fix early churn first.
- [30:44](https://youtu.be/WGRpglQ87XE?t=1844) **[[playtesting-and-feedback]]** Run diary studies of about 40-50 players for several months. They reveal how the game unfolds over time and why players drop, which one-off playtests miss.
- [25:16](https://youtu.be/WGRpglQ87XE?t=1516) **[[analytics-metrics]]** Pair qualitative findings with telemetry. Small samples explain why something happens; telemetry shows how many players are affected.
- [28:43](https://youtu.be/WGRpglQ87XE?t=1723) **[[updates-live-ops]]** After a live event fails, patch fast. MLB The Show added an icon marking eligible players and surfaced the needed info on the card, more than doubling event entry.
- [37:50](https://youtu.be/WGRpglQ87XE?t=2270) **[[core-loops-and-game-design]]** When missions feel repetitive or stall, add varied ways to play such as daily goals and rotating limited-time events, so players always have something meaningful to do.
- [40:36](https://youtu.be/WGRpglQ87XE?t=2436) **[[first-session-onboarding]]** Teach with memorable, high-value examples. Use a famous fan-favourite character as the reward and the main protagonist as the example for leveling, so players learn the right priorities.
- [50:01](https://youtu.be/WGRpglQ87XE?t=3001) **[[playtesting-and-feedback]]** Use open daily diary surveys and rate motivation each day on a 1-7 scale. Plot the scores to find dips in the experience.
- [51:33](https://youtu.be/WGRpglQ87XE?t=3093) **[[playtesting-and-feedback]]** Layer what players say with telemetry. Cooking Dash saw that players loved prize wheels despite misunderstanding them, which led to clearer design.
- [54:14](https://youtu.be/WGRpglQ87XE?t=3254) **[[events-and-seasonal-content]]** Run the diary study before a live event launches to gauge satisfaction before and after. Cooking Dash saw a 16-point NPS-style increase after its fixes.
- [55:25](https://youtu.be/WGRpglQ87XE?t=3325) **[[core-loops-and-game-design]]** If a feature is confusing, give it a real gameplay effect. Cooking Dash's pets were cosmetic, so they were changed to auto-serve food.
- [42:40](https://youtu.be/WGRpglQ87XE?t=2560) **[[common-mistakes]]** Do not judge a game by a single session. Many important issues only appear over days or weeks, so use longitudinal tests.
- [43:29](https://youtu.be/WGRpglQ87XE?t=2609) **[[playtesting-and-feedback]]** Keep longitudinal studies cheap: most play is unmoderated, so reuse short broad daily surveys and spend moderator time on a follow-up interview.
- [58:14](https://youtu.be/WGRpglQ87XE?t=3494) **[[playtesting-and-feedback]]** Use a small sample to understand why something happens. Use telemetry to measure how widespread it is.

## Numbers
- [8:48](https://youtu.be/WGRpglQ87XE?t=528) Day-2 retention example: **35% to 40% (illustrative)** (Glue example: lifting day 2 is expected to lift days 3-7 too)
- [19:31](https://youtu.be/WGRpglQ87XE?t=1171) Diary study size: **~50 players (40-50 typical)** (Sony MLB The Show; mix of spenders and non-spenders, new and experienced)
- [20:11](https://youtu.be/WGRpglQ87XE?t=1211) Diary study duration: **3-4 months** (Sony MLB The Show, tracking a full baseball season)
- [16:09](https://youtu.be/WGRpglQ87XE?t=969) A/B traffic split: **95% control / 5% new onboarding** (Glue Design Home new-user onboarding test)
- [34:06](https://youtu.be/WGRpglQ87XE?t=2046) Longitudinal study structure: **~6 on-site players, 1-2 weeks at home at 30-60 min/day** (Scopely: on-site test, diary study, follow-up interview)

## Games
- **Design Home** [8:08](https://youtu.be/WGRpglQ87XE?t=488): Teach requirements, inventory and filters in onboarding; a 3-challenge tutorial plus a granular filter walkthrough fixed early churn.
- **MLB The Show** [25:56](https://youtu.be/WGRpglQ87XE?t=1556): Events failed when players could not identify eligible players; add in-game markers and surface key info on the card.
- **Star Trek Fleet Command** [35:41](https://youtu.be/WGRpglQ87XE?t=2141): Missions felt repetitive and stalled; daily goals and rotating limited events added variety and relieved repetition.
- **The Walking Dead: Road to Survival** [38:33](https://youtu.be/WGRpglQ87XE?t=2313): Players used weak characters; featuring recognisable fan favourites as rewards and examples taught the right investment priorities.
- **Cooking Dash** [44:57](https://youtu.be/WGRpglQ87XE?t=2697): Procedural live event confused players about style; redesigned visuals, removed progress bar and gave pets a gameplay effect.
