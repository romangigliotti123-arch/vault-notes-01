Personal-use research note. Brief quotes only, with attribution; see the original at the URL above.

## Summary
Roblox creator doc on testing poses after rigging a clothing mesh. Covers showing armature bones In Front, entering Pose mode, rotating bones with R, saving or cancelling rotations, clearing user transforms, and keeping the armature attached so the clothing layers correctly in Studio. Warns that deformation problems need weight painting, which the page does not cover.

## Claims
- c1: Test clothing deformation by enabling Armature Properties > Viewport Display > Show > In Front so bones are visible and selectable for posing. *(doc, high; rigging-animation)*
- c2: Pose by switching to Pose mode, selecting bones, pressing R to rotate; left-click saves rotation, right-click cancels unsaved rotation. *(doc, high; rigging-animation)*
- c3: Reset a saved pose with right-click and Clear User Transforms. *(doc, high; rigging-animation)*
- c4: If clothing deforms badly during posing, fix it with manual weight painting; the doc defers to 'Skin a simple mesh' and 'Skin a humanoid mesh' guides. *(doc, high; common-mistakes-fixes)*
- c5: Do not delete the armature after testing; it is required for the t-shirt to layer correctly in Studio. *(doc, high; avatar-ugc-accessories)*

## Techniques
- t1: **Bone pose test**: Set armature In Front, enter Pose mode, select bones, press R to rotate, click to save, test natural poses, then right-click > Clear User Transforms to reset.

## Quotes (brief, for attribution)
> Do not delete the armature as it is a vital component for your t-shirt to layer correctly in Studio.  (create.roblox.com)
