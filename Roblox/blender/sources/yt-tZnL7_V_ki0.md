## Summary
Weekly 3D-AI news roundup (Stefan 3D AI, #20). Covers Meshy 7.1 4K mesh output, Tripo Smart UV and P2 public release, WorldSculpt object extraction from Gaussian splats, GPT-6 Astra animation claims, Kai Ninja part-level generation, Tencent Hunyuan 3D, Snap3D and HKTex UV-free texturing. Nothing on Blender, blender-mcp, bpy, Claude, or Roblox export/import.

## Claims
- c1: [0:30] Meshy 7.1 adds 4K ('Ultra 4K') mesh resolution; creator's test cost 35 credits. Creator says it is useful for 3D printing and displacement. *(demo, medium; mesh-texture-limits)*
- c2: [3:00] Tripo Smart UV auto-unwraps meshes; about 20 credits yields up to four variations. Creator calls it the first AI UV unwrap that works, for organic and hard-surface models. *(opinion, low; mesh-texture-limits)*
- c3: [3:00] Tripo P2 moved from beta to public and generates four variations for the same price. Creator says low-poly quality improved. *(doc, medium; other)*
- c4: [3:40] Creator says Hunyuan 3D Studio UV was unstable and took up to 10 minutes, and that it has been in beta for six months or more. *(opinion, low; mesh-texture-limits)*
- c5: [4:15] WorldSculpt extracts per-object meshes from 3D Gaussian splats, claimed watertight; example office split into 678 objects. Built on TRELLIS 2. *(doc, medium; other)*
- c6: [6:42] Creator reports GPT-6 Astra produced animation for a multi-legged scorpion, and says it works best with APIs and libraries; he rates the gap versus a prior model as large. *(experience, low; rigging-animation)*
- c7: [7:55] Kai Ninja extends TRELLIS 2 to generate separate part meshes from one image, e.g. 15 parts for a chair. Model not yet released; license unknown. *(doc, medium; other)*
- c8: [9:25] Tencent Hunyuan 3D model (named Miora 3D in transcript) is in beta on a Tencent platform and Tencent Cloud API. Creator sees no major quality gain. *(opinion, low; other)*
- c9: [11:10] HKTex (heat kernel textures) defines texture data directly on the surface, removing the need for UV unwrapping; creator says results look similar to UV-based texturing. *(doc, low; other)*
