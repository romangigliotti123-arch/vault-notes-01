# web-e84b206009

Personal-use research note. Brief quotes only, with attribution; see the original at the URL above.

## Summary
Roblox docs on layered clothing caging: start from official cage templates, keep cage UVs and vertex counts unchanged, fit outer cage tightly over garment, avoid stacked/collapsed vertices, and avoid inner/outer cage misconfigurations that fail validation. Covers no Blender MCP, bpy, or FBX pipeline content.

## Claims
- c1: Caging defines inner and outer surfaces of a layered accessory; proper cages let accessories stretch and layer together. Improper cages can cause cosmetic issues. *(doc, high; avatar-ugc-accessories)*
- c2: Roblox may block uploads with improper cages to the Marketplace and may remove existing catalog items; Roblox is enforcing stricter layered accessory validation rules. *(doc, high; avatar-ugc-accessories)*
- c3: Always start caging from Roblox's provided Clothing_Cage_Templates.zip, which include inner and outer cages with properly set UVs. *(doc, high; avatar-ugc-accessories)*
- c4: Do not edit cage UVs; doing so introduces visual artifacts. *(doc, high; avatar-ugc-accessories)*
- c5: Clothing mesh placed between inner and outer cage is deformed by the layering system; this is the typical configuration for nearly all layered accessories. *(doc, high; avatar-ugc-accessories)*
- c6: Outer cage edits: move vertices outward along normals, keep cage close to clothing, work symmetrically, keep vertex/edge/face count unchanged, do not alter UVs, do not stack vertices. *(doc, high; avatar-ugc-accessories)*
- c7: Stacked (overlapping) cage vertices cause artifacts when other layers go on top; collapsed vertices are rejected during validation. *(doc, high; avatar-ugc-accessories)*
- c8: Keep cage edge spacing even and align cage break lines with joints (e.g., elbow at LowerArm joint) to improve animation and posing. *(doc, medium; avatar-ugc-accessories)*
- c9: Form-fitting clothes: outer cage must fully envelop the garment with minimal gaps; do not move outer cage vertices that do not cover the garment (e.g., lower legs for a t-shirt). *(doc, high; avatar-ugc-accessories)*
- c10: Bulky clothing (puffy jackets, hoodies): displace outer cage along vertex normals, move neighboring vertices in similar directions to keep smooth relative displacement. *(doc, medium; avatar-ugc-accessories)*
- c11: Protrusions (puffy hoods, shoulder pads, spikes): keep outer cage below protruding region so it stays visible when other layers are equipped; placing cage above causes other layers to cover it. *(doc, high; avatar-ugc-accessories)*
- c12: Validation rejects: cages missing heads or limbs; outer cage inside inner cage; layered accessory fully outside its outer cage (behaves like rigid accessory, does not deform). *(doc, high; avatar-ugc-accessories)*
- c13: Clothing mesh inside the inner cage causes artifacts when other layers are worn; clothing partly outside outer cage is not deformed and breaks fit of other layers. *(doc, high; avatar-ugc-accessories)*
- c14: Identical inner and outer cages prevent the layering system from distinguishing the accessory, causing unexpected deformation or failed rendering. *(doc, high; avatar-ugc-accessories)*
- c15: Oversized gap between accessory and outer cage causes severe deformation when combined with other layered accessories. *(doc, medium; avatar-ugc-accessories)*
- c16: Source does not mention Blender MCP, bpy scripting, Claude, or the Blender-to-Roblox FBX/glTF export pipeline; it is only Roblox layered-clothing caging guidance. *(doc, high; other)*

## Numbers
- n1: **Outer cage vertex/edge/face count**: must stay identical to starting template 
- n2: **Cage break line alignment**: aligned to joint positions, e.g., elbow to LowerArm joint 

## Techniques
- t1: **Outer cage normal-direction offset**: Start from Roblox cage template; move outer cage vertices outward along normals, symmetrically, keeping close to garment and UVs untouched.
