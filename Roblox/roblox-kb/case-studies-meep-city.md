# Case study: Meep City

**Genre:** Social hangout Roblox game

Early large Roblox social game built on mesh terrain. Known for friends-list join loops; also cited for hacking, moderation failures and a decline tied to unmoderated player parties.

Also referred to as: MeepCity

## Numbers
- No CCU or revenue figures in the notes.

## What creators say about it
- A friends list with online status and click-to-join teleport lets friend-online notifications drive joins. ([Roblox 2018-07 7:21](https://youtu.be/4rvzuYMtSlA?t=441))
- The developer (2021) says mesh terrain causes ongoing collision and map-building delays. ([Roblox Creators 2021-09 52:15](https://youtu.be/UlKAoiFLgUo?t=3135))
- Unmoderated player parties caused major safety issues and a decline; moderation is essential. Newer 2025 Roblox guidance says developers are not moderated for user behaviour, and bad-scene detection targets servers with design changes requested before action. ([Daiorg 2025-08 11:23](https://youtu.be/GkKUU9mCgwc?t=683), [Roblox 2025-08 26:48](https://youtu.be/cuPGe99m8zs?t=1608))
- Cited as a well-known big game that was hacked, so security is a real risk for large titles. ([BloxianCode 2022-02 0:00](https://youtu.be/SN7MyJFOQkU?t=0))
- Social games may have skewed report distributions; read report counts with caution. ([Last Level Studios 2025-11 21:46](https://youtu.be/KNHngRRMPVI?t=1306))

## Transferable patterns
- Friend-online notifications to drive join loops
- Proactive moderation for social spaces
- Avoid mesh terrain where performance matters (creator-stated)
- Security hardening for large titles
