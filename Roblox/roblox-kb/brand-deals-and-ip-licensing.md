# Brand Deals And Ip Licensing

_brand partnerships, IP licensing, licensed games, sponsored experiences for brands, immersive ads._

Evidence base: 119 extracted items from 25 videos by Business of Creators, Cumin, GameMakers, GamesBeat, Last Level Studios, RoWatcher (tracker blog), Roblox, Roblox Creator Docs, Roblox Creators, Roblox DevForum, ThatGuyEvan, Ventures, VuukStudios, rasdev (2021-12-09 to 2026-10-30). Roblox changes quickly: check the date on each source and see Date context below.

## Overview
Creators and Roblox docs cover two tracks: (1) brand deals and sponsored integrations (Brand Developer Directory, Brand Link, in-game ads), and (2) the platform's License Manager for IP licensing. Indie advice is to take deals only when they fit the game and community. Forced branding can hurt engagement, and some devs reportedly earned less after sponsorships (unverified). Official docs explain the licensing mechanics. Licenses are full-game or in-game sales (game-pass and developer-product level), and either perpetual or time-limited. Rights holders set a 0–95% revenue share and content standards. Offers auto-activate after 14 days unless disputed. Conditional offers need rights-holder approval. Creators should pitch well with visuals and avoid using IP early. They must remove IP and sales items when a license expires. Only one full-game IP is allowed per game. Rights holders need verifiable IP documentation and at least 10 approved images.

## Rules and findings
- **Only take brand deals that fit the game and community** (multiple-creators): Integrate brands so they fit the experience. Forced branding (e.g. a 'Walmart game') hurts engagement and revisits, and poor fits disrupt players. Ventures says some developers reportedly made less revenue after sponsorships than if they had left the game alone (self-reported, unverified). Newest source 2026-10-30.

- **Know that brands pay for placements and events** (single-mention): Brands pay for in-game advertising and event placements. Large games such as Adopt Me and Brookhaven have run them. - **Choose between full-game and in-game sales licenses** (multiple-creators): There are two license types, each perpetual or time-limited. An in-game sales license ties licensed IP to specific game passes and developer products, not the whole game. It can be added to an existing game for an event, season or limited mechanic, with no full game license needed. - **Respect the single-IP and combination limits** (multiple-creators): Only one IP per game is allowed. You can hold multiple in-game sales licenses for different IPs in one game. You cannot combine a full game license for one IP with a sales license for another. Time-limited licenses follow the same single-IP rules. Sources: [Roblox DevForum 2026-06](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/x/4701733), [Roblox DevForum 2026-09](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/x/4868288), [Roblox DevForum 2026-09](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/licensing-platform-update-in-game-sales-license-and-3-new-ko/4868288). - **IP must be central to the full game** (single-mention): IP must be central to and present throughout the full game. Licenses cannot cover a single level or portion of a game (full-game licenses). Sources: [Roblox Creator Docs 2026-10](https://create.roblox.com/docs/ip-licensing/faq). Newest source 2026-10-28.
- **Do not use the IP before the start date** (multiple-creators): Early use can trigger early activation of a request or agreement. Update your game to use the IP before the start date, since monetization activates automatically on the agreed start date. Sources: [Roblox Creator Docs 2026-10](https://create.roblox.com/docs/ip-licensing/creators), [Roblox DevForum 2026-06](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/x/4701733). Newest source 2026-10-28.
- **Give time-limited licenses enough lead time** (multiple-creators): Propose a start date with lead time. Requests starting fewer than 3 days out may not be approved in time. Time-limited duration is 3 days to 3 months. Start and end are based on midnight UTC. Sources: [Roblox DevForum 2026-06](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/x/4701733), [Roblox DevForum 2026-06](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/x/4701733), [Roblox DevForum 2026-06](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/x/4701733), [Roblox Creator Docs 2026-10](https://create.roblox.com/docs/ip-licensing/license-manager). - **Cancel pending requests in time** (multiple-creators): You can cancel a pending request up to 72 hours (3 days) before the start, and only if no early IP use is detected. If you cancel but still want the IP, submit a new request. - **Respond to offers within 14 days** (multiple-creators): Standard offers auto-activate after 14 days unless disputed. To reject one you must dispute it, not ignore it. Creators can accept earlier. Disputes must be filed within 14 days, and you can dispute standard or conditional offers but not a conditional approval of your own request. - **Handle conditional offers actively** (multiple-creators): Conditional offers give you 14 days to make changes and mark them done, dispute, or cancel. They do not auto-activate. The rights holder must approve afterward, and Roblox does not auto-verify. If the window lapses the offer expires and the rights holder may file an infringement claim. - **Plan for license expiry and termination** (multiple-creators): At expiry, remove licensed game passes and developer products from sale (players keep prior purchases). Remove the IP within 3 days and self-attest, or the agreement becomes unresolved and may lead to a perpetual license or DMCA request. On termination for reasons like repeated violations you get at least 30 days notice. ## Checklist
- [ ] Decide whether the brand or IP genuinely fits your game and audience
- [ ] Check the partner's reputation and past event reception
- [ ] Confirm your game is public
- [ ] Open License Manager in Creator Hub and review the IP's revenue share, scope and content standards
- [ ] Choose a full-game or in-game sales license (perpetual or time-limited)
- [ ] Confirm no conflicting IP license on the game (one IP per game)
- [ ] Prepare a pitch: vision, creative elements, gameplay fit, timeline
- [ ] Name the game passes and developer products that will use the IP
- [ ] Attach references, mockups or sketches
- [ ] Set the start date at least 3 days ahead, in midnight UTC terms
- [ ] Do not use the IP before the start date
- [ ] Update your game before the start date
- [ ] Cancel at least 72 hours before start if you cannot make it
- [ ] Respond to any offer within 14 days (accept, dispute or mark changes done)
- [ ] Make requested conditional changes and wait for rights holder approval
- [ ] Track the expiry date
- [ ] At expiry, remove IP and licensed items from sale within 3 days and self-attest
- [ ] Avoid negotiation or off-platform contact in license messages
- [ ] Apply to Brand Developer Directory or Brand Link if you have brand experience
- [ ] Rights holders: upload 10+ verifiable, distinct images with citations
- [ ] Rights holders: set revenue share, duration and visibility before first use
- [ ] Rights holders: file infringement via Rights Manager only as the rights holder
- [ ] Keep a dated record of your original designs, characters and logos to support any clone or infringement claim.
- [ ] When a clone or unlicensed adaptation appears, document it and file Rights Manager takedowns first; involve counsel before any lawsuit.
- [ ] Write a lore and backstory document during development, not after launch.
- [ ] If planning a series, consider a short animated film as a proof-of-audience step before a crowdfunding campaign.
- [ ] For avatar item brand deals, get design credit, distribution scope, royalty rate and term in writing before designing.
- [ ] When proposing a brand integration, consider offering the brand a character or NPC role inside the game, not only a logo or banner placement.
- [ ] Check that a brand character fits the game's tone and community before pitching it, as with any brand deal.

## Instructions for an agent using this file
- If a developer asks whether to take a brand deal, then assess fit with the game and community first. Warn that forced branding can hurt engagement and that revenue gains are not guaranteed.
- If a developer wants an IP for an event, season or limited mechanic, then recommend a time-limited in-game sales license on the existing game.
- If the game is built around one IP, then recommend a full game license. Do not combine it with a sales license for another IP.
- If the developer plans a time-limited license, then set the start date at least 3 days out and keep the duration within 3 days to 3 months. Use the IP only from the start date, and cancel at least 72 hours before start if needed.
- If a developer drafts a pitch, then include vision, creative elements, gameplay fit, timeline, named passes and products, and visuals. Remind them to check the content standards first.
- If a developer receives a standard offer they disagree with, then dispute it within 14 days. Ignoring it means it auto-activates.
- If a developer receives a conditional offer, then make the changes and mark them done within 14 days. Activation still needs rights holder approval.
- If a license is expiring, then remove the licensed items from sale and the IP from the game within 3 days, and self-attest. Players keep prior purchases.
- If the user is a rights holder, then advise on 10+ verifiable images, locking revenue share and scope before first use, perpetual versus time-limited, and specific rejection feedback.
- If the user asks about brand partnerships without an IP, then point them to the Brand Developer Directory or Brand Link (invite-only, ideally 500k+ MAU). Treat indie revenue claims as unverified.
- If asked about clone protection, cite the lawsuit count as self-reported, from a Feb 2026 tracker blog, and note it is unverified.
- If asked about licensing avatar items to brands, state that royalty and contract terms are not covered by the sources and suggest checking current Roblox creator policy.
- If asked about film or TV adaptation of a game, lead with lore and backstory built early, and note that this is a self-reported creator view with no deal data.
- Do not present the Kickstarter animated-film approach as a proven funding path; it is a planned approach from one creator.
- If a brand deal is being structured for a game, consider a character-based integration (as in T17691) as an option alongside logo or event placements.
- Keep any brand character tied to the game's existing story and audience. Do not use it as a standalone ad that players would see as off-topic.
- Do not treat T17691 as proof of payment, duration, or approval terms. Look those up in the official brand program documentation.
