# Game Ideas And Genres

_which genres/ideas to make, what makes an idea good, genre saturation._

Evidence base: 428 extracted items from 234 videos by 1Coal, 1_Bruce, Alex Builds Games, AlvinBlox, BloxxedDev, ByteBlox, CaberJaber, CallMeDogey, Creators on the Blox, Cumin, Daiorg, Deconstructor of Fun, Duckable, Erthlord, GameMakers, GamesBeat, Generalist Programmer, GnomeCode, GosuGamers, ItsZyonic, JustDucksey, Last Level Studios, Lnor Roblox, LovJak, MonarchDev, MonzterDEV, MoseyTube, PineappleDev, Platform Aeronaut (transcript), Player.One, Possible Panda, Raul, RoWatcher (tracker blog), Roblox, Roblox Creator Docs, Roblox Creators, Roblox DevForum, Roblox public API, Roox4, RoyalDev, SmartyRBX, TheDankDeveloper, TheDevKing, Tizzy, VuukStudios, Yann, bubDEV, janisjanis01, kra_DeZ, mats-dept2, rasdev, waveshine (2018-07-24 to 2026-11-01). ## Overview
Creators converge on one thesis: don't invent a new genre. Take a proven loop or genre, change about 20% (up to ~40% for bigger reach) with a researched twist, and combine two or three inspirations. The idea must be explainable in one sentence (10-15 seconds) and built on a clear player fantasy for a mass-market, mostly young, mobile audience. Validate demand and competition on Roblox charts, search and other platforms before building. Beginners should start with simple, fast genres (obby, tycoon, simulator, incremental, meme/trend games) and avoid dream games and studio-dominated genres. Official Roblox sources say older-player genres (RPG, strategy, shooter, puzzle) are underserved and genre is not a ranking factor. Disagreement: trend-following (SmartyRBX, rasdev, Tizzy) versus building what you love or something original (Roblox official, Daiorg). Indie CCU and revenue claims are self-reported and unverified.

## Rules and findings
- **Don't invent a new genre; start from a proven loop** (multiple-creators): Several creators say to take a working formula and add your own twist. Totally original games are harder to blow up. Exact clones rarely blow up (rasdev: nine times out of ten). Counter-view: Daiorg and the older Roblox/DevForum advice favour originality. Sources: [rasdev 2026-05 0:51](https://youtu.be/q0lIbkUC2r8?t=51), [janisjanis01 2024-06 3:33](https://youtu.be/NWyK_PbFdxE?t=213), [rasdev 2026-07 0:00](https://youtu.be/fPT2GCkK1QM?t=0), [VuukStudios 2026-06 1:32](https://youtu.be/catPKgFIZbQ?t=92), [VuukStudios 2026-09 1:31](https://youtu.be/d5YHwCqwgYM?t=91). Newest source 2026-10-28.
- **Change at least ~20% of the reference game; change more for bigger reach** (multiple-creators): Tizzy and AlvinBlox use an 80/20 rule: keep 80-90% familiar and change 10-20%. Tizzy says 20% is enough for 5-10K CCU (self-reported). He says Ride a Pet changed ~40% vs Steal an Egg, aiming at 100K CCU. Changes must be researched, not random. Sources: [Tizzy 2026-09 14:21](https://youtu.be/CRCcsYEB_6A?t=861), [Tizzy 2026-09 16:22](https://youtu.be/CRCcsYEB_6A?t=982), [Tizzy 2026-06 17:04](https://youtu.be/4ms4cp2mrtM?t=1024), [Tizzy 2026-09 14:21](https://youtu.be/CRCcsYEB_6A?t=861), [Tizzy 2026-09 16:22](https://youtu.be/CRCcsYEB_6A?t=982). - **Combine two or three inspirations instead of cloning one** (multiple-creators): rasdev says 'steal like an artist': merge 2-3 sources, including non-game ones (movies, TikTok), into one easy-to-understand idea. Examples: tennis plus tower, fighting plus RPG, Rematch plus Mario Kart items, RNG roll plus tower defense, Dead Rails (history, trains, werewolves), Animal Hospital (mix of three hits). Roblox official also encourages genre mash-ups. Sources: [rasdev 2026-05 3:25](https://youtu.be/N3L63uyLyQ8?t=205), [rasdev 2026-05 4:34](https://youtu.be/N3L63uyLyQ8?t=274), [rasdev 2026-06 1:51](https://youtu.be/q0uePCbBlk0?t=111), [rasdev 2026-06 3:24](https://youtu.be/TqjyNV9La5c?t=204), [rasdev 2026-09 0:49](https://youtu.be/tErJHe9wQn0?t=49). - **Pass the one-sentence test** (multiple-creators): If a stranger or 12-year-old can't repeat the game after one sentence (about 10-15 seconds, understood from the thumbnail), simplify. AlvinBlox says players leave after 10 seconds. rasdev: if it takes 30 seconds, it won't win. 1Coal suggests one or two sentences. Steal An Egg's title explains the loop. - **Build around a clear core fantasy** (multiple-creators): Start with the role the player wants to inhabit or the feeling they want (biker, firefighter, knight, zookeeper, speed, power, chaos), not a bare mechanic. Frame the game as the accessible way to live a fantasy players can't have in real life. - **Design for mass appeal and young players** (multiple-creators): Roblox skews young and mobile. Pick simple, culturally neutral concepts that need no tutorial and work for ages 9-14. Avoid narrow niches and gender-targeted themes. Counterpoint: Roblox official says niche games can win because the algorithm finds the segment, and older docs say to pick one audience. Sources: [AlvinBlox 2025-06 5:48](https://youtu.be/Y93DRfB-Deo?t=348), [AlvinBlox 2025-06 5:06](https://youtu.be/Y93DRfB-Deo?t=306), [rasdev 2026-05 0:11](https://youtu.be/q0lIbkUC2r8?t=11), [rasdev 2026-09 2:43](https://youtu.be/Df5ebFGEOXY?t=163), [kra_DeZ 2026-09 6:01](https://youtu.be/cJ-YzHHhQs8?t=361). - **Pick one primary audience or player type** (multiple-creators): Official 2021-22 talks say not to appeal to all Bartle types. Choose a primary and secondary type and ask how each feature affects the primary one. Limit PvP areas if mixing explorers and killers. RPGs fit explorer elements, not PvP or stat-maxing. Trading suits achievers and socializers. Rotate content so no group feels abandoned. - **Validate demand and competition before building** (multiple-creators): Check the audience, whether the idea exists on Roblox or other platforms, whether it's proven or saturated, and whether it's easy to make content for. Use Roblox charts (look at what's growing, not what's huge), search, and competitor research. Don't assume an audience exists: Bitter Sweet failed. - **Expect saturation and clones; differentiate by mechanic, not just theme** (multiple-creators): Viral trends spawn clones fast. Grass/garden farming, simulators, brainrot, tower defense and one-dimensional collect-then-rebirth loops are crowded. Differentiate with a new mechanic or twist (boxing gloves instead of pets, ice fishing, a clicky-keyboard obby). Anime clones need mechanical differences. ## Checklist
- [ ] Write a one-sentence pitch and test it on a stranger or kid
- [ ] Define the core fantasy: role, actions, feeling
- [ ] Pick a proven reference loop and list its building blocks
- [ ] Decide which ~20-40% to change, with research
- [ ] Check Roblox charts, search and genre competition
- [ ] Check demand outside Roblox (TikTok, other platforms)
- [ ] Identify the primary target audience and Bartle type
- [ ] Pick one hook, not five features
- [ ] Fix the 30-second core loop before opening Studio
- [ ] Confirm the game is simple enough for young mobile players
- [ ] Scope a first game finishable in days or weeks
- [ ] Avoid saturated tutorial-clone niches without a twist
- [ ] Combine 2-3 inspirations, including non-Roblox ones
- [ ] Make sure the title and thumbnail explain the game
- [ ] Set the Creator Hub genre and subgenre by core loop
- [ ] Ask why past games in the genre failed
- [ ] Check whether your idea sits in RPG, strategy or shooter; if so, treat it as a demand-gap candidate but budget for higher polish.
- [ ] Write the one-room version of the idea before adding any second room, chapter or system.
- [ ] Decide at the start whether the game is public or friends-only; friends-only expects about 4 players versus about 10,000 typical reach.
- [ ] Attach an avatar, social or cross-platform element to a proven mechanic to form the mash-up.
- [ ] Choose one distinctive visual style choice that makes the game recognizably Roblox-styled yet unique.
- [ ] Re-check GamesBeat program claims against current official Roblox sources before building around them.
- [ ] For extraction/collection games, check that real trade-offs exist: vary item weight or fragility and confirm the highest-value item isn't always the obvious pick. (T17826)
- [ ] Before building a copy of a hit's mechanic, identify a second layer (endgame, differentiator) that a copycat can't trivially match. (T17980, T17985)
- [ ] Plan a behavior-changing endgame unlock at the design stage, not as a late add-on. (T17985)
- [ ] When making a sequel to a hit, add one clear differentiator, such as night-time stealing from other players' plots. (T17796)
- [ ] Check search results for your genre and candidate title before committing to a name; drop generic single words.
- [ ] Prototype an unproven or weird idea inside a 48-hour time box to test fun before scoping a full build.
- [ ] Before picking a remake, search the Roblox catalog for existing copies of the mobile ad game (T18592).
- [ ] Identify a mechanic from a top-grossing game that has proven itself, and copy the mechanic rather than the whole game (T18447).
- [ ] Scope the first build to about one week and a stud-style look (T18638).
- [ ] Check that Up and Coming games in the same style exist before committing to the style (T18638).
- [ ] Limit scripting to one small system such as a revive system (T18647).
- [ ] For an infinite-climb style concept, clone platforms, add a periodic reward such as a jump boost every second, and remove the base plate (T18644).
- [ ] When copying a trending format, add a clear twist so the result is not a direct clone (T18688, T18702).
- [ ] Before building around a current trend, confirm it is still rising. T18896's grow-a-garden-style example dates from 2025-07, so it may already be stale by late 2026.

## Where creators agree
- Do not directly clone; add a twist (rasdev, Tizzy, VuukStudios, kra_DeZ, RoyalDev, PineappleDev, Roblox official)
- Keep ideas simple and explainable in one sentence (AlvinBlox, rasdev, Lnor Roblox, 1Coal, Tizzy)
- Start beginners on simple genres (SmartyRBX, AlvinBlox, Tizzy, ByteBlox)
- Target mass/young audience (AlvinBlox, rasdev, kra_DeZ, VuukStudios)
- Validate market and competition (Tizzy, Yann, SmartyRBX, rasdev)
- Core fantasy matters (SmartyRBX, Tizzy, VuukStudios)
- Build on proven or popular formats and add a deliberate twist rather than inventing from scratch (T18447, T18688, T18702, Q1922; GameMakers, rasdev, janisjanis01).
- Simple, fast-to-build games can succeed (T18638, Q1911, Q1912; VuukStudios) and are consistent with the existing advice to start with simple, fast-to-build genres.

## Where creators disagree
- **Original vs proven idea**
  - rasdev, RoyalDev, VuukStudios, Tizzy: build on proven concepts
  - Daiorg: just make something original; trends and ambition matter less
  - Roblox DevForum 2021 and 1Coal 2023: be original/unique
  - Roblox Creator Docs: strange games can succeed, but iterating proven designs remains relevant
- **Passion vs market demand**
  - Roblox official, SmartyRBX 2023, waveshine: build what you love
  - SmartyRBX 2025, rasdev: build what players want, not what you think is cool
- **Niche vs broad audience**
  - AlvinBlox: avoid niche framing
  - Roblox Creators 2025: niche games can win via algorithm
  - Roblox 2018 and 2022: pick one audience
  - DevForum 2026: avoid overly narrow, no need for everyone
- **Horror**
  - MonzterDEV citing Roblox: high growth
  - kra_DeZ: kids beat it once and leave
  - rasdev: less competition, high bar
- **Uniqueness on the platform**
  - MonarchDev: platform does not want uniqueness, optimise for profit
  - Roblox Creator Docs/curation: seeks novel games
- **Pioneering new genres**
  - DevForum 2022: avoid genres with no community
  - Roblox 2018: first-mover with deep melee gets easy entry
- **Originality versus derivative success**
  - VuukStudios: many very simple games made in about a week have done very well, and a lot of people will play them (Q1911, Q1912).
  - rasdev: most hits are based on things that already blew up, roughly nine times out of ten (Q1922).
- **Whether to copy a front-page hit's structure and setting, or only its principles**
  - janisjanis01 (T18924, self-reported, low-advice): starts from a proven front-page hit such as 99 Nights in the Forest, remixes its theme and mechanics, and copied its structure and setting.
  - Existing knowledge-file rules: 'Reverse-engineer why top games work; copy principles, not assets' and 'Change at least ~20% of the reference game'. These imply changing the setting and structure, not copying them.

## Instructions for an agent using this file
- If a user asks for a game idea, propose a proven loop plus a ~20% twist and a one-sentence pitch.
- If the idea needs more than one sentence, simplify before proceeding.
- If the user is a beginner, steer to obby, tycoon, simulator, incremental or quiz scope finishable in weeks.
- If the genre looks saturated (garden, simulator, brainrot, tower defense), require a mechanic-level differentiator.
- If the user wants a clone, ask which loop elements change and push toward combining 2-3 inspirations.
- If validating, check Roblox charts for growth, search for competitors, and other platforms for proof.
- If the idea is niche, realistic FPS, plain RPG or scary horror, warn about audience size and quality bars.
- If asked about official guidance, cite RPG/strategy/shooter/puzzle underservice and that genre isn't a ranking factor.
- If the user's passion conflicts with demand, suggest checking demand and talking to target players.
- Flag all CCU and revenue claims as unverified self-reports.
- If a user proposes an RPG, strategy or shooter game, flag the underrepresentation signal (T17593), then check competition and quality bar before recommending it.
- If an idea needs multiple rooms or long progression, first recommend the one-room minimal version (T17693).
- If a user says the game is only for friends, state the expected reach (~4 players) and confirm whether reach is a goal.
- If a user wants a mash-up, require at least one avatar, social or cross-platform layer on top of the known mechanic (T17594).
- When discussing visuals, suggest one distinctive style choice (Q1779) without recommending licensed IP unless it fits.
- Treat GamesBeat and other indie-sourced signals as self-reported; weight official Roblox signals more heavily.
- Never present an underserved-genre signal as a guarantee of success.
- If a proposed idea's core mechanic already has many near-identical clones on the platform, don't build a copy; add a durable differentiator first. (T17980)
- If the design is an extraction or collection loop, add item trade-offs (weight, fragility) before adding more content. (T17826)
- If planning long-term retention, design endgame unlocks that change how play works, not just stats. (T17985)
- If making a sequel to a hit, change one clear thing rather than cloning the original. (T17796)
- If a candidate title is a generic single word in a saturated genre, require a more specific, searchable name before building.
- If an idea is unproven or unusual, run a 48-hour jam-style prototype to test fun before scoping the full game.
- If a top-grossing game's mechanic is the core of the idea, copy the mechanic and change the theme or add a twist.
- If a mobile ad game is the inspiration, search the Roblox catalog for existing remakes first and skip it if one is already widely copied.
- If time is limited to about a week, pick a simple stud-style concept with one reward loop.
- If scripting skill is low, cap the scope at one small system such as a revive system.
- Treat indie success claims (VuukStudios, rasdev, janisjanis01, GameMakers) as self-reported and not as proof of demand; validate demand separately.
- If a user wants to ride a trend, first verify it is still growing on current Roblox charts or Discover. Do not rely on 2025 examples as proof of current demand.
