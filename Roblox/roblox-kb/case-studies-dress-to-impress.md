# Case study: Dress to Impress

**Genre:** Social dress-up and voting-round game

Social dress-up game with themed rounds, a shared wardrobe, fashion voting and intermission prop sales. Chaotic lore and a secret room drove viral content and brought mainstream and non-Roblox streamers.

## Numbers
- No CCU, revenue or visit figures in the notes

## What creators say about it
- Unexpected lore and secret content (secret room, backstory) drive viral posts and content creation. ([Roblox 2024-09](https://youtu.be/LMUQpm0HpEQ))
- Themed voting rounds with a shared wardrobe generate strong approval and a monetisable audience. ([StudioKrew (agency blog, GGAID data) 2026-05](https://studiokrew.com/blog/top-roblox-games-may-2026/))
- Selling Theme Props during intermission lets players choose the next round's theme, tying monetization to a gameplay moment. ([Roblox Creator Docs 2026-10](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/contextual-purchases))
- Streamer outreach (Kaisen, Queso) brought new players and new accounts to the platform. ([Business of Creators 2026-08 26:33](https://youtu.be/lpIzMycpiKo?t=1593), [Deconstructor of Fun 2026-04 1:14](https://youtu.be/PNeLRjewQns?t=74))
- The social co-op dress-up genre was growing rapidly over summer 2024; users asked for more in the category. ([Roblox 2024-09 7:01](https://youtu.be/T7w3Q54JOPE?t=421))

## Transferable patterns
- Viral lore and hidden content to drive creator posts
- Themed rounds plus voting for approval and engagement
- Monetize at intermission moments
- Seed with non-Roblox streamers
