# Trend Research And Validation

_finding trending games early, tools like RoTrends, validating demand/competition before building._

Evidence base: 316 extracted items from 150 videos by #7Phantom, 1Coal, 1_Bruce, Alex Builds Games, AlvinBlox, Arow, Business of Creators, CaberJaber, CallMeDogey, Cumin, Daiorg, Deconstructor of Fun, Dexerto, Duckable, Emancyphur, Erthlord, GameMakers, GamesBeat, GosuGamers, Last Level Studios, Lnor Roblox, LovJak, Matt The Method, MonarchDev, MonzterDEV, MoseyTube, Naavik Gaming Podcast, PineappleDev, Player.One, Quez, Raul, RoWatcher (tracker blog), Roblox, Roblox Creator Docs, Roblox Creators, Roblox DevForum, Roblox Investor Relations, Roblox public API, RoyalDev, SimTek Game Development, SmartyRBX, StudioKrew (agency blog, GGAID data), ThatGuyEvan, TheDevBacon, TheDevKing, Tizzy, Ventures, VuukStudios, Xaiyne, bubDEV, janisjanis01, kra_DeZ, rasdev, tef (2018-07-24 to 2026-11-01). ## Overview
Creators teach that Roblox success starts with validating demand before building. Find games or trends that are rising but have not yet peaked, using Roblox charts (Up and Coming, Trending, 'breakout' tags), trackers (RoTrends, Rolimon's, GGAID, Rebloks, CreatorExchange) and off-platform signals (TikTok, YouTube, Steam wishlists, CrazyGames, mobile charts, Flash sites). Then check competition and saturation. Look for a gap or a weak competitor (bugs, no updates, poor monetisation). Ship a twist on a proven core loop fast, because trends die quickly. Avoid cloning giant front-page hits; prefer small-to-mid games with steady, growing player bases. Bring your own first players via ads or short-form content. Marketplace/UGC creators use Best Selling sorts, price filters, favorites and seasonal data. Indie CCU, visit and revenue figures are self-reported and unverified. Official sources cover platform mechanics.

## Rules and findings
- **Validate demand before building** (multiple-creators): Check that players already want the genre. Viral games come from proven ideas, not cool ideas that pop into someone's head. Look for quantifiable signals (ratings, votes, plays, creator videos) and spend a week or two stress-testing the idea (market need, flaws, current demand) before building. - **Catch rising games early, before the peak** (multiple-creators): Watch CCU trajectory, 90-day CCU charts, creation dates, and 'breakout' tags. Target games trending up just before a peak. Tizzy claims calls at about 50 and 200 CCU that later grew (self-reported). bubDEV caught Steal a Brainrot under 5K CCU. Madsen's game climbed fast. Look for high CCU relative to visits and recent creation. - **Use Roblox charts and the front page, but study growing games, not giants** (multiple-creators): Check Up and Coming, Top Earning, Trending and discover pages. Studying games in the growth phase leaves room for another entrant. A smaller breakout game (about 60k players) can matter more than a large stable one. - **Avoid copying big front-page hits** (multiple-creators): Targets that are performing well but are not the biggest, with steady visits and returning players, are better. Tizzy says front-page clones tend to die within a month. Clones of Don't Wake the Brain Rots fell to roughly 2-31 CCU. - **Speed matters when chasing a trend, and late entry fails** (multiple-creators): Trends pass quickly. Build fast and enter while the trend is new. A good idea on a dying trend (tsunami escape) flops. Same-week leaf clones got 366K and 180K visits against 130M, so being early with a clone alone gave no advantage; execution and the original's momentum mattered. - **Add a twist; don't clone exactly** (multiple-creators): Copy the core loop but change one element (theme, genre, mechanic). - **Check competition and saturation** (multiple-creators): Search the genre or fantasy keyword on Roblox and Creator Exchange sorted by visits. One or two games means the niche is open. A dominant single game, or only roleplay games for a high-interest fantasy, is a gap. Check whether existing games deliver the fantasy. - **Confirm tracker breakouts with velocity before acting** (single-mention): A high breakout score alone is weak evidence. Compare a candidate's growth against these baselines so you don't mistake a rising tide for a real trend. - **Track revenue per game, not just visits** (single-mention): Use creator-exchange-style revenue charts to see which games earn money, not only which are popular. One indie creator estimates ~20-40% of Roblox players play combat games, and a competitor reportedly has ~100M visits. Treat these self-reported figures as rough. Pair the genre share with who the games actually target. - **Watch mechanic decay, not only title decay** (single-mention): A fading hit does not mean the core loop is dead. One creator saw Steal a Brain Rot and Grow a Garden decline significantly while Steal an Egg rose, calling it the same mechanic in a different skin. Track the mechanic across titles to find the next skin to build, rather than abandoning the mechanic when one title drops. Sources: [Deconstructor of Fun 2026-10 10:15](https://youtu.be/NrPjXO_7oKk?t=615). Newest source 2026-10-29.
- **Read ad trends over 90-180 days, not one snapshot** (one-creator-repeated): Track a genre's ad intelligence over 90-180 days to see when a trend started, peaked and faded. A single snapshot cannot show direction, so a genre that looks hot today may already be fading. Sources: [GameMakers 2022-03 21:12](https://youtu.be/t-jpyaQffYE?t=1272). Newest source 2022-03-01.
- **Start ad research with the free ad libraries** (one-creator-repeated): Facebook Ads Library and TikTok Ads Library are free starting points. They show current ads but not spend or intensity, so use impressions per ad as a rough quality proxy where available. Sources: [GameMakers 2022-03 22:50](https://youtu.be/t-jpyaQffYE?t=1370). Newest source 2022-03-01.
- **Track about 10 direct competitors and study their top-impression ads** (one-creator-repeated): Keep roughly 10 direct competitors in a dashboard and pull their top-impression ads. Ask what they do that works, then adapt it within your own asset constraints rather than copying it. Sources: [GameMakers 2022-03 27:42](https://youtu.be/t-jpyaQffYE?t=1662), [GameMakers 2022-03 27:42](https://youtu.be/t-jpyaQffYE?t=1662). Newest source 2022-03-01.
- **Save competitor and non-genre ads on your own device** (one-creator-repeated): Save competitor and adjacent-genre ads regularly. Ad networks serve more ads to users who actively click and install, so a heavily used feed skews what you see. Keep a clean, separate browsing setup for research. Sources: [GameMakers 2022-03 24:53](https://youtu.be/t-jpyaQffYE?t=1493). Newest source 2022-03-01.
- **Answer three questions before building** (single-mention): Before building, answer: what is the market void, what do players want that no game delivers, and what is the objective of the update (live-ops, new content, or retention hook). If you cannot answer all three, do not start the build. Sources: [GameMakers 2024-03 2:11](https://youtu.be/zvNQHk1hUYc?t=131). Newest source 2024-03-08.
- **Treat a genre with 2-3 dominant titles as a hard entry** (multiple-creators): A crowded niche dominated by two or three titles is a hard market to enter. Do not compete head-on. Enter only with a clear twist, a neglected sub-niche, or a demand gap the leaders do not serve. Sources: [GameMakers 2022-11 22:18](https://youtu.be/NaR3-VIg5-I?t=1338), [TheDevKing 2020-05 10:17](https://youtu.be/sBOAlER6HVU?t=617). Newest source 2022-11-24.
- **Read falling ad CTR as a saturation signal** (single-mention): One creator found tycoon interest had declined and click-through rate on tycoon ads was low. Treat falling or low CTR for a genre's ads as a warning and check genre saturation before investing. Sources: [TheDevKing 2020-05 10:17](https://youtu.be/sBOAlER6HVU?t=617). Newest source 2020-05-27.
- **Borrow proven YouTube thumbnail formats for icons** (single-mention): Adapt popular YouTube thumbnail formats, such as before/after transformations, to Roblox game icons. The speaker describes this as an emerging trend, so test it against your own icon variants rather than assuming it works. Sources: [rasdev 2026-07 4:05](https://youtu.be/9_cHgdOo7i0?t=245). Newest source 2026-07-14.
- **Time-box a throwaway build for a short-lived trend** (single-mention): For a hot but short-lived trend (e.g., a plugin-generated simulator meme), a fast build can capture attention while it lasts. janisjanis01 made a game in about 12 hours around one. Keep the scope tiny, and don't expect the trend to last. Sources: [janisjanis01 2024-10 2:50](https://youtu.be/ZyQ73kfh_b4?t=170). Newest source 2024-10-27.
- **Judge ad creatives against a ~3% CTR target** (single-mention): janisjanis01 found ads at ~2% click-through were not enough and aimed for ~3%. Track CTR per ad creative and treat anything near 2% as underperforming. This complements the existing falling-CTR saturation signal. Sources: [janisjanis01 2025-06 13:58](https://youtu.be/W9JYImC0FBg?t=838). Newest source 2025-06-15.

## Checklist
- [ ] Browse Roblox Up and Coming, Trending, Top Earning and breakout charts
- [ ] Open RoTrends and sort by momentum or visits
- [ ] Check 90-day CCU chart and creation date
- [ ] Log candidates in a spreadsheet and recheck in 1-2 weeks
- [ ] Search the genre keyword on Roblox and Creator Exchange sorted by visits
- [ ] Count dominant games and note their genres
- [ ] Check like ratio of competitors
- [ ] Check update cadence and bug complaints
- [ ] Check off-platform demand: Steam wishlists, CrazyGames, YouTube
- [ ] Scroll TikTok, Shorts and Reels for trends and verify their cause
- [ ] Check Google Trends and search autocomplete
- [ ] Check App Store and Google Play charts
- [ ] Pick a differentiating twist and check it hasn't been tried
- [ ] Define target demographic
- [ ] Compute an opportunity score (demand, competition, momentum, replicability)
- [ ] Stress-test the idea for 1-2 weeks
- [ ] Plan a launch audience (ads, TikTok, friends)
- [ ] Budget a small ad test (10k-30k Robux)
- [ ] Compare with similar-player experiences in Analytics
- [ ] For UGC: Marketplace Best Selling with a price filter
- [ ] Check favorites as a demand signal on UGC
- [ ] Check seasonal sales data for UGC
- [ ] Before buying a game, compare peak and average CCU
- [ ] Before buying a template, search for duplicates
- [ ] Rerun research on other platforms if no good trend appears
- [ ] Compare any candidate game's growth to platform-wide growth (visits up 55% YoY, daily users up 10% YoY, per Roblox DevForum, Sept 2026) before calling it a trend (N7526, N7527).
- [ ] Check how many of the top-chart slots in your genre went to games launched in the last 90 days; new games held 28% of the top 100 by hours and 33% of the top 1000 in August 2026 (N7529).
- [ ] Do not treat a single tracker breakout score as validation; check velocity or repeat appearances first (N7559).
- [ ] Record the date of any platform benchmark you use, since the 2026-09 figures will age quickly.
- [ ] Before choosing a genre, look up its approximate player share and visit totals on competitors, then verify with your own data.
- [ ] When a popular title declines, check whether the underlying mechanic is rising in other titles before writing it off.
- [ ] Pull per-game revenue from a creator-exchange-style chart where available, and verify it yourself before using it in a decision.
- [ ] Before building, write down the market void, the unmet player want, and the update objective (live-ops, new content, or retention hook).
- [ ] Count dominant titles in the target genre. If two or three dominate, plan a clear twist or niche before committing.
- [ ] Pull top-impression ads for about 10 direct competitors and note what each does that works.
- [ ] Check Facebook Ads Library and TikTok Ads Library for the genre as a free first pass.
- [ ] Review any genre trend across 90-180 days before acting on it.
- [ ] Save competitor and adjacent-genre ads regularly on a clean device.
- [ ] Check CTR trends for genre ads; falling CTR suggests saturation.
- [ ] Shortlist candidate genres and mechanics from the Reblocks discover page alongside the front page and charts.
- [ ] Test a before/after style icon adapted from YouTube thumbnail formats against your current icon.
- [ ] Pick a reference game with a known player count (e.g., a 44,000-player tycoon) to benchmark expected reach.
- [ ] Set a hard time box (e.g., hours, not weeks) for any build aimed at a short-lived trend.
- [ ] Log CTR for each ad creative separately and compare it against a ~3% target; flag creatives near 2% for replacement.

## Where creators agree
- Speed and early entry matter (janisjanis01, bubDEV, Erthlord, rasdev, VuukStudios, SmartyRBX).
- Add your own twist (SmartyRBX, VuukStudios, rasdev, Duckable, Roblox DevForum, Last Level Studios).
- Study trending or proven games (TheDevKing, SmartyRBX, Tizzy, rasdev, MoseyTube, 1Coal).
- Look off-platform for proven ideas (1Coal, Tizzy, rasdev, RoyalDev).
- Bring your own first players (rasdev, TheDevKing, AlvinBlox).
- Avoid cloning the biggest hits (Tizzy, MonarchDev, rasdev, MoseyTube).
- GameMakers (T18470) and TheDevKing (T18420) agree that crowded or declining genres deserve caution before investment.
- Validating demand before building is supported by GameMakers (T18499), consistent with the existing 'Validate demand before building' rule.

## Where creators disagree
- **Copy vs. original**
  - Roblox DevForum and SmartyRBX: take inspiration, don't copy.
  - janisjanis01: 'Good artists copy, great artists steal' and copy viral Shorts; Arow copies items; rasdev: copy the core loop and change one element.
  - VuukStudios: speed over originality.
- **Enter saturated or top-heavy genres?**
  - Business of Creators: yes, if you find shortcomings to beat.
  - Tizzy and MonarchDev: avoid cloning top hits.
- **Follow mega-trends or the small/steady games**
  - Quez, Arow, bubDEV: ride huge trends.
  - Tizzy: huge front-page games die in a month; choose smaller steady games.
  - 1_Bruce: some genres are dying while others resurge.
- **Being first**
  - bubDEV and rasdev: early entry matters.
  - MonarchDev: same-week clones that got there first gained no advantage; quality and momentum mattered, though the better-rated game (97%) had few players.
- **Whether saturated genres are worth entering**
  - Existing rule 'Saturated genres can still work' (prior source) says saturation alone is not disqualifying.
  - GameMakers (T18470, 2022-11) says a niche with two or three dominant titles is a hard market to enter.
  - TheDevKing (T18420, 2020-05) found tycoon interest declined and CTR low, so would avoid investing.
- **Copying popular front-page hits**
  - Existing rule 'Avoid copying big front-page hits' warns against cloning titles.
  - rasdev (T18687, 2026-07, most recent) says pick a genre or mechanic already trending on the front page.
  - Reconciliation: rasdev's advice applies at genre or mechanic level; the existing rule still applies to cloning titles.

## Instructions for an agent using this file
- If asked for a game idea, first check Up and Coming/Trending/breakout charts and a tracker, then name 3 candidates with CCU trajectory.
- If a candidate is already at peak or on the front page for weeks, don't clone it; look for a smaller growing game.
- If a keyword search shows 0-2 games, treat the niche as open but verify off-platform demand.
- If only one dominant game exists in a high-interest fantasy, recommend a twist on it.
- If a trend's origin is TikTok or a meme, verify it is still rising and ship fast, or skip.
- If the competitor has low like ratio, many bug complaints, stale updates or weak monetisation, recommend a better version.
- If proposing an idea, state your differentiator and confirm the twist isn't already tried.
- If recommending numbers, label indie CCU, visit and revenue claims as unverified and prefer official stats for mechanics.
- If the user wants a launch plan, include an initial traffic source (ads or short-form content) since no organic lift exists at zero visits.
- If the user is buying a game or template, check peak vs average CCU and duplicates.
- If a tracker shows a high breakout score but flat velocity metrics, treat the trend as unconfirmed and keep validating (N7559).
- If a game's growth roughly matches platform-wide growth (visits up 55% YoY, daily users up 10% YoY), do not conclude it is a breakout; look for game-specific signals (N7526, N7527).
- If new games already hold a large share of the charts in your target genre, treat the chart as open to newcomers, but still validate demand first (N7529).
- Always cite the date of platform benchmarks used in a decision.
- If a tracker shows a title declining but its mechanic appearing in new titles, research the new titles rather than dropping the mechanic.
- If a revenue figure comes from a single indie source, flag it as unverified and recommend checking it directly.
- If a genre-share figure is used for a decision, label it as self-reported and check it against platform or tracker data.
- If a genre has two or three dominant titles, do not enter head-on; look for a twist, sub-niche, or unmet player want first.
- If a genre's ad trend has been observed for less than 90 days, collect more data before acting.
- Treat impressions per ad as a quality proxy, not a direct demand measure.
- If you cannot state the market void, unmet want, and update objective, stop before building.
- If genre ad CTR is low or falling, lower the priority of that genre and check saturation.
- When choosing a genre or mechanic, start from front page, charts or Reblocks discover page, but avoid cloning a specific hit title.
- Do not apply mobile UA market figures to Roblox decisions without Roblox-specific data.
- Treat host-claimed figures (e.g., Liftoff reach) as unverified; do not use them as benchmarks.
- Use a reference game's player count only as a rough benchmark, not as a forecast for your own game.
- Save ads from a clean browsing setup so the research reflects competitor creatives, not your own ad-feed bias.
- If a trend is clearly short-lived (meme- or plugin-driven), propose a time-boxed throwaway build instead of a full production effort.
- If ad CTR for a creative is near 2%, test a replacement creative aiming for ~3% before scaling spend.
- Treat single-creator figures such as the 8M CCU peak as outlier context, not as expected outcomes for a new game.
