# Case study: Rebirth Speed Run

**Genre:** Speedrun / rebirth simulator

PolarBear's beta rebirth speedrun game, used as a paid-ads test. Drew bursts of players but earned little Robux and had low pass sales.

Also referred to as: Rebirth Speedrun

## Numbers
- Sponsored campaign of ~5,000 Robux returned ~535 Robux (PolarBear, self-reported, G739)
- ~5,000 Robux campaign gave 140k impressions but only 376 plays (PolarBear, self-reported, G743)

## What creators say about it
- Paid traffic did not cover cost for an unpolished beta: ~5,000 Robux spend returned ~535 Robux. ([PolarBear 2024-03 0:00](https://youtu.be/bwMUqu-wWpw?t=0))
- Sponsored ads underperformed: 140k impressions produced 376 plays. Impressions are not a substitute for retention. ([PolarBear 2024-04 0:00](https://youtu.be/Hh31hjo3J6U?t=0))
- Drawing many players at one point did not translate into Robux; pass sales were low. Monetisation needs to exist before traffic arrives. ([PolarBear 2024-07 7:22](https://youtu.be/yYp23GK9RI4?t=442))
- The sponsored destination was the speaker's own game; polish before buying traffic (inference from the sponsorship results). ([PolarBear 2024-03 5:06](https://youtu.be/sk8HQDgJyE0?t=306))

## Transferable patterns
- Polish the experience before spending on paid traffic.
- Track impressions-to-plays and cost per play, not just reach.
- Have passes or shop monetisation in place before buying ads.
- Compare Robux spend against return on every campaign.
