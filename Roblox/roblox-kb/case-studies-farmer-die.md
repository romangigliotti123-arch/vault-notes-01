# Case study: Farmer Die

**Genre:** Survival (co-op), inferred from Alien Ranch-style loop

Creator Tizzy's survival game built on Alien Ranch's core loop, adding guns, curable NPC survivors and a class system. Creator describes steady CCU but weak D1 retention and monetisation.

## Numbers
- 3.7M visits and 1.8K peak CCU; unprofitable on $12K spend with two full-time hires (Tizzy, self-reported, G1908)
- Steady 80-150 CCU for months (Tizzy, self-reported, G1653)

## What creators say about it
- Cloning a proven core loop (Alien Ranch) and layering distinct systems (guns, classes, curable NPC survivors) sustained a steady 80-150 CCU for months. ([Tizzy 2026-05 0:00](https://youtu.be/BNdHkeKRUdE?t=0))
- Strong co-play engagement did not translate into D1 retention or monetisation. Creator calls the game barely alive. Track engagement and monetisation as separate metrics. ([Tizzy 2026-05](https://youtu.be/inWzxY26NXo))
- Audience scale is not profit: 3.7M visits and 1.8K peak CCU still lost money on $12K spend with two full-time hires. Check unit economics before hiring. ([Tizzy 2026-07](https://youtu.be/LZOohZHsJus))
- Frame the core mechanic (curing survivors) as a reason to care, not just as instructions. ([Tizzy 2026-07](https://youtu.be/FnsIL4zPCQg))

## Transferable patterns
- Clone-plus-differentiator: take a proven loop and add one or two distinct systems.
- Diagnose engagement, D1 retention and monetisation separately; strong co-play can hide weak retention.
- Model spend against revenue before adding headcount.
- Give mechanics stakes and meaning so players care, not just understand.
