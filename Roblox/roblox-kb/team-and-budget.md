# Team And Budget

_hiring, outsourcing, revenue share, contracts, budgets, working solo._

## Overview
Team and budget advice centres on hiring only when necessary, starting small, and matching structure to scope. Core guidance: keep design and core scripting in-house, outsource repetitive or specialist work under written contracts and paid trials, pay on tracked time, and keep a large budget buffer. The 2026 sources push back on a strict "never solo" stance: a solo build can be cheaper and faster for a small scope, while a large scope favours a team. Offering artists a revenue share instead of upfront pay is newly suggested (Lnor Roblox) and conflicts with the older upfront-pay rule. Community resources (map-makers, events staff) and tooling (Trello role labels) add scalable structure. Several figures and claims come from 2019-2021 or low-advice 2024 Fiverr data and should be treated cautiously.

## Rules and findings
- **Cover only your weakest skill with outside help first** (multiple-creators): Identify the one thing you are weak at (e.g. UI) and pay someone specifically for it instead of forcing yourself to be good at everything or hiring a team/co-founder. MoseyTube paid about $50 to a Discord-sourced freelancer for UI polish. SmartyRBX likewise says you need not master every skill; commission or collaborate. Newest source 2026-08-07.
- **Start hiring small to learn how to hire** (multiple-creators): Make a small first hire (e.g. a GFX artist for an icon) to learn delegating and communicating requirements before commissioning bigger work. Commission art like thumbnails early enough to finish before an update. Sources: [SmartyRBX 2025-09 2:13](https://youtu.be/ysqeKxPqBXA?t=133), [Roblox 2025-09 32:51](https://youtu.be/cUxgt4asjFU?t=1971). Newest source 2025-09-26.
- **Once you have capital, outsource non-core work and keep design/core scripting** (one-creator-repeated): With revenue, hire specialists (builder, modeler, UI, VFX, sound, animation) and spend your time on game design, core systems and project management. SmartyRBX example: 1,000,000 Robux starting capital; a map costs roughly 20k-40k Robux (self-reported). He cites earning 10M Robux from simple games before complex passion projects. - **Keep creative direction in-house when outsourcing** (single-mention): Outsource gaps like scripting or UI to a studio partner but keep creative control, to maintain release cadence (Team I2R with Tencel Studios). Hire for complementary skills, define roles in writing, and use a project manager. Solo devs can reach out to respected developers with thoughtful messages. - **Do not develop completely alone** (multiple-creators): Official advice: share your vision and assemble strong people; solo development is long and lonely. One creator spent ~3 years without a coder. Joining a team is also a valid route to release even if you are not the strongest developer. - **Keep teams lean and passionate** (multiple-creators): Typical lean team is 2-4 developers (Tizzy prefers T-shaped hires). A 4-7 person team (artist, engineer, designer, producer) built a premium multiplayer world in 4-6 months. Start with a passionate day-one team, add contractors for specific jobs (e.g. event QA), and avoid scaling for its own sake. Regretevator runs weekly updates with 3-4 people. Roblox's engine and player base mean you don't need a huge team. Sources: [Tizzy 2026-05 4:05](https://youtu.be/7tYPvXYlif8?t=245), [Roblox Creators 2023-08 8:44](https://youtu.be/kNE757v3Bwg?t=524), [Roblox 2023-09 26:44](https://youtu.be/S8ORVEKec_4?t=1604), [Roblox 2023-09 26:44](https://youtu.be/S8ORVEKec_4?t=1604), [Roblox 2025-09 28:16](https://youtu.be/YgyfcMfxQcI?t=1696). - **Scale headcount only after success, adding layers and specialists** (multiple-creators): As games grow, add specialists (animator, character designer, VFX, artists), producers and PMs; one designer/dev/artist setup stopped scaling for a large game. Split art among specialists (15+ person city team). Examples: Grow a Garden ~30 engineers up from 10; Voldex ~20 per game post-acquisition; Zeal 3 to 70+ over ~2 years; The Gang 60+; Toya 5 to 21 (target 40); WonderWorks 4 to 17; Livetopia 230. All studio-scale figures are as stated by speakers. A team grew from 2 to 11-12 (core plus rotating contractors). Don't pull the team from a live game with active players unless it shows clear long-term decline. - [ ] Pay on time as promised

- [ ] Run worst-case analysis on contract clauses
- [ ] Talk to previous creators of an acquirer
- [ ] Be wary of brand minimum guarantees

## Where creators agree
- SmartyRBX, MoseyTube, Tizzy and Creators on the Blox guests agree outsourcing weak or non-core skills is smart.
- SmartyRBX, Tizzy and Roblox Creators officials agree on written agreements and clear pay terms.
- Tizzy and SmartyRBX agree on firing/replacing slow contractors quickly.
- Roblox officials and indie creators agree small teams suffice and scale only after success.
- Roblox officials, AlvinBlox and Creators on the Blox agree on portfolio-based hiring.
- Keep teams small and lean: Deconstructor of Fun (2026) and Roblox official docs (2026, low-advice) both say a small team can ship big games; the platform covers hosting and storage.
- Hire people with shipped work and a portfolio: DevForum (2022) and Deconstructor of Fun's shipped-game focus align.
- Sign a formal agreement before paying developers: DevForum (2020) and the existing written-contract rule agree.
- Keep the team small and vision-led: GameMakers (2021) and Deconstructor of Fun (2026) both favor a small connected team over headcount.
- Hire specialists for the work you are weakest at or that blocks you (T6228, T5824, T6032, T6421), per RoBuilder and janisjanis01.
- Keep the team small and skilled rather than large (T4985, N2228, T6035).
- Critique the work, not the person, and give realistic deadlines (T4960, T4961, T4959).
- Official Roblox docs and indie reports both advise that collaborators should follow shared standards and avoid conflicting edits (T5247, T8044, T8324).
- Commissions (scripting, building, UI, VFX) are a legitimate early income source while building your own game, but should be planned to move on from.
- Build a portfolio before pitching commissions or hiring.
- Partner only with people you know or can verify, and put agreements in writing before work starts.
- Percentage or royalty deals are risky without a track record and an upfront payment.
- Age minimum is 18+ for applicants and submitters, with under-18 team members allowed if compliant with law.
- Plan for dropouts and deadline slips when several people work in parallel.
- Verify identity and track record rather than trusting badges or claims.
- Team size should match project scope: solo can work for small projects, while large projects need a team (studio rook, RoyalDev).
- Large teams carry cost and coordination risk, so keep teams lean (RoyalDev; consistent with existing 'keep teams lean' rule).

## Where creators disagree
- **Friends/trust vs money as motivator**
  - Yeucc (Creators on the Blox), Clemens (RobloxOfficial) and Roblox 2025 talk favor trusted friends and avoiding corporate contracts
  - Zeal (Creators on the Blox) says money is the main incentive and pay well
  - Tizzy and SmartyRBX stress contracts and accountability, even punishing devs
- **Upfront payment**
  - Tizzy prefers pay on completion, 50% upfront fallback
  - SmartyRBX pays weekly
  - Roblox Creators 2023 says state pay upfront (about transparency of terms, not deposit)
- **Team size at scale**
  - Tizzy and Roblox say stay lean (2-4)
  - Zeal (70+), The Gang (60+), Livetopia (230) and 2023 official talks say layers of staff were needed to scale
- **Internal vs studio team**
  - Tizzy hires an internal live ops team over a studio
  - MoseyTube and Team I2R use cheap freelancers or a studio partner for gaps
  - Deconstructor of Fun suggests a publishing partner for scale
- **Ideal team size**
  - GameMakers (2021): small connected team of 6 full-time + ~22 contractors on Vehicle Simulator, and says chemistry beats headcount.
  - Deconstructor of Fun (2026): never more than ~4 per project, ~3 projects at once; ideal studio 10-20.
- **When to hire**
  - Existing rule 'Only hire when absolutely necessary' favours staying solo or lean as long as possible.
  - janisjanis01 (T5824, T6032) says to hire builders or scripters for a big game and team up with specialists when stuck with no traction.
  - RoBuilder (T6228) says to identify bottleneck roles early and hire for them rather than waiting on one person.
- **Whether percentage or royalty splits should be used for team members**
  - Roblox DevForum (T10723, Q1159, 2020-07): avoid royalty for hires; it does not scale; reserve for 2-3 owners on a passion project.
  - VuukStudios (T12237, T12497, 2024): acceptable if the partner has a track record and pays upfront under contract.
  - Generalist Programmer (T13546, 2026-07): recurring percentage payouts are a built-in tool, with rules on totals and owner remainder.
- **Whether a verified status should matter in choosing a partner**
  - Raul (T13778, 2026-09): verify history and identity before committing; a verified claim was false.
  - Raul (Q1429, 2026-09): it should not matter whether someone is verified or not; judge by track record instead.
- **Where to find commission work**
  - Arow (T12789, 2025-05): use developer Discord servers, not Roblox's built-in commission page.
  - VuukStudios (T13192, T13191, 2026-02): also use Roblox Studio Community and a free marketplace listing alongside Discord.
- **Whether to develop solo**
  - Existing rule: 'Do not develop completely alone'.
  - studio rook (T16583, 2026-08): solo was a major speed disadvantage for large scope; consider a team.
  - RoyalDev (T16619, 2026-10): solo devs can be more cost-efficient and sometimes faster; a solo build is a valid path. Big teams can be disastrous.
- **Paying artists with a revenue share instead of upfront pay**
  - Existing rules: 'Avoid royalty splits for hires'; 'Percentage and revenue-share deals are hard early'; 'Take percentage deals only with a proven partner, upfront pay, and a signed contract'.
  - Lnor Roblox (T16527, Q1593, 2026-07): offer artists a revenue percentage instead of upfront pay when budget is tight; a modeler accepted. Most recent advice, but it conflicts with the upfront-pay condition.
- **Team Create group-role editing**
  - Docs (as cited by Emancyphur, N6194): group roles with Create and Edit Group Games can edit.
  - Emancyphur (N6194, 2021-07): owner-only since at least May 2021, per speaker.
  - Existing rule 'Grant Edit by friendship or group role' follows the docs. Unresolved; verify current behaviour.

## Instructions for an agent using this file
- If the user lacks a skill (UI, modeling, scripting), then recommend outsourcing only that piece, starting with a small paid task.
- If the user wants to hire, then require portfolio, referrals or mutuals, plus a paid sample before the main contract.
- If drafting a hiring post, then include role, pay in Robux/USD, timeline and scope.
- If the user pays a contractor, then recommend a written statement of work, pay on completion or weekly, and at most 50% upfront.
- If a contractor misses deadlines, then give one warning and replace them.
- If the user is solo with no capital, then suggest commissions or partnering with a complementary builder/scripter, noting commissions are low leverage.
- If the user considers a brand deal, then warn about minimum guarantees and require a dedicated brand owner.
- If the user considers selling or partnering, then advise own lawyer, own valuation, worst-case clause analysis and due diligence.
- If the game is gaining traction and updates are stressful, then add specialists and a producer/PM rather than crunching.
- Always label indie cost and headcount figures as self-reported and unverified.
- If the budget does not cover every planned hire's quote, do not start; cut scope or delay hiring first (T3460).
- If a project needs more than about 4 people, challenge the scope before adding headcount (T3786).
- If a candidate has not shipped a finished game, treat them as a test hire on a paid sample task, not a core contractor (T3463).
- If you cannot pay someone on a steady schedule, do not hire them; reduce scope instead (Q759).
- If you are considering debt to fund a hire, do not borrow; use only affordable spending (T3467).
- If a team member's work is not reviewed against scope at checkpoints, add inspection before assuming delivery (Q695).
- Before selling, confirm government ID or age verification; a phone number does not count (T4554).
- Before transferring group ownership, remove group-owned commerce items; the transfer is blocked otherwise (T4313).
- Before charging for paid access, confirm the account is at least 30 days old and ID-verified (N2082, N1902).
- When a teammate needs to see a change, point them to Studio activity history and annotations, not screenshots (T3616).
- If a task is a specialist skill (animation, modeling, scripting, music), find a paid candidate for it instead of doing it solo, and verify skill before assigning.
- If a bottleneck role blocks progress for more than one development cycle, hire for it rather than waiting on one person.
- If outsourcing repetitive configuration work, require a recorded process before accepting delivery.
- If setting a deadline, add buffer for contractor delays and never set one shorter than the estimated work.
- If giving feedback, address the work and the fix, not the person; replace a contractor only when quality is clearly below standard.
- If adding a Team Create collaborator, first confirm the age check is complete and that Edit is allowed (friend or group role); otherwise grant Play only.
- If running a multi-developer game, move it into a group, set the group as package creator, and model profit splits before launch.
- If two people might edit the same place, use previousDraftHash on publishes and avoid simultaneous edits from multiple devices.
- If applying to the Incubator, check that the team is experienced and can commit to significant development time; do not present a solo beginner as the target.
- If citing budget figures, label them as self-reported unless they come from official documentation.
- If a contractor is paid by hour, track time and pay on a fixed monthly date; do not pay on royalty or per-assignment estimates.
- If a candidate asks for a percentage deal, check the owner's track record (e.g. 2-3 front-page games) and require an upfront payment and a signed contract first.
- If the group is used for payouts, add collaborators at least 14 days before the first payout and keep percentages at or below 100%.
- If a collaborator is listed as verified, still check history and identity; do not rely on the badge.
- If a commission offer is very low (e.g. 5K Robux for a full game), decline and wait for better posts.
- If preparing a team transfer, verify email, move private assets, and set package ownership to the group before submitting.
- If the submitter is a team or group, confirm they are over 18.
- If recruiting several developers in parallel, plan for at least one dropout.
- If a QA program needs testers, decide structure first, then advertise publicly and scale pay by revenue and bug volume.
- If the team is visibly overloaded, hire for the specific weak area rather than generically.
- If the project scope is small and the developer is solo, a solo build is a valid path; if the scope is large, plan team hires before progress stalls.
- If an artist asks for a revenue share instead of upfront pay, require a signed contract and modelled split math first, and flag that this conflicts with the older upfront-pay rule.
- If a cheap commission (about $50-$120) is proposed, treat the delivery as a playable prototype and budget separately for further development.
- If asked about Team Create group-role editing, verify current official docs and Studio behaviour; do not rely on N6194.
- If asked about DataStore limits, verify current official documentation; do not rely on Q1480.
- If choosing leaders, select by personality, maturity and trustworthiness and publish a promotion path.
- If the community is large and logistics are eating dev time, hire an events or community person.
- If a team shows mixed experience and repeated rework, rebalance roles or tighten vetting before scaling.
- If community-made maps or content are proposed, require closed testing before inclusion.
- Treat [low-advice] items (T16126, N6345-N6348) as lower-confidence and cross-check before recommending.
- If a user asks for a rough dev budget for a simple obby or single-loop sim, cite the $5,000-$15,000 agency band (N7232) and state it is unverified.
- If the scope includes a full economy, game passes, saves, or an update pipeline, use the $25,000-$75,000 band (N7233) as the reference.
- If the scope is large multiplayer with anti-cheat or LiveOps, state the ~$100,000 floor (N7234) and flag that it is an agency estimate.
- If a user wants to keep an original creator in the revenue split, prompt them to define the update-idea role and percentage in writing before launch.
- Do not present agency cost bands as market averages; label them as indicative and self-reported.
- If a co-op or multiplayer feature has no available testers, a bot or second test account can provide the second player for early checks.
- If testing with a bot, do not treat its results as proof of real player behavior. Schedule human playtests before release.
- If the user is hiring for a first role, first check whether the role can be covered by the owner or outsourced for a trial period.
- If a referral is proposed, still route the candidate through the standard interview pipeline rather than skipping steps.
- If a candidate is from a large studio, verify specific contributions and use back-channel references before offering.
- If runway is short, propose a fractional or one-month paid trial instead of a full-time commitment.
- If a hire is proposed, plan at least two weeks of onboarding before judging fit.
- If the user is working solo, advise disabling Team Create until a collaborator joins.
- Treat speaker-reported churn and process figures as anecdotal, not benchmarks.
- If a user asks about commission pricing, cite N7991 and N7992 as self-reported speaker estimates, not market data.
- If a user asks about outside investment, cite N7866 as a 2020 example and warn that current terms may differ.
- If a user asks how to get paid for services in USD, point to T18587 and recommend confirming each processor's fees and availability in the user's country.
- Do not treat the Robux-to-USD conversion as fixed; flag it as approximate.
- If a project has not yet passed soft-launch validation, do not hire a full marketing team; hire at most one internal marketer or use a trusted partner.
- If a soft-launch test has not reached a few thousand users through the funnel, treat the project as unvalidated and do not add headcount.
- If a data or dashboard task repeats across games, automate it with tooling before hiring for it.
- If creative output is the bottleneck, use AI for concepts and asset drafts, but keep final creative direction with a human.
- If scaling beyond the initial team, run an explicit buy-vs-build analysis before hiring.
- If a required skill is missing (e.g. pathfinding, animation), source it early through a named community such as Hidden Devs.
- When a bottleneck role blocks validated work, hiring early is acceptable; otherwise keep the team lean before validation.
