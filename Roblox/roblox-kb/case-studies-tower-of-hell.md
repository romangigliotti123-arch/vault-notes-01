# Case study: Tower of Hell

**Genre:** Obby / platformer

Minimal-mechanic obstacle course that uses varied modes and challenges instead of a single completable course. Shows other players' progress on screen for soft competition. Sustained play for years in an uncontested category.

## Numbers
- 43K playing, listed as a comparable Obby & Platformer (RoWatcher tracker blog, 2026-10)

## What creators say about it
- Replayability comes from varied modes and challenges rather than one completable course; this flipped the obby genre. ([AlvinBlox 2025-04 1:25](https://youtu.be/Le4QhC3dDGk?t=85), [AlvinBlox 2025-04 1:25](https://youtu.be/Le4QhC3dDGk?t=85))
- Showing other players' progress on screen makes competition and replay more engaging. ([1Coal 2023-05 2:01](https://youtu.be/JzB8fpyofnw?t=121))
- Minimal mechanics plus replayability and soft competition sustain a game for years. ([StudioKrew (agency blog, GGAID data) 2026-05](https://studiokrew.com/blog/top-roblox-games-may-2026/))
- In an uncontested category a dominant title is hard to displace; Parkour Tower tried and failed. ([Roblox Creators 2026-09 7:00](https://youtu.be/hohwBQGQO7c?t=420))

## Transferable patterns
- Replay through modes, not one finished course
- Live progress visibility as soft competition
- Minimal mechanics for long lifespan
- Dominance in an uncontested niche is hard to challenge
