# Retention D1 D7

_day-1/day-7 retention, session length, daily rewards, hooks that bring players back._

Evidence base: 498 extracted items from 263 videos by 1Coal, 1_Bruce, Alex Builds Games, AlvinBlox, ArTheDev, Arow, ByteBlox, CORSO, CaberJaber, CallMeDogey, Creators on the Blox, Cumin, Daiorg, Deconstructor of Fun, Duckable, Emancyphur, Erthlord, GDC Festival of Gaming, GamesBeat, GnomeCode, ItsZyonic, Izzzyzzz, Last Level Studios, Lnor Roblox, LovJak, Matt The Method, MonarchDev, MonzterDEV, MoseyTube, Mulch, PineappleDev, Player.One, Poke, PolarBear, Quez, Raul, RoTrends (tracker), RoWatcher (tracker blog), Roblox, Roblox Creator Docs, Roblox Creators, Roblox DevForum, Roblox Investor Relations, Roblox public API, RoyalDev, Seibo, SimTek Game Development, SmartyRBX, Stewiepfing, StudioKrew (agency blog, GGAID data), TheDevKing, Tizzy, VuukStudios, Yann, anomalousDucky, bubDEV, janisjanis01, kra_DeZ, rasdev, waveshine, {EISABOOS} "RBX" (2017-11-29 to 2026-11-01). ## Overview
Creators and Roblox staff agree that retention (D1, D7, and now D8-28) is the top priority, because Roblox's algorithm gives more impressions to games players return to. Fix the first 5 minutes and onboarding for D1. Give visible short, mid and long-term goals plus weekly content for D7/D30. Add return hooks: daily rewards, login streaks, playtime rewards, offline progress, daily quests, timed events, FOMO drops, collections. Playing with friends is the strongest official predictor of return. Benchmarks: D1 about 10-16%, D7 about 2-3%, average session above 10 minutes. Caveats: Roblox staff warn against manipulative daily-login pressure. One creator (Yann) says daily rewards cannot fix a weak core loop. Most numbers are indie self-reported and unverified. Official Roblox sources say the algorithm now judges a 28-day window (day 1, days 2-7, days 8-28).

## Rules and findings
- **Prioritise retention before acquisition and monetisation** (multiple-creators): Roblox says order is fun/retention first, then monetisation, then acquisition. Retentive games get more impressions, so better retention brings free traffic even at some near-term monetisation cost. Fix retention before scaling ads or spend. Creators agree: ItsZyonic, VuukStudios ('retention is king'), rasdev, bubDEV. Sources: [Roblox 2023-09 2:27](https://youtu.be/9eaev-ypgWo?t=147), [Roblox 2023-09 9:31](https://youtu.be/Z-ZKqBPdl44?t=571), [ItsZyonic 2023-06 3:20](https://youtu.be/ODh2jPEGWUc?t=200), [Roblox 2026-10 9:06](https://youtu.be/bkRZeTZAvLw?t=546), [Roblox 2026-10 9:06](https://youtu.be/bkRZeTZAvLw?t=546). Newest source 2026-10-28.
- **Make the first 5 minutes (and first 30 seconds) fun and easy** (multiple-creators): Free-to-play players not hooked in about 5 minutes are exponentially unlikely to return. Avoid hard early obstacles, grind and long waits at the start. Keep early leveling fast (e.g. 2 kills per level). Cut first-action timers (a 30s crop growth made players quit; ~5s fixed it). Self-reported: rasdev says hook window is 8-15 seconds. - **Fix onboarding by finding the drop-off point** (multiple-creators): Use the New User First Session Retention curve and onboarding funnels to find where players leave, then replay the tutorial with a timer. Look for common actions or defeats just before quitting. Don't over-explain with text (speaker claims ~30% lost). Official example: a Dungeon Quest onboarding change gave +40% D1. - **End onboarding with a moment of joy and a reason to return tomorrow** (multiple-creators): Finish the tutorial with rewards, animations or celebratory effects. Give a curiosity hook for the next session (new base or pet to unlock, a daily quest on the main screen after the tutorial). Build for the second session, not just the first. - **Design for playing with friends** (multiple-creators): Official: a meaningful play session with a friend is the number one predictor of return. Roblox data: ~30% longer sessions, 60% more likely to return D0-D1 with friends, and the gap grows to week 12. Reward invites for both inviter and invitee. Social play makes players evergreen content for each other. - **Add daily rewards and login streaks as D1 return hooks** (multiple-creators): Daily login rewards, streaks, daily quests and limited events are the most common D1 hook across creators and official docs. Streaks work through fear of losing progress. Start small (consumables on day 1) and give a durable item after 7 straight days. Frame it as what you lose if you skip. Dailies give small, reliable soft currency. Indie examples: gifts every 5 minutes growing in value; boosts at 3, 6, 9 minutes; 3 and 10 minute thresholds. Timed rewards make boosts feel more valuable than a 30s timer. - **Provide short, mid and long-term goals visibly (D7 fix)** (multiple-creators): Low D7 usually means players don't know what to work toward. Add goals completable in a session, days and weeks, and show them in UI/world (skill trees, season passes, quests, collections). Dailies should be single-session short-term goals only. VuukStudios suggests visible goals at 3 hours, 10 hours, 7 days and 2 weeks. - **Build endgame and long-term goals for D30** (multiple-creators): Low D30 points to weak endgame, social bonds and ongoing challenges. Add evergreen goals. Give long grinds new things at each level (new currency, area, power-ups). Rebirth/prestige that keeps collected items carries progress over. Stretch power gains with timed dungeon entries and never double power. - **Show progress clearly and make level-ups matter** (multiple-creators): Show level X of Y and XP needed per level. A level-up that does nothing loses engagement. Add progress bars, XP effects, animations. Visible invested time raises the cost of leaving. Official data: a lightweight XP/level system with a leaderboard gave +7% D3 on Zoo Samurai. - **Pace content with progression and avoid multi-day level gaps** (multiple-creators): Estimate average daily earnings and price content to last a target number of days. Avoid 4-5 day levels at high levels. Use multi-week season passes (Jailbreak: good D7/D30 impact, two-month cycle). Season length: start at one month with a rest week between seasons. Space pass rewards relative to session length. - **Reward players even when they lose** (multiple-creators): Give XP on loss and visible persistent daily tasks so stuck players keep grinding. Use timed hints for struggling players. Avoid permadeath with long respawn waits (~10 min) and keep retry cheap. - **Use FOMO hooks, but recognise the ethical trade-off** (multiple-creators): Limited-time events, rare drops, scarce shop restocks (~5 min), leaderboards, expiring items and seasonal drops pull players back. AlvinBlox and a DevForum post say fear of missing out is stronger than desire to play. Roblox staff caution against pressure to log in daily (see disagreements). - **Offer offline progress and idle earnings** (multiple-creators): Progress that continues offline (crops growing, offline earnings, eggs hatching on timers) gives players something waiting at the next login and removes AFK auto-clicker advantage. - **Give collections, bases and sunk investment; add ranked and classes** (multiple-creators): Persistent bases, customisable spaces, collection indexes, pedestals visible to the server and limited skins create sunk cost and reasons to return. Ranked mode with medals and demotion, classes, trading and leaderboards also keep players maintaining status. - **Avoid endless loops; allow natural stopping points** (multiple-creators): Roblox considers continuous feeds without breaks inappropriate for younger users. - **Target new and returning players differently** (multiple-creators): Use new vs returning or first-played attributes to give easier settings to the first 0-30 days. - **Make every join feel new and give replay reasons** (multiple-creators): Open-ended roleplay and expandable systems, daily runs, seasonal events, unlockables, mysteries and story hooks. - **Frame limited-time content around loss, not just reward** (single-mention): AlvinBlox (indie, self-reported, 2025-06) claims fear of missing something beats the desire to play. ## Checklist
- [ ] Instrument D1, D7, D30 (and D8-28) by first-play cohort and acquisition source from day one
- [ ] Make first 30s and 5 min fun, low-grind, low-wait
- [ ] Find onboarding drop-off in First Session Retention curve and fix it
- [ ] End tutorial with a celebratory moment and a next-day hook
- [ ] Add daily login reward with 7-day streak and durable item
- [ ] Add playtime rewards at ~10/30/60 min (or shorter thresholds early)
- [ ] Add daily quests/challenges that reset on join and clear quickly
- [ ] Show short, mid and long-term goals in UI with progress bars
- [ ] Add offline progress or offline earnings
- [ ] Add timed events, rare drops and limited shop restocks
- [ ] Add collection index, base or pets for sunk investment
- [ ] Add rebirth/prestige or ranked system for D30
- [ ] Reward losing players with XP; keep respawns short
- [ ] Add friend-play features and invite rewards for both sides
- [ ] Prompt favorites (2-5 min delay) and notification opt-in
- [ ] Use experience notifications (13+, 1 per day) at meaningful moments
- [ ] Plan a day-8 update/event before launch
- [ ] Ship weekly or biweekly updates
- [ ] Make group/favorite rewards highly visible
- [ ] Reduce intermissions and walking; optimise performance for mobile
- [ ] Cap rewarded ads at 5-10 per day, let players bank
- [ ] Hit ~10+ minute average session before spending on ads
- [ ] Target D1 ~12-15%, D7 ~2-3%
- [ ] Fix the core loop before bolting on dailies
- [ ] Avoid manipulative daily pressure; don't reset player progress on updates
- [ ] Wrap friend-join and return-to-game teleports in a capped retry (example: 5 attempts, 1 s delay), then surface a clear failure message.
- [ ] Respect the flood delay (example: 15 s) so repeated teleport attempts don't get throttled and strand returning players.
- [ ] Verify current SafeTeleport retry and delay values in the official docs before hard-coding them.
- [ ] Tag lapsed players (no login for a set window) and run a win-back flow; track their return rate and spend separately.
- [ ] Add social account linking (e.g. Discord social SDK) and compare D28 retention for linked vs non-linked players.
- [ ] Compare play days per month for linked vs non-linked players to test the claimed 1.5-day lift in your own game.
- [ ] In evolution or rebirth games, check how early rebirth is available and test whether earlier access lifts D1 and D7.
- [ ] Log average session length alongside D1 return; if sessions are ~30 minutes with no return, add return hooks earlier.
- [ ] Confirm every death or wipe state leads to a restart, revive, or return-to-lobby option.
- [ ] Verify a lobby or restart path exists in every game mode, not just the main mode.
- [ ] Playtest the core loop for 30 minutes straight and note where it starts to feel repetitive.
- [ ] Time a typical run from start to finish or to party wipe, and shorten it if it exceeds one sitting.
- [ ] Test multiplayer runs with a full party (3 to 4 players) to see real run length.
- [ ] Use milestone letters or notes instead of constant on-screen dialogue to re-engage players.
- [ ] Record where playtesters quit so you can compare against the 10 to 30 minute sampling window.
- [ ] After a major update or event, watch daily player count for sharp drops and treat a steep fall as a signal to ship a fix or new content quickly.
- [ ] Pick a calendar length (for example 24 days) and decide whether a missed day resets rewards or only locks them.
- [ ] Test any 24-hour daily crate with a 30-second timer first to validate the claim loop.
- [ ] Add a one-time group-join plus favorite reward and track group and favorite counts over time.
- [ ] Check how far players actually progress on your daily ladder before adding more days.
- [ ] Use tiered gift boxes awarded from many different actions, so several activities feed the same reward hook.

## Where creators agree
- Retention (D1, D7, D30) is the most important metric and drives algorithmic discovery (Roblox official, VuukStudios, rasdev, bubDEV, ItsZyonic, janisjanis01).
- First minutes decide D1: Roblox Creators, MonzterDEV, VuukStudios, Lnor Roblox, Tizzy.
- Daily rewards, streaks and daily quests are standard D1 hooks (Roblox Creator Docs, SmartyRBX, rasdev, MoseyTube, janisjanis01, VuukStudios, LovJak).
- Mid/long-term goals fix D7/D30 (Roblox Creators, Roblox Docs, Tizzy, VuukStudios, rasdev).
- Social play improves retention (Roblox official, Roblox Docs).
- FOMO/limited-time events work (AlvinBlox, rasdev, DevForum authors).
- Offline progress is a cheap, strong hook (MoseyTube, AlvinBlox, rasdev).
- Regular updates sustain D7 and beyond (rasdev, VuukStudios, 1_Bruce, kra_DeZ, Lnor Roblox).

## Where creators disagree
- **Value of daily rewards / daily login pressure**
  - Roblox (2025-09) says avoid pressure to log in daily; fun to return to after two weeks.
  - SmartyRBX, janisjanis01, rasdev, VuukStudios recommend daily rewards and loss framing.
  - Yann says daily rewards won't fix a core loop problem.
  - DevForum authors say cheap daily rewards alone are weak and call them a small trick.
- **Which metric to prioritise**
  - bubDEV: D7/D30 over D1 or playtime.
  - DevForum (2023): average session time and D1 first.
  - Roblox Creators: day 1 and day 7 both; later algorithm windows add days 8-28.
  - VuukStudios: D7 above D1 is the strongest sign.
- **What counts as good D7**
  - 2019 DevForum: 2.68% is insanely low.
  - VuukStudios, MoseyTube (2026): 2-3% is good.
  - Tizzy: expects ~4% when populated; 1.45% seen.
- **Retention loss handling (ads/traffic dips)**
  - RoyalDev: weak retention from ads reflects game quality.
  - ItsZyonic: don't judge by CCU dropping when ads stop.
  - Yann: drop may come from similar-game players leaving.
- **Are session-time gimmicks effective**
  - PineappleDev and Arow claim UI polish and quote signs increase playtime (weak evidence).
  - Izzzyzzz warns of session-time padding patterns in brand games.

## Instructions for an agent using this file
- If D1 is below ~10%, then audit the first 5 minutes, the tutorial funnel and performance before adding rewards.
- If D1 is fine but D7 is under ~2%, then add visible short/mid/long-term goals, weekly events and content depth; don't just add daily rewards.
- If asked for benchmarks, then cite D1 12-15%, D7 2-3%, session above 10 min and label them indie self-reported except the official 16% D1 90th percentile.
- If building a return hook, then stack daily reward + streak + playtime rewards + daily quests + offline progress, and use loss framing sparingly given Roblox's anti-pressure stance.
- If D1 drops suddenly, then segment by acquisition source and cohort before changing the game.
- If launching, then plan a day-8 hook and weekly updates because spikes halve in ~3 weeks and the algorithm judges days 8-28.
- If a game is social, then add invite rewards for both sides and friend-play incentives.
- If using notifications, then restrict to 13+ opted-in users, max 1 per day, tied to meaningful events.
- If ads bring players who leave fast, then fix the game, not the ad; use Beta mode to optimise out of home recs.
- If a source makes an unverified claim (90% event churn, quote signs raising playtime), then flag it as weak evidence.
- Do not treat platform-wide DAU (N7469) as a retention benchmark for an individual game; use cohort D1/D7/D30 data from the game's own analytics instead.
- Q1768 supports the existing FOMO rule (Use FOMO hooks, but recognise the ethical trade-off); do not add it as a new rule and do not weaken the ethical caveat when citing it.
- When a retention flow depends on teleporting (friend joins, return-to-game links), add a capped retry with a delay and a visible failure state, citing N7596's example values.
- Do not treat the SafeTeleport example values as fixed platform limits; confirm against current official docs before use.
- If a game has a social-linking option (e.g. Discord social SDK), treat linking as a D28 retention feature and compare linked vs non-linked cohorts before crediting it.
- If a player has been inactive for a set period, route them into a win-back flow and measure their spend and return rate separately from new users.
- If the game is an evolution or rebirth style, test how early rebirth is reachable against D1 and D7 cohorts before tuning it.
- Treat all figures from indie sources here (31% D28 lift, 1.5 extra play days, 30-minute sessions, 10-minute unlock wait) as self-reported and unverified; do not present them as benchmarks without caveats.
- If a game has no lobby, restart, or revive path after death, add one before tuning other D1 or D7 hooks.
- If a run takes longer than one sitting in playtests, shorten it or add natural stopping points before adding rewards.
- Treat the Cumin session-length figures as one self-reported data point; do not generalise them into a Roblox-wide benchmark.
- When assessing a core loop, playtest it for at least 30 minutes, since the reported drop-off point is 10 to 30 minutes.
- Prefer milestone letters or notes over constant on-screen dialogue when building re-engagement touches.
- Cite the T and N IDs above when recommending run-length or lobby changes, and flag the figures as self-reported.
- If a player has been absent for more than a day, prioritise a return hook (login reward, update note, or event) over a long-term reminder.
- Do not treat YouTube watch-percentage targets (N7958) as in-game retention metrics.
- Skip adding new FOMO or update-cadence advice that repeats existing rules; cite the existing rule instead.
- If asked for a day-1 retention benchmark, state that the sources give none (N8141) and do not invent a number.
- Treat the tripled session-time claim (N8080, Q1950) as an unverified self-report and say so when citing it.
- When recommending a daily login mechanic, offer both a reset-on-miss calendar (T18844) and a simple 24-hour crate (T18877), and say which fits a habit loop vs. a casual return.
- When designing a daily reward ladder, check leaderboard or cohort depth data before going past day 10 (N8135).
- Flag that all janisjanis01 figures are self-reported and from a single indie developer.
