# ThatGuyEvan - How to make successful Roblox games

ThatGuyEvan's 8 videos (2018-2022) are mostly commentary, event history and UGC opinion, not a repeatable game-building method. The actionable material is narrow: a 512x512 PNG icon with outer glow and gradient stroke (WLU95XteiqA); Premium Payouts pay for Premium-subscriber playtime without gamepasses (PHi1aaPhDzg, hEEFmlIrWKw); and event lessons from the egg-hunt history (-4kisZLNwrk, APeSOO8WRPQ): build a connected hub, avoid thin or paywalled content, protect rewards server-side, and announce only what you will ship. Treat all revenue figures as unverified anecdotes.

**Style:** Casual, entertainment-first commentary built on opinions, personal anecdotes and event retrospectives. Useful for Roblox history and for lessons on what players punished. Offers little structured pipeline, so it suits developers wanting context and cautionary examples rather than a step-by-step build-and-launch method.

## Playbook
1. **Pick a concept with a clear hook and narrative** [[game-ideas-and-genres]]: Story-led events scored best: the 2018 hunt's gated finale and story gave the strongest reception (-4kisZLNwrk). The 2015 hunt used three minigames for 22 eggs and is widely seen as the worst. Scope content to what you can actually ship.
2. **Design a connected, explorable world** [[building-and-level-design]]: The 2013 hunt spread 34 eggs across six teleporter-linked areas and reached 2M visits in a week (-4kisZLNwrk). Connected spaces give players reasons to keep moving and return.
3. **Plan UGC-friendly items if you sell cosmetics** [[ugc-and-avatar-economy]]: The speaker likes letting creators make catalog items and speculated a 70% creator share, unconfirmed (BLsdGUmVhjE). He suggests learning Blender first. Judge concepts on looks, uniqueness and wearability before pricing (aOjCEe0LL6k).
4. **Protect rewards and leaderboards on the server** [[anti-exploit-and-security]]: Verify leaderboard and reward results server-side; footage of a suspected exploit winner was repeatedly removed, fuelling cheating suspicion (APeSOO8WRPQ). Vet free models before inserting them, and avoid one-of-one rewards that reveal a winner's identity.
5. **Keep the event in one hub with no paywalled progression** [[events-and-seasonal-content]]: The 2020 hunt split 48 eggs across 48 games with no hub and sold paid passes for easier eggs, drawing heavy backlash (-4kisZLNwrk). Keep progression in one connected hub. The 2019 hunt's forced second boss fight for the good ending was also disliked.
6. **Make a 512x512 PNG game icon** [[thumbnails-icons]]: Create a 512x512 canvas, cut out the subject, add outer glow and a gradient stroke, export PNG, then upload via the Configure Place Game Icon page in Creator Dashboard (WLU95XteiqA, 2018). Keep the file in a GFX folder for re-uploads.
7. **Keep trailers, merch and chat age-appropriate** [[age-ratings-moderation-and-tos]]: Content acceptable in one context may break rules on another surface, so keep promo trailers and merch age-appropriate. Route all in-game chat through filtered channels, since guest or special-guest chat may bypass filters (APeSOO8WRPQ).
8. **Treat Premium Payouts as a bonus, not a plan** [[monetisation-other]]: Premium Payouts pay Robux for Premium-subscriber playtime with no gamepass needed (PHi1aaPhDzg). The rate is undisclosed; 5 Robux per 10 minutes is illustrative only. A same-day test showed about 15 Robux per 5 hours (hEEFmlIrWKw), a single unverified anecdote.
9. **Check Developer Stats before projecting earnings** [[analytics-metrics]]: The speaker estimated ~50% Premium share from ~300 daily visitors and a 2-minute average session (hEEFmlIrWKw). Treat that method as rough. Read your own Premium share and session length in Developer Stats before forecasting.
10. **Concentrate the event for concurrency** [[social-features-and-virality]]: The 2012 hunt in one game peaked near 20,000 players, above the prior 9,350 record (-4kisZLNwrk). Concentrating an event in one place drove record concurrency; spreading it across 48 games drew backlash in 2020.
11. **Vet sponsors and partner events** [[brand-deals-and-ip-licensing]]: Sponsored artist concerts were widely disliked, while sponsor events with Nazix and 21 Pilots were well received (APeSOO8WRPQ). Choose partners whose audience, tone and rating fit your game.
12. **Announce only what you will ship** [[common-mistakes]]: Cancelled sponsored events and scrapped near-finished events frustrated players (APeSOO8WRPQ). Promising a limited item and not delivering was treated as a scam. Avoid leaks and early promises you may not keep.
13. **Flex event length on engagement** [[events-and-seasonal-content]]: The 2017 hunt was extended by a week after strong performance (-4kisZLNwrk). Use live metrics to decide whether an event should run longer rather than fixing its length in advance.
14. **Use updates to spike visits, then measure** [[updates-live-ops]]: The speaker says the last major update lifted Adopt Me visits from about 536 thousand to over 1 million (FkQRh1r1OpM). Treat that as one anecdote. Track visits around each update to test the effect in your own game.

## Common mistakes
- Shipping thin event content: the 2015 hunt used three minigames for 22 eggs and was widely seen as the worst (-4kisZLNwrk).
- Fragmenting progression across many games with no hub, as in the 2020 hunt across 48 games (-4kisZLNwrk).
- Paywalling easier progression with paid passes, which drew heavy backlash (-4kisZLNwrk).
- Making the best ending require repeating a boss fight, disliked in 2019 (-4kisZLNwrk).
- Announcing or leaking content you may not ship; cancelled and scrapped events frustrated players (APeSOO8WRPQ).
- Promising a limited item and not delivering, which was treated as a scam (APeSOO8WRPQ).
- Showing public winner names tied to valuable rewards, which led to doxxing (APeSOO8WRPQ).
- Inserting unvetted free models and running without server-side anti-exploit checks (APeSOO8WRPQ).
- Running gamble-style blind-box or gift purchases without clearly disclosing contents (APeSOO8WRPQ).
- Quoting unverified estimates as fact: the Adopt Me revenue totals and the 10-minutes-to-5-Robux example are not confirmed (FkQRh1r1OpM, PHi1aaPhDzg).

## How the advice changed over time
- Icon guidance is dated: the 2018 video names the Configure Place Game Icon page in Creator Dashboard, while later sample notes use Creations > game > Configure > Game Icon. UI paths have changed since 2018, so verify current steps.
- Premium Payouts location conflicts: sample notes cite both Developer Stats (business-and-payouts) and Creator Dashboard > Premium Payouts (monetisation-other). The 2020 video (hEEFmlIrWKw) uses Developer Stats, so prefer that, and verify.
- Premium Payouts scale: the illustrative 5 Robux per 10 minutes (PHi1aaPhDzg) conflicts with the same-day observed ~15 Robux per 5 hours (hEEFmlIrWKw). The observation is a more realistic sense of scale, but it is a single day and unverified.
- UGC creator share: the 2019 70% figure is speculation drawn from the paid-access split. No confirmed creator share appears in these videos, so do not plan around it.
- Event design advice shifted over time: the 2013 and 2017-2018 hunts were praised for connected maps, teleporters and story, while the 2019-2020 hunts drew criticism for fragmentation and paywalls. The 2022 retrospective (APeSOO8WRPQ) is the most recent view and favours avoiding both.
- Revenue sources are inconsistent: the 2020 Adopt Me estimates (FkQRh1r1OpM) include an internally inconsistent DevEx conversion (600M Robux at ~3 USD per 1,000 is about 1.8M USD, not the 6.5M stated) and an unreliable gamepass total, so they should not be used as benchmarks.
