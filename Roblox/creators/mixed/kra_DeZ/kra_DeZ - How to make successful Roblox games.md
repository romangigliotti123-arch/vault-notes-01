# kra_DeZ - How to make successful Roblox games

Build a small, instantly understandable game for Roblox's mostly-kid audience, fast (days, with AI help), and check first that the trend has few existing Roblox games. Launch with minimal scope and a replayable loop. Sell cheap, repeatable consumable devproducts. Test thumbnails with short ad bursts, but only once organic retention is already good. Then update at least weekly (more often in launch week), using event hype and group notifications. Drop dead projects rather than pouring earnings into ads. The creator's CCU, Robux and group-growth figures are self-reported.

**Style:** Fast-build, number-heavy creator who shows his own games (Spin or Die, Spinner Dye, The Darkrooms). Strong on ad-test mechanics and update cadence. Light on verifiable method, heavy on motivation and course promotion. Best for solo devs running quick, cheap experiments aimed at kid audiences. Treat earnings claims and currency conversions as unverified.

## Playbook
1. **Search Roblox for the trend before building** [[trend-research-and-validation]]: Check whether the trend already has Roblox games. One or two existing games means the niche is open; many means skip it or add a clear angle. Pivot to an open niche when you find one. (sB9W5BXjQpk, cJ-YzHHhQs8)
2. **Adapt a proven idea into a simple game** [[game-ideas-and-genres]]: Pull ideas from recent TikTok trends not yet on Roblox, Steam hits not yet on Roblox, or remixes of games played with friends. The rule is that a kid should understand the game in seconds. (sB9W5BXjQpk, dE0njfPNsdY)
3. **Add a unique angle instead of cloning** [[game-ideas-and-genres]]: Exact copies underperform. A dodgeball clone got about 58 visits, while baseball-bat variants drew thousands of players. Trend-driven games often fade fast, so plan the next idea in advance. (cJ-YzHHhQs8, sB9W5BXjQpk)
4. **Ship a minimal first version** [[development-process-and-speed]]: Spin or Die began as a two-baseplate game with near-zero scope, built in about 3 days. Spinner Dye took 3-4 days and outearned a 3-year project. Time spent is not a proxy for income. (g6z18oHjbYE, ZradA-cygP0, dE0njfPNsdY)
5. **Use AI for most of the build, but understand the code** [[ai-and-tools]]: The creator cites Claude for most of the build and says it cut build time to about two days. Use AI for scripts and thumbnails, but read and understand every script so you can fix bugs yourself. (cJ-YzHHhQs8)
6. **Add a clear finale and a reason to replay** [[core-loops-and-game-design]]: Replace a boring final showdown with a one-on-one duel for the last two players; the creator says growth began after this change. A game beaten once gives no reason to return, so design a loop that brings players back. (cJ-YzHHhQs8, dE0njfPNsdY)
7. **Sell consumable devproducts, not gamepasses** [[monetisation-devproducts]]: In Spin or Die, devproducts (e.g. Nuke All, 19,349 Robux in 90 days) earned most of the Robux while gamepasses earned almost nothing. Cheap, fun items such as a four-bullet gun can be bought repeatedly. Exception: a horror game's gamepass tied to a zombies mode earned 600k Robux. (ZradA-cygP0, dE0njfPNsdY)
8. **Generate many thumbnails and test them in the first ad run** [[thumbnails-icons]]: Generate 20+ AI thumbnails and shortlist up to about 15 creatives for the first run. The creator's newer advice is to test many rather than two; his two-thumbnail test is one he regrets. (lrubtajkaeI, cJ-YzHHhQs8)
9. **Benchmark CTR to keep or drop each thumbnail** [[thumbnails-icons]]: Under about 1-1.5% CTR, drop the thumbnail. Around 2.5% is average, and 3.5%+ is exceptional. The winning thumbnail reached about 4%. (lrubtajkaeI)
10. **Run a cheap test, then a scaled campaign on the winner** [[ads-and-sponsored-experiences]]: Test two thumbnails for about 3 days on a lifetime budget of ~30 credits. Then run ~90 credits over 4-5 days on the winner only (about 27,000 plays). Set the objective to Plays with audience All Players, and start the next campaign as the previous one ends. (lrubtajkaeI, cJ-YzHHhQs8)
11. **Spend on ads only after organic stats are good** [[ads-and-sponsored-experiences]]: Ads produced spikes that faded: roughly 40-80 players during ads, 5-10 afterward. Check retention and playtime first. The creator reinvested under half of Darkrooms' earnings into ads with declining results. (cJ-YzHHhQs8, dE0njfPNsdY)
12. **Add in-game follow and group-join prompts** [[social-features-and-virality]]: Place a touch part that prompts players to follow the scheduled event; followers are notified when the update drops, and disable it after release. Paste your group ID into a group-join prompt. The creator claims his group grew from ~19k to 150k members in weeks. (oMI-GhUcfFE)
13. **Update at least weekly, and push daily tiny updates in launch week** [[updates-live-ops]]: Small frequent updates revived Spin or Die from about 20 active players, and player count rose after each one. Big content dumps ran out of ideas. Skipping updates led to a flatline and lost algorithm recommendations. Quiet games still deserve updates. (g6z18oHjbYE, cJ-YzHHhQs8, sB9W5BXjQpk)
14. **Hype each update with an event, a countdown icon and group notifications** [[community-and-discord]]: Create an Events and Updates entry about one week ahead with a thumbnail, category and description. Set a countdown icon (e.g. 24 hours, then 1 hour) as the game icon. On release, send a group notification to all members. (oMI-GhUcfFE)

## Common mistakes
- Judging a quiet game's design before promoting it; most failures come from nobody knowing it exists (sB9W5BXjQpk)
- Copying a trend exactly with no unique selling point; clones get few visits (cJ-YzHHhQs8)
- Making a scary or hard horror game for a kids-heavy platform (dE0njfPNsdY)
- Bolting a second mode such as zombies onto an existing game instead of building a separate game (dE0njfPNsdY)
- Testing only two thumbnails and calling the result final (lrubtajkaeI)
- Pouring earnings into ads to keep a dying game alive (dE0njfPNsdY)
- Stopping updates, which led to a flatline and lost algorithm recommendations (cJ-YzHHhQs8)
- Running ads before organic retention and playtime are good (dE0njfPNsdY)
- Judging ad results too early; growth lagged for months and came via a later mechanic update (lrubtajkaeI)
- Treating pending Robux as earned cash; check the DevEx conversion before setting expectations (oMI-GhUcfFE)

## Launch checklist
- [ ] Search Roblox for the trend; confirm only one or two existing games
- [ ] Add a unique angle and state the game's rule in one kid-readable sentence
- [ ] Ship a minimal playable v1 with a single core loop
- [ ] Design a finale and a reason to replay
- [ ] Add a few cheap, fun consumable devproducts; avoid relying on gamepasses
- [ ] Create an Events and Updates entry with thumbnail, category and description
- [ ] Place a follow touch part for the scheduled event
- [ ] Add a group-join prompt using your group ID
- [ ] Generate 20+ thumbnails and shortlist up to ~15 for testing
- [ ] Run a ~30-credit, ~3-day test on lifetime budget, objective Plays, audience All Players
- [ ] Drop any thumbnail under ~1-1.5% CTR; target 2.5%+
- [ ] Confirm organic retention and playtime are good before any large spend
- [ ] Scale ~90 credits over 4-5 days on the winning thumbnail only
- [ ] Schedule the next campaign to start as the previous one ends
- [ ] Plan small updates for the first week after release
- [ ] Set a countdown icon as the game icon for hype
- [ ] Send group notifications to all members on each update
- [ ] Disable the follow script after release and track DevEx conversion to set expectations

## How the advice changed over time
- Thumbnail testing: the Sep 6 advice tested two thumbnails. The newer Oct 20 advice, which prevails, says test up to ~15 creatives in the first run because two was too few.
- Ads: in Oct 11 he reinvested earnings in ads to keep The Darkrooms alive, with declining results. Oct 20 advice is to advertise only games with good organic retention and playtime. The newer advice prevails.
- Persistence conflict: Aug-Sep messaging says 'don't give up' and keep updating; Oct 11 says move on rather than pouring years and earnings into a failing game. Reconciling the two: keep cheap updates on games with a live audience, and stop reinvesting when retention is dead.
- Build time varies by source: 3 days (Aug 31), 3-4 days (Sep 25), about two days with AI (later tip). Treat these as a range of roughly 2-4 days.
- Monetisation stance is consistently devproduct-first, but The Darkrooms' Survivor Kit gamepass (600k Robux) shows gamepasses can sell well when tied to a specific mode.
