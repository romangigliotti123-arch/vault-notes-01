# ThePurpleGuy_0 - How to make successful Roblox games

Based on four videos, this creator treats Roblox as a long-run bet with slow payback. Paid ads buy exposure and player-base building, not immediate Robux (VEkuM5ZG6G4, JXg2ACdk_wM). Only ship a game once it is fully ready, since ad spend is committed once credits are used. For UGC, expect roughly a year to recoup upload and promotion costs, and rely on consistent releases plus limited items for the biggest earners (EDHExZQHk4I, zurNJYYXVhk). The sources say little about game ideation or building, so the playbook covers launch, ads, and UGC economics.

**Style:** Candid, numbers-first "I spent X Robux" transparency. Shows losses and results openly and reviews campaigns after the fact. Best suited to developers weighing paid ads or UGC spend who want realistic payback expectations, not a polished growth formula.

## Playbook
1. **Idea: pick a concept you can finish and ship** [[game-ideas-and-genres]]: Sources give no detailed ideation method. The creator's Burning Simulator test used a game bought from Fiverr, so a finished, playable base was the starting point for the paid test (JXg2ACdk_wM).
2. **Idea: for UGC, pick a genre you can build an audience around** [[trend-research-and-validation]]: Build a community around one UGC genre. The creator's Naruto Buddy item was part of this approach (EDHExZQHk4I, zurNJYYXVhk).
3. **Build: finish and bug-fix before any paid promotion** [[testing-debugging-and-qa]]: Unfinished beta bugs cost six dislikes and forced a pause and relaunch. Ads only go to a stable build (VEkuM5ZG6G4).
4. **Build: make the first session strong enough to convert** [[first-session-onboarding]]: Burning Simulator had a 7.14% player conversion rate and 4.6-minute average play time, which the creator called not bad (JXg2ACdk_wM).
5. **Launch: plan the ad budget as exposure spend** [[ads-and-sponsored-experiences]]: Budget as if the spend will not return. Burning Simulator spent 10,000 Robux (40 credits) over 2 days and earned 260 Robux (JXg2ACdk_wM).
6. **Launch: test two ad creatives and compare CTR and cost per play** [[ab-testing-and-experiments]]: In the first campaign, ad 2 beat ad 1 on CTR (3.62% vs 2.19%) and cost per play (0.008 vs 0.010 credits) (VEkuM5ZG6G4).
7. **Launch: check thumbnails and icons before buying ads** [[thumbnails-icons]]: Burning Simulator had 380k impressions but only 1.34% CTR, which the creator called low. Improve the thumbnail or icon before spending (JXg2ACdk_wM).
8. **Launch: set up monetisation before traffic arrives** [[monetisation-gamepasses]]: Ad traffic from Burning Simulator earned 140 Robux from developer products and 0 from gamepasses. Slap Glove's 49 Robux gamepass earned 102 Robux (VEkuM5ZG6G4, JXg2ACdk_wM).
9. **Growth: track ad metrics and exclude your own test buys** [[analytics-metrics]]: Remove self-test purchases from sales totals. The creator removed two self-tests from developer product earnings in the Burning Simulator data (JXg2ACdk_wM).
10. **Growth: use creator rewards as passive income** [[monetisation-other]]: Creator rewards paid 135 Robux in the first campaign (5 Robux per paying user playing 10 minutes, if one of their first three games that day) and 120 Robux in the Burning Simulator campaign (VEkuM5ZG6G4, JXg2ACdk_wM).
11. **Growth: for UGC, release a steady stream of items** [[development-process-and-speed]]: Total UGC sales rose from 221 (8 months) to 758 (12 months). Upload fee is 750 Robux per item, so each release adds cost before it earns (EDHExZQHk4I, zurNJYYXVhk).
12. **Growth: price-test and promote UGC limiteds carefully** [[ugc-and-avatar-economy]]: Limited items are the biggest earners. Ice Antlers reached 414 sales and 12,082 Robux by 12 months, far above the rest of the catalogue (zurNJYYXVhk).
13. **Live ops: keep reinvesting in content and recheck costs each period** [[business-and-payouts]]: Spend rose from about 31,150 Robux (Feb) to about 34,900 Robux (Jul). The creator reached first profit only after one year, with 35,966 Robux earned (EDHExZQHk4I, zurNJYYXVhk).
14. **Live ops: consider new limited drops and track which items keep earning** [[updates-live-ops]]: Lower-tier items barely moved over time. Devil's Halo stayed at 3 sales and Fire Buddy at 24 sales, so keep reviewing item performance before investing further (zurNJYYXVhk).

## Common mistakes
- Buying ads before the game is bug-free; beta bugs forced a pause and relaunch (VEkuM5ZG6G4)
- Treating ad spend as payback; the Burning Simulator campaign spent 10,000 Robux and earned 260 (JXg2ACdk_wM)
- Launching ads with a low CTR (1.34% in the Burning Simulator test) instead of fixing the thumbnail or icon first (JXg2ACdk_wM)
- Counting your own test purchases as sales; two self-tests were removed from developer product totals (JXg2ACdk_wM)
- Expecting one UGC item to pay back its 750 Robux upload fee and promotion costs quickly (EDHExZQHk4I)
- Stopping releases; sales growth came from continued drops, so a pause slows the catalogue (zurNJYYXVhk)
- Assuming a good UGC item will pay off within months; the creator needed about a year to break even (zurNJYYXVhk)

## Launch checklist
- [ ] Game is fully playable with no known bugs before buying any ad (VEkuM5ZG6G4)
- [ ] Set a Robux budget you can afford to lose as exposure spend (JXg2ACdk_wM)
- [ ] Prepare two ad creatives or thumbnails to A/B test CTR and cost per play (VEkuM5ZG6G4)
- [ ] Confirm the thumbnail or icon gets a CTR above 1.34% before spending (JXg2ACdk_wM)
- [ ] Add gamepasses and developer products before the campaign starts (VEkuM5ZG6G4, JXg2ACdk_wM)
- [ ] Enable creator rewards and check the 10-minute and first-three-games conditions (VEkuM5ZG6G4)
- [ ] Track impressions, CTR, plays, and conversion from day one (JXg2ACdk_wM)
- [ ] Exclude your own test purchases from sales data (JXg2ACdk_wM)
- [ ] For UGC, budget 750 Robux per item plus promotion costs (EDHExZQHk4I)
- [ ] Plan at least one new UGC release per period to keep sales rising (zurNJYYXVhk)
