# DaiorgYT - How to make successful Roblox games

DaiorgYT's core advice: make something simple, original and replayable that is fun to play, rather than cash-first or a trend clone. Clones only succeed with a distinct new mechanic or spin. Build a short, repeatable loop with learnable, luck-free difficulty, give players reasons to retry (such as death-revealed lore), and expect long dev cycles for big updates. His notes are mostly entertainment and lore commentary, so direct production, scripting and business advice is thin.

**Style:** Opinionated essay and niche-analysis commentary aimed at players and aspiring devs. Strongest on design principles: replayability, unique spins, fun over monetisation, and what makes games last. Light on tooling, scripting and business specifics. Suits devs wanting a design lens on genres and trends, not a how-to build guide.

## Playbook
1. **1. Idea: start from a concept you care about** [[game-ideas-and-genres]]: Pick a game or topic you are passionate about and script a first version rather than waiting for the perfect idea. Blend proven inspirations (e.g. Doors gameplay, Iron Lung underwater, SCP lore) into something distinct, keeping it simple and accessible (nutv8RT5UrQ, GkKUU9mCgwc).
2. **2. Validate: check the trend before following it** [[trend-research-and-validation]]: A viral trend will spawn many clones quickly. Join a proven niche only with a distinct spin, ideally a new mechanic. The speaker suggests nostalgia-style aesthetics may get overmilked and fatigue players (nutv8RT5UrQ, tMNQooZtTQo).
3. **3. Build: design a short, repeatable core loop** [[core-loops-and-game-design]]: Use a loop players can repeat quickly. Grow a Garden is the example: plant, wait about five minutes for seeds to grow, harvest, buy seeds, repeat. Simple cheap concepts can also spark genre waves because they need little scripting knowledge (GkKUU9mCgwc).
4. **3. Build: make failure feel like learning** [[core-loops-and-game-design]]: Use learnable, skill-based difficulty with no luck element so players can see why they failed. Give freedom to experiment, memorable mechanics, and a large payoff on completion. Rage games like Getting Over It and Jump King show these traits (vdxPCysBo70).
5. **3. Build: vary runs and give each threat its own counter** [[core-loops-and-game-design]]: Generate rooms randomly from many variants with several routes each so runs feel unpredictable. Give each threat a distinct mechanic and counter rather than repeating one scare type. Reveal lore through death so each failure rewards a reason to retry (Yku_51UE_GM).
6. **3. Build: balance roles in two-player games** [[core-loops-and-game-design]]: Balance each player's role and difficulty so neither carries more weight. Otherwise players get frustrated and cooperation drops. Two-player obbies are one niche the speaker covers (nutv8RT5UrQ).
7. **3. Build: let ambience drive tension** [[building-art-and-assets]]: Use layered ambient sound (growls, whispers, water bubbling) as the main scare driver. The speaker says ambience scares more than the monsters themselves (Yku_51UE_GM).
8. **3. Build: ship rough but playable, then refine** [[development-process-and-speed]]: Ship rough-but-playable versions and keep refining. The speaker expects polish to arrive as the developers finish the game, and he praises DOORS' loop and visuals despite rough edges (LXRBAzLaxwQ).
9. **4. Launch: gate social features behind moderation** [[social-features-and-virality]]: Ship player-hosted parties or other social features only with moderation in place. Meep City's unmoderated parties caused safety issues and a decline (GkKUU9mCgwc).
10. **4. Launch: keep monetisation secondary to gameplay** [[monetisation-other]]: Do not rely on monetisation-heavy design with little gameplay. The speaker criticises RNG and cash-grab games for lacking fun, which he says hurts long-term quality. Adopt Me shows a monetisation-heavy design can still succeed (GkKUU9mCgwc).
11. **5. Growth: read player counts with caution** [[analytics-metrics]]: Treat headline concurrent-player figures skeptically, since the speaker notes bots may inflate records (Grow a Garden cited near 20M CCU). Judge health against current benchmarks, not old ones (GkKUU9mCgwc).
12. **5. Growth: a trading and cosmetic economy can keep earning** [[monetisation-gamepasses]]: Trading and cosmetic item economies can keep a game earning long after content updates stop. Murder Mystery 2 sells weapon skins and rarely gets content updates (GkKUU9mCgwc).
13. **6. Live ops: plan long cycles for big expansions** [[updates-live-ops]]: A major content update can take about two years to develop, as DOORS' Floor 2 Mines update did. Plan for long dev cycles for large floor-style expansions (LXRBAzLaxwQ).
14. **6. Live ops: keep the core simple so it lasts** [[updates-live-ops]]: Natural Disaster Survival and Work at a Pizza Place stayed popular for years with minimal objectives. Keep the core design simple and fun, and avoid cloning existing video games, which can drive early growth but hurts reputation with gamers (nutv8RT5UrQ, GkKUU9mCgwc).

## Common mistakes
- Designing monetisation-heavy, gameplay-light games (RNG or cash-grab) that the speaker says hurt long-term quality (GkKUU9mCgwc).
- Shipping player-hosted social features without moderation, as Meep City did (GkKUU9mCgwc).
- Cloning existing video games such as Murder Mystery from Garry's Mod; it drives early growth but hurts platform reputation (GkKUU9mCgwc).
- Making a clone that repeats the original without a new mechanic or spin (nutv8RT5UrQ).
- Unbalanced two-player roles or difficulty, which frustrates players and reduces cooperation (nutv8RT5UrQ).
- Relying on one scare type or monsters alone instead of layered ambience and varied threats (Yku_51UE_GM).
- Chasing a fading nostalgia aesthetic that may get overmilked (tMNQooZtTQo).
- Using luck-based difficulty, where failure does not feel like learning (vdxPCysBo70).
- Judging success by bot-inflated concurrent-player spikes (GkKUU9mCgwc).
- Waiting for the perfect idea instead of scripting a first version and shipping (sample tip; source video not specified).
