# GameMakersDev - How to make successful Roblox games

Build a fun, engaging base game first, because monetisation only augments a solid game. Understand what Roblox players actually want, test many versions cheaply and quickly, and keep what works. Publish once to all platforms and let Roblox handle netcode and infrastructure. Keep the team small and connected, with a shared vision rather than money as the goal. Monetise through game passes and optional items, avoid pay-to-win, and use Premium Payouts tied to engagement. Expect discoverability to be hard: keep promoting, iterate after launch, and leave trusted people in charge of live ops. Source figures are from 2020-2021 and should be verified against current platform terms.

**Style:** Interview and panel-based, drawing on platform executives and working developers rather than tutorials. Stresses vision, player understanding, and engagement over monetisation tactics. Suits developers who want strategic context on the Roblox ecosystem, team building, and the business side before writing code.

## Playbook
1. **1. Pick a novel idea that players and creators want to talk about** [[game-ideas-and-genres]]: Make a novel idea that YouTubers would want to cover, since creator coverage is a major discovery driver on Roblox. Build a strong shared vision that attracts people into loose orbits around the project.
2. **2. Research what Roblox players actually want** [[trend-research-and-validation]]: Successful developers take time to understand what players on Roblox want, test ideas, and keep what works. Note that Roblox games are social and synchronous, unlike mostly asynchronous mobile games, so do not assume mobile designs transfer.
3. **3. Start with a small, connected team** [[team-and-budget]]: Chemistry matters more than headcount; a small connected team can outperform 20 people. Reference point from a 2021 panel: a successful title ran with 6 full-time salaried staff plus about 22 contractors.
4. **4. Learn Lua/Luau by dissecting and breaking code** [[scripting-and-performance]]: Don't fear Lua; it is relatively easy to pick up. Luau is backward compatible with Lua and adds strict typing. Learn by setting incremental goals, such as a part that kills on touch, and dissecting others' code line by line before experimenting in Studio.
5. **5. Build the core loop and engagement first** [[core-loops-and-game-design]]: Prioritise engagement first; monetisation is an augmentation of a solid base game, not a substitute. Roblox handles netcode and backend, so focus effort on gameplay.
6. **6. Iterate quickly and cheaply** [[development-process-and-speed]]: Roblox makes publishing, launching and acquiring users fast and cheap, so test many versions. Publish once to all platforms; only minor UI/UX changes are needed per platform, except Xbox.
7. **7. Design the first session to hook players** [[first-session-onboarding]]: Keep the opening fast and clear so new players quickly reach the core fun. Treat the first session as the place where the engagement loop from step 5 must already work.
8. **8. Monetise with game passes and optional items** [[monetisation-gamepasses]]: Reserve monetisation for game passes and show purchasable options in the main menu so players see what they can unlock. In progression or social games, sell pay-to-skip or pay-to-get-ahead rather than only cosmetics that split paying and non-paying players.
9. **9. Choose monetisation that fits the game** [[monetisation-other]]: Developers choose their own monetisation: cosmetics, in-game items, both, or none. Socially visible items such as UGC accessories sell well because players wear them to express identity. Loot boxes were about 1% of one game's revenue and were dropped for integrity reasons.
10. **10. Launch and set up discovery** [[discovery-algorithm]]: Good games can flop at launch and succeed later; discoverability is a real issue. Plan to keep promoting after launch. Watch teams in Roblox's accelerator program, whose projects tend to be strong bets.
11. **11. Build the thumbnail and title for clicks** [[thumbnails-icons]]: Discovery depends on how a game looks and is named in listings and feeds. Use a clear, readable thumbnail and a title that signals the core fun. (Specific guidance was not covered in these two videos; treat as general practice.)
12. **12. Track retention and engagement metrics** [[analytics-metrics]]: Premium Payouts pay developers based on user engagement, so track engagement hours and return visits, not just purchases. Roblox reported 1.5 billion monthly engagement hours in 2020 as the platform-level scale to aim within.
13. **13. Grow through social and creator reach** [[social-features-and-virality]]: Consider paid social boosts that affect nearby players in RPGs, which make spending more social and fun. Use community and internship routes via Roblox groups and Discord to find help and collaborators.
14. **14. Keep updating and leave trusted people in charge** [[updates-live-ops]]: After release, leave trusted team members in charge of maintaining the game creatively and analytically. Rigid company structure usually forms only after release. Keep shipping updates to hold players, since over-monetised games lose players rather than whales staying.

## Common mistakes
- Over-monetising: pay-to-win designs self-correct because players leave for other content.
- Assuming mobile game designs transfer, when Roblox games are social and synchronous.
- Treating money as the end goal instead of a clear shared vision for the game.
- Skipping player research and assuming the team knows what Roblox players want.
- Expecting a good game to succeed immediately; discoverability is a real issue, so keep promoting and iterating after launch.
- Hiring a large team early; chemistry matters more than headcount.
- Fearing Lua and delaying building, when Lua is relatively easy to learn.
- Neglecting account security; change passwords often because breaches are common and can end income.
- Building per-platform versions instead of publishing once, when only minor UI/UX changes are needed (except Xbox).
- Trying to self-host infrastructure, when Roblox handles servers, storage, moderation and legal.

## Launch checklist
- [ ] Core loop is fun and engaging before any monetisation is added
- [ ] Shared vision and a clear idea that creators would want to cover
- [ ] Small connected team with defined roles
- [ ] Publish once to all platforms; check minor UI/UX per platform (not Xbox)
- [ ] Game passes shown in the main menu so players see what they can unlock
- [ ] Monetisation avoids pay-to-win and does not split players visibly where it harms fairness
- [ ] Account security: strong unique password, changed regularly
- [ ] Tracking set up for engagement hours and return visits
- [ ] Plan for post-launch promotion; launch is not the finish line
- [ ] Creator and YouTuber outreach prepared for novel ideas
- [ ] Accelerator-program and Roblox group networks used for feedback and help
- [ ] Trusted team members assigned to live maintenance after release
- [ ] Verify current Roblox revenue split and Premium Payouts terms before relying on 2020-2021 figures
