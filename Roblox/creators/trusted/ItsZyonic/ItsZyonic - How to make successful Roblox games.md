# ItsZyonic - How to make successful Roblox games

Make a game that is fun to play and holds players, then get the first players cheaply through short social posts and YouTuber playtests. Engagement and retention drive Roblox's recommendation and search, and friends joining from the game drive word-of-mouth growth. Paid ads, sponsors, and cash-grab monetisation were unreliable in this creator's tests. The most recent case (RIWPfMJyVhQ) grew organically after the ads were turned off. Start from a mechanic you find fun, prototype and playtest it constantly, add monetisation and progression only after the core loop works, and ship updates daily.

**Style:** Practical, first-person, and experiment-driven: the creator mixes entertainment challenges and stunts with real case data. Strongest on mindset and the "fun first, then growth" sequence. Good for solo or part-time developers who can build a prototype quickly. Less useful for detailed scripting or monetisation specifics.

## Playbook
1. **1. Idea: pick a mechanic you find fun** [[game-ideas-and-genres]]: Build from a mechanic you personally enjoy, then test whether it holds up. The Dodger Dot case (RIWPfMJyVhQ) started from dodging Align Position balls and was prototyped before anything else was added.
2. **2. Build: lock movement and the round loop first** [[core-loops-and-game-design]]: Establish a smooth movement system and a working round loop before anything else. Add long-term progression such as upgrades, crates, and tools only after the loop is fun (RIWPfMJyVhQ).
3. **3. Build: playtest every stage with outsiders** [[playtesting-and-feedback]]: Playtest each development step, even unpolished versions, with Discord fans and other developers. Announce a live-server join link to a few players and watch them play to find breaks quickly. Keep listening to feedback even when the dislike ratio is poor.
4. **4. Build: keep performance and assets light** [[building-art-and-assets]]: Part size has a hard cap, so build large maps by duplicating parts instead of scaling one baseplate. Max-scaled characters and oversized assets made Studio laggy (C1wSHlu8vVM). Dress maps with Toolbox models scaled to fit, and raise rendering settings only as needed.
5. **5. Build: debounce action remotes** [[scripting-and-performance]]: Add a debounce to slap, ability, and other remote-fired actions so they cannot be spammed (C1wSHlu8vVM).
6. **6. Build: add monetisation after the loop** [[monetisation-gamepasses]]: Add gamepasses and developer products only after the core loop and progression are in place. Avoid gamepass-spam designs; the low-effort cash grab earned 28 Robux in 72 hours (Cd6mpD4doGs).
7. **7. Launch: name for a searchable term** [[titles-descriptions]]: Name the game to target a highly searched term with low competition, since search can bring a large number of players (sample tip, titles-descriptions).
8. **8. Launch: test the icon for clicks** [[thumbnails-icons]]: Test icon and thumbnail variants because they directly affect click-through. A clickbait icon produced about 1.5% CTR after roughly one day of ads (b7qEitHbhUA).
9. **9. Launch: seed initial players with short social posts** [[social-features-and-virality]]: Post short TikToks that take under five minutes to make, on a consistent schedule. TikTok is the preferred channel, with YouTube Shorts as a secondary outlet (ODh2jPEGWUc). Get YouTubers to play and video the game.
10. **10. Growth: fix retention before scaling traffic** [[retention-d1-d7]]: Keeping players in the game longer and bringing them back is the most important part of the game. Roblox pushes games with good engagement and retention into recommended and search, though exact rules are unclear (ODh2jPEGWUc, RIWPfMJyVhQ). Fix this before buying acquisition.
11. **11. Growth: design for friend-driven spread** [[social-features-and-virality]]: Keep players in the game long enough that friends see them online and join. Word-of-mouth from friends joining was the closing growth mechanism in ODh2jPEGWUc.
12. **12. Growth: treat paid ads as optional and test small** [[ads-and-sponsored-experiences]]: Roblox ads need at least 2,800 Robux per game (b7qEitHbhUA). If running sponsors, set age targeting to 17+ and verify the audience, since one sponsored audience skewed under 18 (ODh2jPEGWUc). Test sponsored experience against portal ads on a small budget before scaling. In the latest case, ads underperformed and were turned off (RIWPfMJyVhQ).
13. **13. Growth: check like ratio and session time** [[analytics-metrics]]: Check the like-to-dislike ratio and average session time in the analytics overview. A 33% like ratio and 3-minute average session signalled weak engagement and a failed cash grab (Cd6mpD4doGs).
14. **14. Live ops: ship updates daily from a list, then expand** [[updates-live-ops]]: Keep shipping updates daily from a running list of improvements; a released game will not take off on its own. Once momentum returns, feed it with a battle pass, clans, more tools, and cosmetics (RIWPfMJyVhQ).

## Key numbers
- Sponsor age targeting: 17+ (ODh2jPEGWUc, 2023 recommended setting)
- Roblox ad minimum: 2,800 Robux per game (b7qEitHbhUA, 2024)
- Clickbait icon ad CTR: ~1.5% after about one day (b7qEitHbhUA)
- Cash grab: 3,000 Robux sponsored over 2 days, 28 Robux revenue in 72 hours, ~25 CCU at one hour (Cd6mpD4doGs)
- Cash grab engagement: 33% like ratio, 3-minute average session (Cd6mpD4doGs)
- Freelance builds: $10 game delivered by day 7 after a 3-day check-in; $100 game took about 3 weeks (INsV8HkDHIA)
- Visit challenge: creator ended at 1,500 visits and was ~100 visits behind after day one (b7qEitHbhUA)
- Dodger Dot first ad campaign: 280 credits over ~7 days, ~50 average CCU during ads (RIWPfMJyVhQ)
- Dodger Dot organic growth: 900 peak CCU on a weekend, dipped to 300 CCU, then ~5,000 CCU about 2 months post-release (RIWPfMJyVhQ)
- Dodger Dot peak: over 7,000 CCU last weekend and 20 million total visits (RIWPfMJyVhQ)
- Dodger Dot buyout offer: $24,000 for 70% of the game, declined (RIWPfMJyVhQ)
- Dodger Dot development time: about one month, part-time (RIWPfMJyVhQ)
- Friend-made bonus game on a $1 budget drew about 40 players (INsV8HkDHIA)

## Common mistakes
- Relying on Roblox ads or sponsors as the growth engine; in the latest case ads underperformed and were turned off (RIWPfMJyVhQ).
- Running sponsors without verifying the audience; age targeting skewed under 18 and many players lied about their age (ODh2jPEGWUc).
- Stress-testing extreme scale late: max-scaled characters and huge baseplates caused rendering failures and Studio pauses (C1wSHlu8vVM).
- Paying unverified third-party promo services (streamer or Discord outreach, fake-view TikTok promos); they produced almost no results (b7qEitHbhUA).
- Low-effort cash grab with gamepass spam and sponsored traffic; 3,000 Robux of ads returned 28 Robux (Cd6mpD4doGs).
- Selling or cashing out during a mid-growth dip; the creator declined a $24,000 offer and CCU fell to 300 before recovering. This is one anecdote, not a general rule (RIWPfMJyVhQ).
- Shipping remote-fired actions (slap, ability) without a debounce, so they can be spammed (C1wSHlu8vVM).
- Ignoring like ratio and average session time while chasing traffic (Cd6mpD4doGs).
- Expecting a released game to grow on its own without a daily update cadence (RIWPfMJyVhQ).

## Launch checklist
- [ ] Core mechanic is fun on its own and has been prototyped and tested
- [ ] Smooth movement system and working round loop are in place
- [ ] Progression (upgrades, crates, tools) added only after the loop works
- [ ] Monetisation (gamepasses, dev products) added after the loop, not as spam
- [ ] Live-server join link sent to a few players and their session watched for breaks
- [ ] Playtest feedback collected from Discord fans and other developers at each stage
- [ ] Large maps built by duplicating parts; no single oversized baseplate
- [ ] Rendering settings kept at the minimum needed; assets kept light
- [ ] Remote-fired actions debounced
- [ ] Game name targets a highly searched, low-competition term
- [ ] At least two icon or thumbnail variants prepared and tested for clicks
- [ ] Short TikTok or Shorts plan ready, with clips under five minutes
- [ ] YouTuber playtest outreach planned
- [ ] Retention checked before any paid traffic
- [ ] Sponsor age targeting set to 17+ if sponsors are used, with audience verified
- [ ] Small ad test budget planned before any scaling
- [ ] Analytics checked for like ratio and average session time
- [ ] Daily update list maintained; avoid paid promo services that are unverified

## How the advice changed over time
- 2023 (ODh2jPEGWUc): Called Roblox ads and sponsors inefficient for small developers; recommended sponsors with 17+ targeting and social posts as the main launch channel.
- 2024 (b7qEitHbhUA, Cd6mpD4doGs): Tested paid Roblox ads (2,800 Robux minimum) and a sponsored cash grab; both underperformed, shifting emphasis to polish, effort, and retention.
- 2026 (RIWPfMJyVhQ, most recent): Ads underperformed (280 credits, ~50 CCU), were turned off, and organic growth followed. This supersedes the earlier sponsor-first framing: do not depend on paid ads.
- Across versions, the launch channel (short social posts) stayed constant, while the 2026 advice frames friend word-of-mouth and retention as the main growth engine.
