# Your First Roblox Game Should Be Bad

[Watch](https://youtu.be/wG9XzLnJC5k) | ArTheDev | 2,002 views | 2026-06-07

## Summary
Argues that a first Roblox game should be small, unpolished and finished rather than an ambitious dream project. Failure and repetition build skills, finishing teaches more than abandoned big projects, and the goal of the first game is learning, not money or virality. Includes a channel plug for a Cookie Run-style anime game and Discord.

## Tips
- [0:56](https://youtu.be/wG9XzLnJC5k?t=56) **[[mindset-and-strategy]]** Don't make your dream game first; expectations far above your skill level make early development painful and lead to quitting.
- [2:43](https://youtu.be/wG9XzLnJC5k?t=163) **[[development-process-and-speed]]** Finish and publish small games; a tiny finished game with 12 players teaches more than a massive unfinished one.
- [2:43](https://youtu.be/wG9XzLnJC5k?t=163) **[[development-process-and-speed]]** Pick a game you can finish, e.g. a tiny horror game, simple tycoon, physics toy, or obby with one unique mechanic.
- [4:08](https://youtu.be/wG9XzLnJC5k?t=248) **[[mindset-and-strategy]]** Treat the first game as training: its job is to teach you, not to make money or go viral.
- [2:13](https://youtu.be/wG9XzLnJC5k?t=133) **[[mindset-and-strategy]]** Build repeatedly instead of planning or watching tutorials; repetition is what stops you Googling every problem.
- [4:08](https://youtu.be/wG9XzLnJC5k?t=248) **[[development-process-and-speed]]** Ask 'what game can I actually finish?' rather than 'what game will succeed?'
- [1:42](https://youtu.be/wG9XzLnJC5k?t=102) **[[common-mistakes]]** Don't avoid small projects because they seem unimpressive; most beginners never ship anything at all.
- [2:13](https://youtu.be/wG9XzLnJC5k?t=133) **[[scripting-and-performance]]** Learn data handling carefully early on, since doing it wrong can completely break your game.
