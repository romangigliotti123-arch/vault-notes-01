# deconstructoroffun - How to make successful Roblox games

Build a small, bite-size MVP fast (5-6 weeks, 2-4 people), launch with console and cross-device friend play, then run weekly live ops driven by player data. Win on core loop and social hook, not visuals. Track six benchmarks (playtime, D1, D7, payer conversion, ARPPU) toward the 90th percentile. Grow through in-platform discovery, friend invites and creators. Treat ads as immature and unreliable. Expect most projects to fail, so release often and learn.

**Style:** Commentary-driven and analytical. Mixes industry and stock analysis of Roblox platform changes with interviews of developers and executives. Strong on economics, small-team shipping cadence and expectation-setting. Suits small teams and solo developers weighing ads, DevEx payouts and fast release cycles. Light on hands-on tutorial detail.

## Playbook
1. **Pick a simple core loop with a social hook** [[game-ideas-and-genres]]: Do not chase only high-fidelity visuals. Breakthrough hits can be simple if the core loop, gameplay and social hook are strong. (v_PNeLRjewQns)
2. **Approach Roblox as a beginner** [[mindset-and-strategy]]: Treat it as a new platform regardless of past success; past hits do not guarantee the next. Expect a couple dozen failures per success and release often. (v_PNeLRjewQns)
3. **Deliver the core loop in the first minutes** [[first-session-onboarding]]: Short-form traffic gives only seconds of attention. Show what is special immediately and get players into the core loop within the first minutes.
4. **Keep the team small** [[team-and-budget]]: Survive Bikini Bottom: 5 weeks with 2 people. SpongeBob TD: 6 weeks with 4 people. Never more than about 4 per project, roughly 3 projects at once. The 10-20 person studio size is a speculative guess. (v_PNeLRjewQns)
5. **Ship a bite-size MVP in 5-6 weeks** [[development-process-and-speed]]: Replaces the older 3-4 month cycle. Iterate many times a day: Survive Bikini Bottom shipped 10 builds in 3 days with no app-store review step. (v_PNeLRjewQns)
6. **Launch with console support** [[common-mistakes]]: Wonder Works regretted dropping console at release, because players expect cross-device friend connection. (v_PNeLRjewQns)
7. **Instrument the six benchmarks** [[analytics-metrics]]: Track average playtime per user per day, D1 and D7 retention, payer conversion and ARPPU. Aim for the 90th percentile on each. (v_PNeLRjewQns)
8. **Prioritise in-platform SEO and discoverability** [[discovery-algorithm]]: Influencer traction, Discord growth and live-op attention follow from discovery. Roblox does not prioritise playtime beyond a threshold, so design healthy sessions rather than time sinks. (v_PNeLRjewQns)
9. **Build friend-invite co-play** [[social-features-and-virality]]: Give in-game sharing tools and milestone rewards for week-one friend invites, plus bonus currency for group play, without punishing solo players. Moments only helps if it is part of the core loop. (v_LRyThu3bFyg)
10. **Run an influencer program** [[community-and-discord]]: Give creators early update access and gamepass resources or codes so they publish videos the day an update drops. Discord growth follows from this traction. (v_PNeLRjewQns)
11. **Use paid traffic to seed the algorithm** [[ads-and-sponsored-experiences]]: Buy traffic to find your audience and let engagement data feed the algorithm. Paid advertising can re-kick a stale game after big updates. Rewarded video needs 100k DAU and an 18+ developer. (v_0RADBCTlw, v_LRyThu3bFyg)
12. **Run a weekly live-ops cadence** [[updates-live-ops]]: Sprint Monday, ship Friday, with updates driven by community feedback. SpongeBob TD ran 52 weekly updates with 200+ towers and 6-7 new modes. A hyper-casual title updated only once or twice still reached ~100M visits. (v_PNeLRjewQns)
13. **Set realistic payout expectations** [[business-and-payouts]]: DevEx rate rose 8.5%, but developers keep only ~21% after fees. About 29,000 of 3.1M developers cashed out in a year (~1%). Ad share of revenue ranges 0-50% by game. (v_LRyThu3bFyg, v_PNeLRjewQns)

## Common mistakes
- Dropping console support at launch; players expect cross-device friend connection (v_PNeLRjewQns)
- Chasing high-fidelity visuals over a strong core loop and social hook (v_PNeLRjewQns)
- Treating ad revenue as a reliable income stream; ads are immature and eligibility is gated (v_PNeLRjewQns, v_0RADBCTlw)
- Overestimating ad revenue, as with the Candy Crush projection versus actual (v_0RADBCTlw)
- Designing for addictive time sinks; the algorithm does not reward playtime beyond a threshold (v_PNeLRjewQns)
- Treating Moments or shareable clips as ad slots rather than core-loop value (v_LRyThu3bFyg)
- Spending 3-4 months on a build before launch instead of shipping an MVP (v_PNeLRjewQns)
- Growing teams past about 4 per project (v_PNeLRjewQns)
- Assuming past success guarantees the next hit (v_PNeLRjewQns)
- Assuming ad revenue scales with MAU; a developer claimed five-figure ad revenue on ~100M MAU (v_PNeLRjewQns)

## Launch checklist
- [ ] Core loop reachable within the first minutes of play
- [ ] Console support included at launch
- [ ] Cross-device friend connection working
- [ ] In-game share and friend-invite tools in place
- [ ] Week-one milestone rewards for friend invites
- [ ] Bonus currency for group play that does not punish solo players
- [ ] Six benchmarks instrumented: playtime/day, D1, D7, payer conversion, ARPPU
- [ ] Weekly update schedule set (sprint Monday, ship Friday)
- [ ] Discord server ready for community feedback
- [ ] Influencer kit ready: early update access, gamepass codes
- [ ] Team no more than about 4 people per project
- [ ] Developer account 18+ before any ad integration
- [ ] Check 100k DAU before applying for rewarded video
- [ ] Confirm payout economics: developers keep ~21% after fees
- [ ] Paid traffic budget set aside to seed the algorithm
- [ ] Session design checked for healthy play, not time sinks
- [ ] Plan for re-kicking the game with paid ads after big updates
- [ ] Do not rely on ad revenue as a core income assumption
