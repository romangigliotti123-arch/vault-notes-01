# 1Coal - How to make successful Roblox games

Build a small, simple game you can finish in weeks, based on a proven trend or a real-life love/hate. Fix presentation before spending money: a distinctive icon and title, clear UI, a progress bar, and timed rewards. Test icons with small ad budgets and benchmark CTR. Then push early players through external virality (TikTok, trends) so Roblox's recommendation system takes over. Ads alone plateau.

**Style:** Practical, teardown-driven advice from a developer who reviews struggling games and reports measured results. Favors fast prototypes, cheap icon A/B tests, and concrete UI/icon fixes over big builds. Suits small teams and solo devs with limited budgets who want to revive stalled games.

## Playbook
1. **Find a proven concept** [[trend-research-and-validation]]: Sort popular gaming YouTube videos by views, check App Store trending and Fortnite's Browse tab, and watch real-life trends (e.g. fidget spinners). Build a similar game quickly. Also draw on things people love or hate (school, dentist, hospitals) and childhood recess games.
2. **Keep the idea simple and scoped** [[game-ideas-and-genres]]: Explain the game in one or two sentences. Start with an obby or tycoon you can finish in a few weeks. Avoid ambitious clones of big games. Leave room for player choice and self-made stories such as customizable houses or roleplay jobs.
3. **Prototype fast and playtest hard** [[playtesting-and-feedback]]: Get a working prototype out quickly and judge fun by playtesting, not by the empty base plate. Log many small fixes (visible damage numbers, shorter rounds, 10-second countdown label). Apply the Xbox test: would a kid's friends keep watching instead of scrolling TikTok?
4. **Add stakes and clear core loop** [[core-loops-and-game-design]]: Give the core loop real stakes such as losing loot or dying. Label boost items as part of the core system so players see how to improve their run. Give the hunted side a 3-second head start in asymmetric rounds.
5. **Make rules obvious in the first session** [[first-session-onboarding]]: If new players are confused about what to do or what just happened, add small in-game cues that explain the action.
6. **Clean up the UI** [[ui-ux-design]]: Replace text progress with a large progress bar. Make side panels collapsible or smaller so gameplay fills the screen. Add a live player-progress tracker showing other players on the server for competition (as Tower of Hell does).
7. **Differentiate stages visually** [[building-art-and-assets]]: Give each stage a distinct color instead of uniform gray or sand to reduce boredom.
8. **Design the icon and title** [[thumbnails-icons]]: Make the icon show the core mechanic in a fun, distinctive way, using blue and green (the speaker says these tend to get higher CTR). Choose a unique, intriguing title rather than generic names like 'Prison Obby' or 'House Tycoon'.
9. **A/B test icons with small ad budgets** [[ab-testing-and-experiments]]: Order several icons and test them with about $20 of Roblox ads. Benchmark CTR: above 3% is good, above 4% is excellent. Ship the winner. Rewrite the description shorter, keeping only what a player needs to decide to click.
10. **Set the genre category deliberately** [[discovery-algorithm]]: Pick the genre category on purpose. If the game grows, Roblox can feature it in that genre's front-page section.
11. **Drive an early player spike from outside Roblox** [[social-features-and-virality]]: Pay a TikTok creator (about $400 in the 2024 example). Early spikes from external traffic give the recommendation algorithm enough engagement data to start recommending the game. Pair paid ads with organic virality, since ads alone plateaued at about $50/day.
12. **Add timed rewards for retention** [[retention-d1-d7]]: Give timed free rewards (e.g. a boost at 3, 6, and 9 minutes) so longer play yields better rewards. Boosts then feel more valuable than a 30-second timer.
13. **Monetise at the right moments** [[monetisation-devproducts]]: Prompt a gamepass purchase when the player runs out of a resource (e.g. 2x drawing power when fuel runs out). In obbies, offer a skip-stage button. Add a time-limited starter pack (79 Robux, 15-minute window); revenue roughly doubled to tripled.
14. **Prune the shop and iterate live** [[updates-live-ops]]: Cut shop items players will not buy and re-price the rest, since a bloated shop dilutes purchases. Keep shipping updates driven by playtest feedback and the metrics from each change.

## Common mistakes
- Cloning big games (e.g. a full ambitious clone) before you have a few hundred consistent players; these take years and large budgets (YBSsksqfxo0)
- Making a game that looks unfun in its icon and thumbnail; fail the Xbox test and kids keep scrolling (YBSsksqfxo0, JzB8fpyofnw)
- Generic titles and icons like 'Prison Obby' or 'House Tycoon' that copy other games (gWNOANEPYLY)
- Overcomplicated concepts that are hard to explain in one or two sentences (YBSsksqfxo0)
- Relying on paid ads alone; spend plateaus without organic virality (gWNOANEPYLY)
- Uniform gray or sand stages that bore players (JzB8fpyofnw)
- Judging a prototype on an empty base plate; nearly shelved a project that needed playtesting (gWNOANEPYLY)
- Bloated shop with items players won't buy, which dilutes purchases (JzB8fpyofnw)
- Hiding boosts or rules so players don't understand what happened (JzB8fpyofnw)
- Long, cluttered descriptions and side panels that hide the gameplay (JzB8fpyofnw)

## Launch checklist
- [ ] Concept is a trend, real-life love/hate, or recess game, explainable in one or two sentences
- [ ] Finishable scope (obby or tycoon, a few weeks)
- [ ] Stakes present (loot loss or death) in the core loop
- [ ] Boosts labeled as part of the core system
- [ ] Onboarding cues for rules and outcomes
- [ ] Large progress bar; collapsible or small side panels
- [ ] Distinct color per stage
- [ ] Progress tracker or competition element on the server
- [ ] Icon shows core mechanic, uses blue and green
- [ ] Unique, non-generic title
- [ ] Short description that gets the click
- [ ] Icon A/B tested with about $20 of ads; winner above 3% CTR (target above 4%)
- [ ] Genre category set deliberately
- [ ] Timed free rewards at 3, 6, and 9 minutes
- [ ] Gamepass prompt at resource depletion and obby skip-stage button
- [ ] Time-limited starter pack (79 Robux, 15 minutes)
- [ ] Shop pruned to items that sell
- [ ] Plan for an external traffic push (creator video) to start the recommendation loop

## How the advice changed over time
- 2023 advice centered on organic fixes (UI cleanup, icon and thumbnail color, progress bars, timed rewards) and reached ~2,100 CCU with no ad spend (JzB8fpyofnw).
- 2024 advice adds paid icon testing and external virality. It says ads alone plateau (about $50/day), so pair them with a TikTok push (gWNOANEPYLY). This is an extension, not a reversal.
- 2023 framed a few hundred consistent players as the milestone before ambitious ideas (YBSsksqfxo0). Later content treats the first viral spike as the way to break a stall. Both can hold: build small first, then push growth.
- Note: ad spend is quoted in Robux in one place (10,000 Robux/day) and in USD in another ($50/day). The units differ across videos, so compare with care.
