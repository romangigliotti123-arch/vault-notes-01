# Developer Secrets to Amazing Roblox Building

[Watch](https://youtu.be/QRwTkEPPChM) | GnomeCode | 30,133 views | 2023-03-11

## Summary
Nemco's video on Roblox building fundamentals: use reference images for proportions, design map flow with multiple routes and avoid dead ends, keep workspace organized with folders and naming, break up flat surfaces with added detail, and use restrained color palettes with contrast.

## Tips
- [1:31](https://youtu.be/QRwTkEPPChM?t=91) **[[building-and-level-design]]** Gather reference images before building, especially for real-world objects; it fixes proportions and catches details like keypad order that you miss from memory.
- [2:51](https://youtu.be/QRwTkEPPChM?t=171) **[[building-and-level-design]]** Prioritise how players move through space over lighting; good flow improves gameplay more than pretty visuals.
- [3:32](https://youtu.be/QRwTkEPPChM?t=212) **[[building-and-level-design]]** In PvP maps, add lanes, crossroads and elevated walkways so players have multiple routes and sightlines, instead of an open field or a single wall.
- [6:17](https://youtu.be/QRwTkEPPChM?t=377) **[[building-and-level-design]]** Sketch the layout on paper before entering Studio to clarify the map plan.
- [6:17](https://youtu.be/QRwTkEPPChM?t=377) **[[building-and-level-design]]** Give every area multiple exits and avoid long dead ends, while allowing a few so monsters can corner players in chase games.
- [6:57](https://youtu.be/QRwTkEPPChM?t=417) **[[building-and-level-design]]** Test the map's route topology from a top view; a single circular loop forces a full lap to escape a chaser and hurts gameplay even if the map looks good.
- [7:39](https://youtu.be/QRwTkEPPChM?t=459) **[[building-and-level-design]]** Rotate buildings by a slight angle off the grid to add visual variety; use Ctrl+X to toggle local-axis movement for precise placement.
- [8:37](https://youtu.be/QRwTkEPPChM?t=517) **[[development-process-and-speed]]** Tidy the workspace at the end of each build session using folders, groups and consistent naming; messy workspaces greatly slow development and collaboration.
- [11:01](https://youtu.be/QRwTkEPPChM?t=661) **[[building-art-and-assets]]** Avoid long flat wall surfaces; break them up with blocks, trim, columns, and small offset parts, which can triple visual detail with very few parts.
- [11:44](https://youtu.be/QRwTkEPPChM?t=704) **[[building-art-and-assets]]** Do not rely on a single texture like brick; add subtle real-world details such as slab supports, contrasting courses and skirting boards.
- [13:50](https://youtu.be/QRwTkEPPChM?t=830) **[[building-art-and-assets]]** Build colour palettes from two or three colours; take a base colour and slightly darken or lighten it for contrast rather than using clashing brights.
- [15:59](https://youtu.be/QRwTkEPPChM?t=959) **[[building-art-and-assets]]** For a cartoony look, lower saturation one step from pure neon to avoid a garish, chaotic result; desaturate further for realistic or post-apocalyptic themes.
- [17:22](https://youtu.be/QRwTkEPPChM?t=1042) **[[building-art-and-assets]]** Vary palette and lighting per map area to give each zone a distinct mood, as the Wild West map does with warm and swampy zones.
