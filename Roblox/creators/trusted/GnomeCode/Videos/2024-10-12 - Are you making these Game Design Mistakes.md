# Are you making these Game Design Mistakes?

[Watch](https://youtu.be/H9CbgCe47Mo) | GnomeCode | 12,184 views | 2024-10-12

## Summary
Game-jam review of common Roblox game design mistakes: difficulty vs engagement, long loading and story-heavy intros, cliched concepts, overly dark horror, flashlight and shadow tricks, unique mechanics, and disabling spawn force fields.

## Tips
- [1:31](https://youtu.be/H9CbgCe47Mo?t=91) **[[core-loops-and-game-design]]** Don't over-worry about difficulty; keep players engaged with next milestones instead, as in Pet Simulator, since too-hard moments make players leave.
- [2:13](https://youtu.be/H9CbgCe47Mo?t=133) **[[first-session-onboarding]]** Drop players into the action immediately; avoid long cutscenes that can't be skipped, especially before first gameplay.
- [3:34](https://youtu.be/H9CbgCe47Mo?t=214) **[[first-session-onboarding]]** If load-in takes more than about 5-10 seconds on desktop, players may leave; stream assets rather than using a heavy loading screen.
- [4:15](https://youtu.be/H9CbgCe47Mo?t=255) **[[first-session-onboarding]]** Get gameplay in early; use interactive tutorials or exposition during play (e.g., an NPC guiding the player) rather than long text-based story.
- [4:46](https://youtu.be/H9CbgCe47Mo?t=286) **[[game-ideas-and-genres]]** Avoid cliched openings like waking up to an alarm clock; if you use a common idea, twist it into something novel.
- [6:07](https://youtu.be/H9CbgCe47Mo?t=367) **[[game-ideas-and-genres]]** Reusing a popular mechanic is fine if you differentiate via story, feel, level design, visuals, or an iconic IP angle (e.g., a teddy bear skin on the piggy/granny mechanic).
- [7:20](https://youtu.be/H9CbgCe47Mo?t=440) **[[genre-mechanics]]** In horror, avoid large areas of pure darkness; it reads as frustrating on a 2D screen rather than scary.
- [8:02](https://youtu.be/H9CbgCe47Mo?t=482) **[[genre-mechanics]]** Only give a flashlight if needed, and make it aim freely vertically; don't lock it to view height.
- [8:42](https://youtu.be/H9CbgCe47Mo?t=522) **[[building-and-level-design]]** Place many objects in rooms to cast shadows; this creates a sense of space and unknown danger better than empty darkness.
- [9:45](https://youtu.be/H9CbgCe47Mo?t=585) **[[game-ideas-and-genres]]** Seed of an interesting idea matters in jam-scale games; crazy or unusual mechanics can be fun even when rough.
- [10:30](https://youtu.be/H9CbgCe47Mo?t=630) **[[ui-ux-design]]** Disable the spawn location's force field; the default glittering shield breaks immersion, especially in first-person.
- [11:01](https://youtu.be/H9CbgCe47Mo?t=661) **[[core-loops-and-game-design]]** Ensure real gameplay exists early and is fun; a basic obby with more gameplay beats a flashy game with little to do.

## Games
- **Teddy** [0:49](https://youtu.be/H9CbgCe47Mo?t=49): Clicking play drops players straight into a round with no waiting for the previous round to end.
- **Pet Simulator** [1:31](https://youtu.be/H9CbgCe47Mo?t=91): Little difficulty curve but strong engagement via constant next milestones.
- **GTA / The Witcher** [0:49](https://youtu.be/H9CbgCe47Mo?t=49): Popular games would stay fun even with one-hit-kill enemies; story and world carry engagement.
- **Piggy / Granny / Puppet games** [6:48](https://youtu.be/H9CbgCe47Mo?t=408): Share a mechanic with Teddy; differentiation via IP and visuals.
- **Five Nights at Freddy's clones** [5:26](https://youtu.be/H9CbgCe47Mo?t=326): Multiple versions exist; need iconic look or novel twist to stand out.
