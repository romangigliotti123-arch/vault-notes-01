# callmedogey - How to make successful Roblox games

Build a simple, playable core loop fast using a plugin base and free assets, then monetise with developer products placed at spawn, from cheap to about 10,000 Robux. Keep the game and its ownership: never trade equity for advice, ads, or thumbnails, because studios often take 20-50% and don't deliver. After launch, check the six Creator Hub benchmarks and fix any below the 75th percentile before spending on ads. Ads were a small early boost but are now ranked below benchmark fixes. Update carefully and test before pushing. Cash out only what you need through DevEx.

**Style:** Solo, low-budget operator who publishes real revenue numbers and his own mistakes. Favors fast, scoped builds from free assets, developer-product monetisation, and keeping full ownership. Suits small devs making simple clicker or simulator games who want control over their studio deals and growth decisions.

## Playbook
1. **Pick a simple, proven loop** [[game-ideas-and-genres]]: Use a clicker or simulator loop: click for money, buy pets, unlock new zones. LeBron Clicker was built on this (USJC5l_wpuY). Keep the loop playable before polishing. Use cheap settings such as low gravity and floating platforms to give a distinct theme (rHyiN2GV-J0).
2. **Study proven hits before committing** [[trend-research-and-validation]]: Look at high-player games in your genre and compare their approach. Dead Rails was remade in 7 days with a distinct space theme (rHyiN2GV-J0). Validate that the idea has a player base before investing weeks in it.
3. **Generate a base with plugins and free assets** [[ai-and-tools]]: A Simulator Generator-style plugin produced a playable clicker base with no coding. Free Toolbox assets carried a map that was about 99% free assets (USJC5l_wpuY). Plan to replace free models with custom assets during post-release refinement.
4. **Split roles and ship to a deadline** [[development-process-and-speed]]: A builder makes environments and lobby while a coder builds systems in parallel, which enabled daily progress (rHyiN2GV-J0). Set a fixed deadline, ship a scoped-down playable version, accept rough systems, and iterate from player feedback.
5. **Fast start and fair deaths** [[first-session-onboarding]]: If playtime is low, make the first minutes quick with a fun loop. Add revive stations and a checkpoint-style Outpost, placed every 10,000 studs in the Dead Rails remake, so falls are not punishing and players stay in the game (rHyiN2GV-J0).
6. **Add progression if D1 or D7 is weak** [[retention-d1-d7]]: If D1 or D7 retention is low, add progression and daily rewards. Retention work comes after the start is fun, so fix the opening session first.
7. **Place cheap dev products at spawn** [[monetisation-devproducts]]: Put a cheap-to-expensive pet product, such as a 300 Robux pet, right at spawn so players see it immediately. This drove most repeat purchases. Developer products earned 1.9M Robux vs 841k for game passes (USJC5l_wpuY).
8. **Price across spending tiers** [[pricing]]: Offer dev products from low cost up to about 10,000 Robux so both low and high spenders have an option. The top 10,000 Robux product was bought by 9 people, so high tiers are niche but valid (USJC5l_wpuY).
9. **Game passes and Premium without pay-to-win** [[monetisation-gamepasses]]: Do not add pay-to-win because an outside party suggests it (ZAfE-Row9tU). If conversion is low, make purchases visible and effective. If ARPU is low, add more expensive, worthwhile purchases. Premium payouts still add income from non-spenders, about $1,500 (USJC5l_wpuY).
10. **Fix thumbnails by CTR and QPTR** [[thumbnails-icons]]: Target a 0.7% click-through rate as a small dev (ZAfE-Row9tU). If qualified playthrough rate is low, make new thumbnails: cut object images, remove backgrounds, add a stroke in a free editor. Hire an artist or do it yourself, and check moderation before uploading.
11. **Check the six benchmarks first** [[analytics-metrics]]: Review playtime, D1/D7 retention, conversion, ARPU, and QPTR in Creator Hub. Improve any stat below the 75th percentile. The speaker claims this is enough for algorithm promotion (IQm6zE2fKUI). Treat this as the priority over ads.
12. **Use ads as a small reinvestment test** [[ads-and-sponsored-experiences]]: About $40 over two months brought roughly 9,000 visits, and reinvested Robux funded ads (USJC5l_wpuY). Newer advice ranks ads among the least important steps (IQm6zE2fKUI), so spend only after benchmarks are fixed.
13. **Update carefully and test first** [[updates-live-ops]]: Bugs after updates caused outages, and one update kept the creator up overnight fixing them. Test each update before pushing. Get feedback from players who spend real time in the game, and be skeptical of players who only play 2 minutes (ZAfE-Row9tU).
14. **Cash out and keep ownership** [[business-and-payouts]]: Check the DevEx page; if the amount shows less than paid out, wait up to 72 hours. Use the RoMonitor calculator: 2M Robux is about $7,000 gross (Cn_m9tamq4g). Keep games rather than selling them; small unupdated games earned $30-$50/day (ZAfE-Row9tU).

## Common mistakes
- Selling a percentage of your game or giving equity in exchange for advice, thumbnails, or ads (ZAfE-Row9tU, IQm6zE2fKUI).
- Adding pay-to-win monetisation because an outside party suggested it; it can disappoint players later (ZAfE-Row9tU).
- Judging the game from feedback by players who stayed only 2 minutes (ZAfE-Row9tU).
- Paying an editor upfront without vetting; the creator lost $70 and the upload slipped over a month (rHyiN2GV-J0).
- Uploading a thumbnail without checking moderation; a rejected image got the thumbnail removed and a one-day ban.
- Pushing updates without testing; bugs caused outages and overnight fixes.
- Spending on ads before fixing benchmarks below the 75th percentile.
- Making big decisions about the game early in its life before understanding its potential.
- Cashing out everything when Robux could fund ads for other games (about 2.3M Robux was exchanged for about $8,000 total).

## Launch checklist
- [ ] Build a playable core loop first (click, buy, unlock) before polishing.
- [ ] Use a plugin base and free assets to get to a playable build quickly.
- [ ] Place the cheapest pet dev product at spawn.
- [ ] Price dev products across tiers up to about 10,000 Robux.
- [ ] Keep monetisation fair; no pay-to-win additions from outside advice.
- [ ] Make the first minutes fast and fun to protect playtime.
- [ ] Add revive stations and checkpoints so deaths are not punishing.
- [ ] Create thumbnails and check moderation before uploading.
- [ ] Vet any editor or contractor and pay after delivery, not upfront.
- [ ] Test every update before pushing live.
- [ ] Set up the DevEx account with the same password as the Roblox account.
- [ ] Check the DevEx page before cashing out; allow up to 72 hours for Robux to become eligible.
- [ ] Review the six Creator Hub benchmarks and list any below the 75th percentile.
- [ ] Hold ad spend until benchmarks are improved, then run a small test.
- [ ] Get feedback from players who spend significant time in the game.
- [ ] Do not give equity or revenue share to studios for advice or ads.
- [ ] Wait before making big decisions about a new game's future.
- [ ] Keep the game long-term rather than selling it.
