## Summary
Short Roblox simulator-design video covering six ideas: playtime rewards, anchor pricing (showing an expensive gamepass first), rabbit-hole discoverability, small and big goals, satisfying feedback, and replayability. Uses Pet Simulator X/99 as examples and the creator's own gift-reward system.

## Tips
- [0:00](https://youtu.be/v8tFfBTr6M8?t=0) **[[retention-d1-d7]]** Add playtime rewards (e.g. a gift claimable every 5 minutes of play, growing in value) to give players a reason to stay longer.
- [1:36](https://youtu.be/v8tFfBTr6M8?t=96) **[[pricing]]** Show a very expensive gamepass (e.g. 900 Robux) as soon as players join so cheaper items (100-200 Robux) look like good deals.
- [3:27](https://youtu.be/v8tFfBTr6M8?t=207) **[[core-loops-and-game-design]]** Keep the core game simple for young audiences, but leave deeper mechanics for players to discover and research, which signals investment.
- [4:50](https://youtu.be/v8tFfBTr6M8?t=290) **[[core-loops-and-game-design]]** Pair short, frequent small goals (next egg, currency threshold) with big optional goals (huge pet, leaderboard rank, completing the game).
- [6:28](https://youtu.be/v8tFfBTr6M8?t=388) **[[core-loops-and-game-design]]** Give strong feedback on every achievement (sound effects, checkmarks, particle fireworks) to create dopamine hits that keep players playing.
- [7:44](https://youtu.be/v8tFfBTr6M8?t=464) **[[core-loops-and-game-design]]** Build replayability through multiple worlds, many pets/currencies to unlock, and varied content such as new opponents or multiple endings.
