# Yann4dev - How to make successful Roblox games

Make small, proven-loop games fast, price cheap, and earn mainly from developer products; use gamepasses only for lasting perks. Validate the idea and its originality before building, scope tightly, and build core systems first. Playtest with outsiders and low-end devices, then launch with a small Roblox Ads Manager test budget, judged by CTR, cost per play and retention. Stop ads when engaged players stop arriving and let home recommendations take over. Track day-1 and day-7 retention. If day-7 is weak, fix the core loop and add content depth rather than daily rewards.

**Style:** Transparent solo or small-team developer who publishes real Robux figures, flops and ad spend. Emphasises cheap pricing, developer products and disciplined, benchmark-driven ad testing over big spend. Suits solo beginners building quick, proven-loop simulators or multiplayer games who want measured, cautious promotion.

## Playbook
1. **1. Idea: set goals** [[mindset-and-strategy]]: Define why you make Roblox games and what this project should achieve. Goals change over time and should match your skill and experience. Set budget and time to match. [zWeDpmrjhUM]
2. **2. Idea: validate before building** [[game-ideas-and-genres]]: Check the target audience, whether the idea already exists on Roblox or other platforms, whether it is proven, saturated, and easy to make content for. Reuse a working loop but add a meaningful differentiating mechanic. [zWeDpmrjhUM, Bw2ty2T46z4]
3. **3. Build: scope and template** [[development-process-and-speed]]: Set a clear core loop, a release checklist, team, budget, timeline and paid assets up front. Build a reusable template place with your standard structure, modules, configs, lighting and services. Assume tasks take longer than you think. [zWeDpmrjhUM, dbNA3K8lt2w]
4. **4. Build: draft marketing first** [[titles-descriptions]]: Before building, draft the title, icon and a couple of thumbnails so the game is designed around how it will be sold. [zWeDpmrjhUM]
5. **5. Build: core systems first** [[core-loops-and-game-design]]: Build and polish the core systems so they feel right. Build dependent systems such as shops and inventory only after the systems they rely on exist. Cut anything that is too slow, unaffordable or off the core loop. [zWeDpmrjhUM]
6. **6. Build: assets** [[building-art-and-assets]]: Gather most assets before building systems. Placeholders are fine if they resemble the final look. [zWeDpmrjhUM]
7. **7. Build: playtest with outsiders** [[playtesting-and-feedback]]: Ask other developers to test and give serious feedback on theirs. Watch new players load in and go through the tutorial, noting confusion and logoff points. Test many players per server, long-lived servers, bad network, rejoins, varied screens and inputs, and free vs paid play. Budget extra dev days after each test. [zWeDpmrjhUM, dbNA3K8lt2w]
8. **8. Build: low-end optimisation** [[optimization-and-streaming]]: Do a final optimisation pass before release. Test on low-end devices, especially if your audience is in lower-income countries. A strong PC barely ran one game, so FPS problems can hit many players. [Bw2ty2T46z4]
9. **9. Launch: price cheap** [[pricing]]: Price low early to boost payer conversion. In the first week, sell gamepasses and products at roughly half price to measure a realistic payer conversion rate and ARPPU from ad traffic. The creator believes prices could have doubled at peak. [dbNA3K8lt2w, Bw2ty2T46z4]
10. **10. Launch: developer products first** [[monetisation-devproducts]]: Bundle gamepass-style perks into one developer product (the 500-coin product was the best seller). Add a low-price starter pack for new players, and put a high-value consumable in the shop or wheel. Random gambling-style products sold well. Reserve passes for ongoing perks, such as cheap 2x training speed. [dbNA3K8lt2w, Bw2ty2T46z4]
11. **11. Growth: run ads in phases** [[ads-and-sponsored-experiences]]: Use Roblox Ads Manager with a daily budget, maximize plays, all regions and all devices. Set budget by trend urgency, multiplayer need and genre supply. Simulators need less spend than tag or knockout games, and racing needs more. Day 1 is a learning phase with no creative changes. Stop once engaged players stop arriving, not on a fixed date. [yw32DZtz4mQ, Bw2ty2T46z4]
12. **12. Growth: thumbnails** [[thumbnails-icons]]: On days 2-4, swap out underperforming thumbnails and remove the worst after winners emerge. Target CTR of at least 2.5% and cost per play of 0.008 or better. [yw32DZtz4mQ]
13. **13. Growth: read benchmarks and stop ads** [[analytics-metrics]]: Compare the six overview stats against the 50th percentile, and set benchmarks by geography because lower-income regions convert less. Stop ads once home recommendations fill at least two full lobbies at the lowest-CCU time. Watch play-through and bounce rate. [yw32DZtz4mQ, Bw2ty2T46z4]
14. **14. Live: retention and content depth** [[retention-d1-d7]]: Track day-1 retention daily, since a spike from a similar game can hide weak core retention. If day-7 is weak, add content depth rather than daily rewards. Low day-7 retention is a core loop problem. [Bw2ty2T46z4]

## Common mistakes
- Assuming the first game will succeed; the creator's first took 3 months and lost money [dbNA3K8lt2w]
- Copying a popular core loop too closely or without knowing why it works; add a differentiating mechanic [Bw2ty2T46z4, zWeDpmrjhUM]
- Adding filler monetisation rather than meaningful products [Bw2ty2T46z4]
- Selling perks as gamepasses when they should be developer products [Bw2ty2T46z4]
- Running ads after engaged players stop arriving; the 19-day run at $20/day was too long [Bw2ty2T46z4]
- Buying ad traffic before retention is proven; the 100K Robux campaign did not recoup [Bw2ty2T46z4]
- Trying to fix weak day-7 retention with daily rewards instead of the core loop [Bw2ty2T46z4]
- Testing only on a powerful PC and ignoring low-end FPS [Bw2ty2T46z4]
- Developer blindness: not viewing the game through a new player's eyes or skipping outside playtests [zWeDpmrjhUM]
- Underestimating how long tasks take and skipping extra days after playtests [zWeDpmrjhUM]

## How the advice changed over time
- Ad run length: the May 2026 video suggested reassessing after about 2 weeks, possibly longer under new eligibility rules. The July 2026 video ran 19 days and said it should have stopped sooner. Prefer the newer event-based rule: stop when engaged players stop arriving.
- Ad spend scale: May 2026 advised about $15/day for 7-10 days. July 2026 tested 100K Robux over 30 days, which did not recoup. Treat large spend as a cautionary case, not a default.
- Monetisation: both videos favour developer products over passes. July 2026 says many items sold as passes should have been developer products, which strengthens the May 2026 advice.
- Conversion benchmarks: May 2026 set a CTR target of 2.5%+ as a rule of thumb. July 2026 observed 3.3%, rising to about 4.5% after unlocking all audiences, so the rule is conservative for that genre.
- Retention: day-1 retention was about 13% before ad-driven players arrived and about 5-8% after they left. Newer guidance is to track it daily and to read it against similar-game spikes.
