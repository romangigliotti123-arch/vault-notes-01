# RaulStudios - How to make successful Roblox games

RaulStudios sequences Roblox work as: validate the genre against trending competition, clone a proven loop with a fresh theme, reuse tested code, ship with thin but complete scope, and make monetisation and thumbnails ready before paid traffic. His evidence shows external promo (secondary channel, friends, community post) brought zero players, and ads returned plays but little Robux without gamepasses. Discord growth from free templates and a steady flow of small payouts from many published games were his most reliable levers. Most of his advice comes from small, anecdotal samples.

**Style:** Candid, numbers-first devlogs from a beginner-scale developer who shares failures (zero players, 50K Robux of ads) alongside results. Best for solo developers making clicker and incremental games, templates, or commissions who want honest, budget-level data rather than polished tactics.

## Playbook
1. **Validate the genre before building** [[trend-research-and-validation]]: Check the trending list for competitors first. The creator dropped a natural-disaster survival idea (kTUl4ZwjS4c) after finding zero trending games in that genre. Pick ideas from genre sites, such as merge games, which he saw as popular.
2. **Clone a proven loop with a fresh theme** [[game-ideas-and-genres]]: Copy a proven genre such as the incremental clicker and change the theme (buy streamers instead of characters). Add multiple unlockable variants early, since a single option felt boring (CbAL1e8NgSU).
3. **Reuse tested code and cut scope deliberately** [[development-process-and-speed]]: Reuse modules like a global leaderboard instead of rebuilding. Skip costly features (he skipped an upgrade tree) to ship faster, but weigh that against retention. Budget realistically: the streamer game took 3 days vs 7 planned; a basic button with VFX took over 2 hours vs 30 minutes estimated.
4. **Make the start easy and show the next goal** [[first-session-onboarding]]: Avoid steep early scaling; the reference game required 5 trillion to progress, which he saw as a reason players quit. Combine the tutorial with a visible task list so new players always know their next goal.
5. **Build a reward ladder** [[core-loops-and-game-design]]: Set the first rebirth low (1,000 views and 500 subscribers) so players get a quick reward. Set prestige far harder (1,000,000 views and 100,000 subscribers) as a long-term goal. Place rebirth as an on-screen button, not a physical stand.
6. **Make early clicks feel rewarding** [[player-psychology-and-game-feel]]: Give instant XP and plus-one visual effects on each click. Add satisfying sound and pop-ups to the first purchase. Show costs above buttons so requirements are clear before interaction. Game feel was a key reason he found the reference game fun.
7. **Keep players always chasing an objective** [[retention-d1-d7]]: Auto-replace each completed objective with a new one, with an animation, so players always have a goal. This is a simple retention tactic he used alongside the task list.
8. **Restyle a free UI template and pick interaction carefully** [[ui-ux-design]]: Start menus and shop/backpack from a free UI template to save time, then restyle. Use proximity prompts instead of click-to-use tools; he found them more usable. Where player feedback favored a simple close-proximity interaction, he switched and found it much better.
9. **Ship gamepasses before any paid traffic** [[monetisation-gamepasses]]: Sell multiplier passes (2x views, 2x subscribers, 2x rewards, 2x prestige) and an instant-unlock pass for premium tiers, plus a Robux-only special play button. His ad campaign returned 137 Robux mainly because the game had few gamepasses.
10. **Test thumbnails before buying ads** [[thumbnails-icons]]: Test thumbnails with a generator (RoThumbs) and study top games' layouts. A thumbnail swap lifted CTR from ~1% to 1.95%, nearly doubling plays. Treat 1% as bad and about 2% as average. Roblox cannot swap thumbnails on an active ad; you must cancel and restart.
11. **Run ads only on a polished game, for 7 days** [[ads-and-sponsored-experiences]]: Fix bugs and polish first; he updated daily for a week before launch because ads to a rough game waste money. Run campaigns for 7 days, not 5. Wait about a day for data before judging. His 50,000 Robux campaign drove ~1,800 to ~3,000 plays but little income.
12. **Seed a Discord and share screenshots** [[community-and-discord]]: Create a Discord and post gameplay screenshots in community servers. Free template releases grew his Discord to 100+ in one day and 500+ overall. Ask players to leave specific feedback on what to add or fix next, and announce each update on release day.
13. **Finish live updates before starting new games** [[updates-live-ops]]: Ship updates for a live game with waiting players before starting a new project. He built his largest update over at least 5 hours straight. Check Creator Dashboard retention, engagement, acquisition and demographics; acquisition data can lag several days.
14. **Keep publishing and take commissions for income** [[business-and-payouts]]: Small premium payouts come from many old games (about 35 Robux per 30 days from dead games). Take paid commissions per task rather than full-project payment, and skip very low offers. For US 18+ users, DevEx earnings ran 42% higher.

## Common mistakes
- Relying on a secondary YouTube channel, friends, or a community post for launch players; these produced zero to one player (dAL8ohgzN28)
- Buying ads before bug fixes, thumbnail tests, or gamepasses are in place, so spend returns little Robux (7vYv8DA7pqU)
- Rushing a game to release in 3 days with minimal content, which signals thin gameplay (CbAL1e8NgSU)
- Steep early scaling (5 trillion start cost) that makes new players quit (CbAL1e8NgSU)
- Adding towers or weapons that players ignore; in the defense game only Maya and Golem were used (kKavvUDtHOg)
- Accepting very low commission offers, such as 5K Robux for a full game (NE8OWWbAia8)
- Trusting a 'verified' badge claim or working one-sided; a partner left him doing most UI and scripting (GLmlPsYLsbM)
- Abandoning a live game mid-cycle to start a new one (kTUl4ZwjS4c)
- Ignoring age-rule policy changes; the Roblox 16+ rule reportedly cut player access and joins (vnpCimQkHoQ)
- Stopping consistent building after a large Robux payout (NE8OWWbAia8)

## Launch checklist
- [ ] Check the trending list for competition in your genre
- [ ] Reuse tested modules such as a leaderboard script
- [ ] Pick a free UI template and restyle it
- [ ] Show costs above every buy button
- [ ] Add a tutorial combined with a visible task list
- [ ] Make the first rebirth or reward reachable early (about 1,000 views equivalent or quick progress)
- [ ] Add instant click feedback: plus-one effects and sound on the first purchase
- [ ] Auto-replace completed objectives with new ones
- [ ] Set up multiplier gamepasses (views, subscribers, rewards, prestige)
- [ ] Add an instant-unlock pass and a Robux-only special play button
- [ ] Fix bugs and polish daily for about a week before launch
- [ ] Test thumbnails with a generator and compare against top games' layouts
- [ ] Confirm CTR against the 1% bad / about 2% average benchmark
- [ ] Create a Discord server and share gameplay screenshots in community servers
- [ ] Ask players for specific feedback in comments
- [ ] Plan a 7-day ad campaign and wait about a day for data
- [ ] Check age rules and policy changes before counting on audience size
- [ ] Set revenue to the group, not a personal account, if the game is group-owned

## How the advice changed over time
- June to July advice treated external promo (secondary channel, friends, community post) as a launch path; by August and September the focus shifted to free templates and Discord growth as the main audience builder.
- Conflict: an August 20 video advised keeping dead games published for small premium income, while the September 6 video sold an unprofitable game to a front-page developer and called selling worthwhile. Prefer the September advice for games earning nothing.
- July 16 said to add gamepasses before running ads; the August 6 campaign confirmed the same lesson, and the creator added thumbnail testing before paid spend.
- June 30 blamed the Roblox 16+ age-rule change for lost joins; no later video revisits that claim, so treat it as the creator's single observation.
