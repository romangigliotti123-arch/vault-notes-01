# Making A Viral Roblox Game Is Easier Than You Think

> Tagged **low-advice**: mostly entertainment/experiment; notes kept short.

## Summary
A Roblox dev explains that a game needs four green signals to go viral: long playtime, players returning, players spending, and enough click appeal. He shows analytics for his 1k-CCU game Steal a Gotcha, stresses that a strong core concept drives those numbers, and argues originality beats copying winning games. Much of the video promotes his templates.

## Tips
- [0:37](https://youtu.be/kg_h09_x7no?t=37) **[[analytics-metrics]]** Judge a new game by four signals: long session time, players returning, players spending, and enough click appeal. Aim to make all four green.
- [1:36](https://youtu.be/kg_h09_x7no?t=96) **[[analytics-metrics]]** Check a game's analytics a couple of days after release. Read each of the four signals as green (good) or red (bad) to decide whether it has viral potential.
- [2:49](https://youtu.be/kg_h09_x7no?t=169) **[[game-ideas-and-genres]]** Design the game concept around the four engagement signals before building. The core idea can carry a game further than polish can.
- [2:49](https://youtu.be/kg_h09_x7no?t=169) **[[development-process-and-speed]]** Expect the first winning concept to take months of iteration; improve with each game rather than hoping for a quick hit.
