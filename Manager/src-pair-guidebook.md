# Google People + AI Guidebook (feedback+control, errors, mental models)
## Key claims
- Feedback + control: "When users have the right level of control over the system, they're more likely to trust it." Three considerations: align feedback with model improvement (implicit vs explicit; tell people what is collected), communicate value and time-to-impact of feedback, balance control and automation (let users opt out of giving feedback). Feedback options should be mutually exclusive and collectively exhaustive (thumbs up/down).
- Errors + graceful failure: define what counts as an error from the user's expectations; identify error sources; "Provide paths forward from failure": explain why a result could not be given and offer alternatives ("Try checking again in a month"). When handing control back: "make it easy and intuitive for users to quickly pick up where the system leaves off... awareness of the situation, what they need to do next, and how to do it." "Be human, not machine"; error messages may need to disclose the system made a mistake.
- Mental models: "Mismatched mental models can lead to unmet expectations, frustration, misuse, and product abandonment." Onboard in stages: say what it can do, cannot do, how it may change, how to improve it. Plan for co-learning. Communicate the system's limits to avoid unintended deception when users expect human-like capability.
